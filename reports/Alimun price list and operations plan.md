# Alimun price list and operations plan

This is the working price list and operating model that comes out of the two research reports and the follow-up discussion. It covers every product from the free app to private lessons, the margin each one keeps, how learners are placed into classes, how many teachers the timetable needs, and how the app and the classes sell each other. All prices are per learner, for 60-minute live classes, and EU prices include 20% VAT. The cost model is the one used in both reports: teacher pay from the rate card plus 3% for payouts, video at €0.21 per person per class hour, Stripe Ireland card fees (1.5% + €0.25 for EEA cards plus 0.7% Billing and 0.5% Tax; 3.15% + €0.25 plus 2% conversion for international cards), and ECB reference rates of 23 October 2026 (MAD at 11.0 from an aggregator). Margins are contribution before support, software, marketing and salaries. Re-check exchange rates, taxes and fees on launch day.

## The formats

Five live formats cover every learner, each with its own size, opening threshold and fallback rule.

| Format | Size | Opens at | Planned attendance | If attendance falls | Who it is for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Large class | up to 20 | 10 | 12 | merge into another large class at the same level below 8 | entry level, hobby and travel learners, cheaper markets, extra practice |
| Group | up to 8 | 4 | 5 | with 2 learners the class runs 45 minutes for at most two weeks, then merges | the main product: same teacher, same group, fixed times |
| Pod | up to 4 | 3 | 3 | with 2 learners the class runs 45 minutes for at most four weeks, then the pod merges or each learner chooses a group or private | partners, friends, families, learners who want more speaking time |
| Exam group | up to 8 | 6 | 7 | merges with another exam group for the same exam date | TEF/TCF, Goethe B1/B2, telc, IELTS, CELPIP, Italian B1 for citizenship |
| Private | 1 | — | 1 | — | executives, interviews, last-minute exam preparation |

Large classes use short breakout rooms in pairs and threes so each learner speaks for 15-20 minutes an hour, and they cannot be marketed as "small groups". Groups, pods and exam groups can. Exam groups meet three times a week, start on fixed dates and end two to three weeks before the exam they prepare for.

## How classes are allocated

A class is defined by four things only: language, level, time-zone region and time slot. Goal is not a dimension of its own, except for exam groups (one per exam and date) and German for healthcare, where demand fills whole groups. Every extra dimension splits demand and leaves classes half-empty.

**Levels.** The placement test sets the starting level, and progress in the app and class assessments move it. Groups use half-levels (A1.1, A1.2 up to B2.2), so a group stays together for about 8-10 weeks per half-level. Large classes use whole levels (A1, A2, B1, B2), because a wider range is manageable with breakout rooms.

**Regions.** Europe and North Africa (the EU, Morocco, Tunisia, Egypt, Turkey, within two hours of each other), the Americas (Canada and Latin America) and South Asia (India, Pakistan). A German B1 evening group can mix Moroccan, Tunisian and EU learners. Prime slots are weekday evenings 18:00-21:00 local time and weekend mornings; off-peak slots exist only where demand shows it.

**The life of a class.** A class moves through five states:

| State | What happens |
|---|---|
| Forming | the slot is shown on the timetable with the real number of learners who have joined and the number it needs to open; nobody is charged |
| Open | the threshold is reached, the teacher is confirmed, learners are charged and the first class is scheduled |
| Running | same teacher, same learners, fixed times; free seats are offered to passes, trials and reward seats |
| Checkpoint | every four weeks, at the start of a new unit, new learners can join and learners who have moved level transfer |
| Merging or closing | when attendance stays below the merge threshold, the class merges with another at the same level and region, or switches to its fallback rule; learners are told at least 48 hours ahead and never pay more |

**Who gets a seat first.** Subscribers in their own class come first, then exam-group learners, then subscribers taking extra classes, then single passes and packs, then trial learners, then reward seats. A trial or reward never takes a seat a paying learner wants.

**The money check.** Before a class opens, the engine adds up the net revenue per class of the seats sold (after VAT, card fees and the learner's band) and compares it with the teacher's cost. A class opens only if it keeps at least 15%, and the engine aims for 25% at planned attendance. This lets classes mix learners from different bands safely, and stops a class opening on cheap seats alone.

**Rescheduling and missed classes.** A learner can move a class to another class at the same level with a free seat up to 24 hours before it starts. A missed class without notice is not refunded, but the learner gets one make-up seat in a large class at their level that month. Subscription classes that are not used within the month roll over for one month, then lapse. Alimun pays the teacher for every scheduled class, including when learners do not show up.

**Pods.** A pod that drops to two learners runs 45-minute classes for up to four weeks while the engine looks for a third learner. After that the pod merges with another pod at the same level, or each learner chooses between a group and private lessons at their prices. A pod booking with no other learner becomes a private class at the private price.

**Instant classes, later.** The calendar and the matching engine above run from day one. Instant private classes and pods (a learner asks to practise now and an available teacher accepts) start per language and region once enough teachers commit to standby hours, at the single private class price. Instant groups, formed when three or four learners at the same level are waiting within ten minutes, come last and only where waiting numbers show it works. Learner prices never surge; hard slots are filled by paying teachers more.

## Teachers needed

**Pay.** The rate card per class hour is €13 for English and Spanish, €15 for French, €16 for Italian and €20 for German, wherever the teacher lives. Large classes add €3 an hour. Early-morning and late-night slots add €2 an hour. When instant classes launch, teachers on standby get a small fee per standby hour plus the full rate for time taught (the standby fee is to be set). Teachers are paid for every scheduled class, including no-shows, and not for classes Alimun cancels or merges with at least 48 hours' notice. A teacher doing 15 hours a week earns about €845 a month in English and €1,300 in German; at 20 hours, about €1,125 and €1,730. Recruit German teachers who live outside Germany, where €20 is well above local pay.

**At launch.** The smallest timetable that covers one language in one region is two large classes a week at each of four levels, one group meeting twice a week at each of eight half-levels, one exam group three times a week and a few pods. That is about 31 class hours a week: two teachers at about 15 hours each, plus one teacher on cover. It fills with about 110 learners at planned attendance. At English EU prices that timetable keeps roughly €1,800 a month after teacher pay, fees, video and VAT, before fixed costs; in Morocco about a quarter less.

**At scale.** For every 1,000 active class learners in one language, assuming 40% in large classes, 35% in groups, 8% in pods, 10% in exam groups, 5% on the intensive mix and 2% private, and average attendance at 85% of plan because of level and time fragmentation, the timetable needs about 426 class hours a week. That is about 25 teachers at 20 hours a week including a 15% cover pool, or 20 teachers at 25 hours.

| Teaching hours per teacher a week | Teachers per 1,000 learners, including cover |
|---|---|
| 15 | about 33 |
| 20 | about 25 |
| 25 | about 20 |

**When to hire.** Add a teacher when the average load passes 20 hours a week, when the cover pool falls below 15% of scheduled hours, or when a forming class has reached its threshold and no teacher's calendar fits. Add an academic coordinator per language once it has about ten teachers, to own levels, materials and teacher quality. These roles are not in the margins above.

## How the app and the classes sell each other

The app finds learners cheaply and the classes earn the money. Every moment where an app user is likely to buy shows them a real class they could join.

| Moment | Offer |
|---|---|
| Signup goal question | learners with an exam or move date go straight to the exam group whose dates fit; career learners to groups; hobby learners to large classes |
| Finishing a unit in the app | "Your level has a group on Tuesdays and Thursdays at 19:00 with Sara. 2 seats left." The seat count is real |
| Running out of speaking-practice minutes | a large class or the trial class |
| Weekly free taster class (up to 20, one teacher) | the €3.50 trial seat in a running class |
| After the trial | single classes, packs, or a subscription with the app month credited |
| Third single pass bought | "You have spent €15 on three classes. On the once-a-week plan these would have cost €12, with the same group every week." |
| A subscriber buys extra classes twice in a month | the next frequency up, prorated |
| A group learner asks for more speaking time | a pod, or the intensive mix |
| Three weeks before an exam date | a mock exam marked by the teacher, and private hours |
| A partner or friend is invited | a pod, and the bring-a-friend reward |
| Cancelling | pause for up to a month, or move down a frequency or format; in Germany the cancel button leads straight to confirmation |

Free seats are filled in this order, cheapest first: reward seats, app users at the right level, single passes, then merging. Subscriptions are never discounted to fill seats. If seats stay empty in a time slot for weeks, the slot moves or merges.

## Rules that keep the tactics legal

Seat counts and "places left" come from the database and are never invented. Enrolment deadlines are real dates that close. The founding offer is a price lock, not a crossed-out "was" price. Any announced discount is checked against the price charged in the previous 30 days. Renewals are announced before they are charged where the law requires it (Spain from 28 December 2026, Mexico, India for e-mandates), trials remind the learner before converting, and cancelling takes no more steps than signing up. The EU's two-step online withdrawal function, required since 19 June 2026, still has to be built: today's Terms only offer withdrawal by email. This is a summary of the research notes, not legal advice.
