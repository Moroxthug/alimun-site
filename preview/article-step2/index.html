IELTS or Cambridge: which English certificate should you take?

Both prove your English, and both are recognized around the world. They are built differently, though, and the right one depends on what you need it for: a visa, a university place or a stronger CV.

The short answer

Take the certificate your visa office, university or employer names. If the choice is yours, IELTS suits migration and short-term needs, while a Cambridge exam gives you a lasting proof of a level you have already reached.

Two tests, two ideas

IELTS and the Cambridge English Qualifications come from the same family: Cambridge University Press & Assessment is one of the owners of IELTS, together with the British Council and IDP. Yet the two answer different questions. IELTS asks "how good is your English today?" and places you on one scale. A Cambridge exam asks "have you reached this level?" and certifies it.

That difference shapes everything else: how you prepare, what happens if you have a bad day, how long the result stays useful and who will accept it.

IELTS

One test for every level. You get a band from 0 to 9 in Listening, Reading, Writing and Speaking, plus an overall band. It comes in two versions: Academic, for study, and General Training, often used for work and migration.

Cambridge

One exam per level, from A2 Key to C2 Proficiency. B2 First and C1 Advanced are the ones most people take. You pass at the level of the exam, with a score on the Cambridge English Scale, and the certificate does not expire.

What each test looks like on the day

Both test the four skills, but the format of the day is different. IELTS is a single test of just under three hours for Listening, Reading and Writing, with the Speaking test on the same day or within a few days. You can take it on paper or on a computer at a test centre, and in many places online.

In IELTS Writing you write two texts: in the Academic version, a description of a graph, table or diagram of at least 150 words, then an essay of at least 250 words. Cambridge B2 First and C1 Advanced also ask for two texts, an essay plus a choice of formats such as an email, a report or a review, so you show that you can adapt your style to the reader.

The Speaking test

In IELTS you speak one to one with an examiner for 11 to 14 minutes, in three parts: questions about yourself, a short talk on a topic card, and a discussion. Cambridge Speaking tests are usually taken in pairs with two examiners. You talk to the examiner and also with the other candidate, so your ability to interact counts as well as your fluency.

Not sure which level you are?

The level test places you from A1 to C1 in a few minutes, and shows the class that fits you.

When IELTS is the better choice

If you need English for a visa or migration, IELTS is often the test the authorities name, and some require a specific version such as General Training. It is also accepted by a very large number of universities, especially in the UK, Australia, Canada and New Zealand.

Because everyone takes the same test, IELTS also suits you when you are not sure of your level. Your band shows exactly where you are, with no risk of choosing an exam that is too hard or too easy.

Start from the requirement, not from the test.

When Cambridge is the better choice

If you want a certificate for your CV that lasts, a Cambridge exam is a strong option. It does not expire, and employers and universities across Europe know B2 First and C1 Advanced well.

It suits you best when you know your level, since each exam targets one. Preparing for a specific exam also gives your studies a clear goal and a date to work towards.

Questions people ask

Is IELTS harder than Cambridge?

Neither is harder in itself: they are built differently. IELTS measures you on one scale, while a Cambridge exam checks whether you have reached one specific level.

Which one do UK universities accept?

Most UK universities accept IELTS Academic, and many also accept C1 Advanced or C2 Proficiency. Each university lists the tests and scores it accepts for each course.

Can I fail a Cambridge exam?

If your score falls below the exam's level, you may receive a certificate at the level below, or none if the score is too low. With IELTS you always get a band.
