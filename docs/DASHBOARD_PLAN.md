## Step 5 decisions (owner, 25 Oct 2026: "do what is best")
- Player: one shared exercise engine (`js/app/exercises.js`, moved out of `reader.js`) used by the book pages, unit tests and a new Practice screen (`#/practice`). Practice serves short mixed rounds built from the student's level, goal, level-test topics and past mistakes, drawn from the book item banks, with no repeat until the bank runs out. Quizzes, long practice sets and timed rounds stay in step 7.
- Item results: a new table for per-item attempts (item id, right or wrong, answer, time) so mistakes come back spaced and the Practice round can be personalised. Migration under `supabase/migrations/`, row-level security like `practice_progress`.
- Audio: generated once per sentence and stored as files, never live, so each sentence costs once and plays instantly. Groq's text-to-speech (Orpheus) only has English and Saudi Arabic and caps requests at 200 characters, so it cannot voice the German, French, Spanish, Italian and Portuguese books. Owner (25 Oct 2026): Google only, ElevenLabs costs too much. Plan: Gemini text-to-speech through a Google AI Studio key (Vercel variable `GOOGLE_AI_API_KEY`, not `GEMINI_API_KEY`, which would move the text features from Groq to Gemini); several voices per language, two speakers per dialogue; a short comparison page first so the owner picks the voices by ear. Owner (25 Oct 2026, after listening): Charon and Orus are the best and every shortlisted voice is fine; then: a main woman's voice with Orus, for balance. So the narrators are Sulafat (woman) and Orus (man), alternating by lesson, and dialogue speakers get a voice that matches them (women Sulafat, Kore, Aoede, Leda, Vindemiatrix; men Orus, Charon, Puck, Achird, Sadaltager), the same voice for a name across a book. Plan per book: `site/src/data/books/audio.js` → `/data/audio/<book>.json`; files made by `/api/speech?action=generate` from the admin page `/app/audio` into Supabase Storage `course-audio/<book>/<hash>.mp3` (40 kbps MP3); the reader shows play buttons on examples and Listen on dialogues and readings when the files exist. Files go to Supabase Storage (public bucket for course audio).
- Speaking: Groq Whisper (`whisper-large-v3`, already on the Groq account) transcribes the recording in the language being learned; the player compares it with the expected sentence word by word and shows what was heard, with the missed words marked. Open answers ("answer the question", "describe the picture") get short feedback from the existing Groq text model through `/api/ai`. Recordings are sent, transcribed and discarded, not stored. Whisper is forgiving of accents, so this checks words, not true pronunciation; a pronunciation score (for example Azure Pronunciation Assessment) can come later if the owner wants it.
- Drawings (board at `/app/drawings`; owner, 25 Oct 2026: keep all styles, all tutors are good; in the dashboard as `js/app/cast3.js`: sticker objects, ink-and-wash scenes, paper-cut spot drawings, six tutors with lip-sync mouths; sources `docs/design/sources/cast3.py`): the approved cast stays, plus new drawings in more styles and a wider range (people in everyday scenes, places, objects for picture exercises, small spot drawings across every dashboard screen), all SVG in the repo, in the approved palette, shown to the owner on a board before use.
- Build order inside step 5: shared engine and the remaining text exercise types first (word bank, drop-down gap, build the sentence, sort into groups, odd one out, find and fix the mistake, transform, conjugate, word formation, collocations, highlight, categorise, crossword, word search, memory pairs), then the drawing board and picture types, then audio (after the owner picks a voice), then speaking.

## Step 8 decisions (owner, 25 Oct 2026: "go ahead with mock tests")
- Thirteen full-length practice tests, all written by Alimun (drafts for native-teacher review, `review: 'draft'`): in the format of B2 First, C1 Advanced, IELTS Academic, Goethe-Zertifikat B1 and B2, telc Deutsch B1 and B2, and the Alimun end-of-level tests for English and German A2, B1 and B2. French, Spanish, Italian and Portuguese tests (DELF/DALF, DELE, CILS/CELI, CAPLE) come with their course books.
- The student sees the tests of their course language, their level first. One paper at a time with the exam's time (a timer that can be hidden; when time is up the student may finish and is told so), parts as tabs, answers kept in the browser if they leave; nothing is marked until they finish. Then the score, part by part, every answer with its explanation, transcripts of the recordings and model answers. True or false questions use the exam's own words (Richtig / Falsch).
- New engine kinds for exams: a whole text with gaps (`cloze`: drop-downs, typed, word formation, or lettered sentences), fixed-list questions (`pick`), writing (`write`, with a choice of tasks and a word count) and speaking (`talk`: the examiner's question in the examiner's voice with the tutor's lip sync, recordings up to four minutes, transcribed). Key word transformation shows the original, the key word and the gapped sentence.
- Writing and speaking get drafted marks against the exam's own criteria (Cambridge 0 to 5, IELTS bands, Goethe, telc), with comments in the student's language and one improved sentence, through the existing `/api/ai?action=grade` (same access and caps: students with a plan); pronunciation is not marked. A score is never turned into a predicted exam grade; Goethe and telc show the published 60% pass mark.
- Listening recordings and the examiner's questions are course audio: the plan per test is `/data/audio/<id>.json`, made from `/app/audio` like the books (tick "Mock tests first" to make them before the remaining book audio). Until a recording exists the part offers its transcript. The exam's play count is kept (IELTS once, most others twice).
- Results: practice_progress `mock:<test>:<paper>` with the score and the answers and marks (no database change); a finished paper counts for the streak. Signed out: this browser.
- Not official material: every page says so, in the student's language. Instructions do not refer to question numbers, which are not shown.
