# Course books: notes for the native-teacher review

Points the writers flagged while drafting (docs/BOOKS.md). Each needs a decision from the reviewing teacher; delete a line once it is settled.

## English A2
- u1 p2, u4 p4: some "tap the words" targets are two-word phrases (am walking, more expensive), shown as one button.
- u2 p1: the -ed sounds use the plain labels t, d, id instead of phonetic symbols.
- u3 p1: "I’d like ___ orange juice" accepts *an* and *some*; the test gap "a ___ of milk" accepts carton, bottle, glass, jug.
- u3: *fruit* is treated as uncountable.
- u4 p2: *farther* is accepted next to *further*, though the table shows only *further*.
- u6 p4: general health advice in the leaflet (a cold does not usually need a doctor, 7 to 8 hours of sleep); check the wording.
- u5, u7: British forms (at the weekend, single and return ticket) with *one-way* and *canceled* also accepted in gaps.
- u8 p3: the been/gone items have two options each.
- u5 p1: the tip says the second *go* is often dropped in *I’m going to go to the beach*; check the wording.

## English B1
- u6 p1: the reported-speech gaps accept only backshifted forms, although the tip says the present can stay when still true.
- u6 p4, u7 p4: invented usernames and place names (Ashby Vale and others).
- u7 p4: the reading uses non-defining relative clauses, which the lesson does not teach yet.
- u7 p1: one gap accepts *whom*; keep it at B1?
- u7 p3: customs (kissing on both cheeks, paying in cash, tipping) are deliberately not tied to a country.
- u8 p1: a future gap accepts *is starting*, *is going to start*, *starts*; check the list and the explanation.
- u8 p2: two word-order items are conditionals; only the punctuation on the tiles fixes the clause order.

- u2 p1: "Halfway up the hill we stopped ___ at the view": only the context rules out *looking*.
- u3 p1: *must* and *have to* are both glossed as "necessary"; the external and internal difference is kept simple.
- u3 p3: lost luggage: airlines paying back essentials is presented as common practice, not a rule.
- u4 p1: conditionals are practised as a match instead of word order, because the clauses can swap.
- u5 p2: "Most scientists agree that climate change is causing more extreme weather" stated as fact; u5 p4 Westbridge and its figures are fictional.

## German B1
- u2 p4: Grace trained as *Bürokauffrau* (in Kenya); today's German title is *Kauffrau für Büromanagement*.
- u3 p3: house-rule facts (quiet hours 22 to 6 or 7, Sundays and holidays) are given as general practice, not law.
- u4 p3: sick note "from the fourth day for many employers, some from the first"; *Versichertenkarte*; the electronic sick note is not mentioned.
- u4 p2: *sich verletzen* with accusative and dative (*dich* / *dir das Knie*), explained in the why.
- u5 p1, p4: impersonal and marked passives (*Bei uns wird nicht ferngesehen*, *Bei mir wird die Zeitung gelesen*), on the edge of B1.
- u5 p2: check every indirect-question choice has one right answer.
- u6 p1: the tip says spoken German often uses the dative after *wegen* and *trotz*; only the genitive is taught.
- u6 p4: station announcements are simplified.
- u7 p2: in the word-order item *Wirst du mir beim Umzug helfen?*, *beim Umzug mir* is marginal but possible with emphasis.
- u7 p3: waste bins (Biotonne, Papiertonne, Altglas) and the 25-cent deposit: true in general, differs by town.
- u8 p3: customs (Nikolaus, Heiligabend, Fasching areas, national days 3 Oct, 26 Oct, 1 Aug): check the wording is not too absolute.
- u8 p1: five-column adjective tables (they shrink to fit on phones and open full screen).
## English B2
- u1 p1: present perfect simple and continuous items with *this week* and *recently*: check only one fits.
- u1 p2: invented wrong prefixes (*inorganised*, *imorganised*); *unorganised* left out because it exists.
- u2 p3: "In the end, I chose…" accepts only *in*.
- u3 p1: a wrong option *will be completing* was replaced by *will have complete* (some speakers accept the continuous).
- u3 p2: collocation gaps accept several verbs (gain or get experience, earn or make a living, got or received a promotion); check the *earn* and *do overtime* match pairs.
- u4 test: *got her phone stolen* is accepted next to *had her phone stolen* (informal).
- u4 reading: check the history of the fridge (ice houses, refrigerants, home fridges from the early twentieth century); "millions of emails are sent every minute" is vague.
- u5 p2: mixed-conditional gaps accept *would be working* and *would work*, *were* and *was*; the arguable wrong option *wouldn’t have been so tired today* was replaced.
- u6: two opinion pieces of about 120 words each, with a "by …" line in each.
- u7 p1: "Kenji ___ sleeping" accepts *must*, *might*, *may*, *could be*; the reading says an upright piano weighs more than 200 kg.
- u8 p3: *ask* is accepted next to *enquire* and *inquire*; *Yours sincerely* and *Yours faithfully* are labelled British usage.
- Word-order items in this book sometimes rely on a comma or capital letter on a tile to be unique.

## German A2
- Word-order items: a few main clauses could also be built with a fronted word for emphasis (*Der Zug ist schneller als der Bus.*, *Ich lerne Deutsch, weil ich in Wien arbeite.*); most items are questions or imperatives so only one order works.
- u1 reading, u3: use the Perfekt of common verbs before u6 teaches it.
- u1 p3: the gap hint "(ihr, die Kollegen)" may read oddly.
- u3 p1: container match (Tafel, Becher, Flasche, Dose, Scheibe): check no other pairing fits.
- u3, u4: general facts (tip of five to ten percent, *Zimmer* excludes kitchen and bathroom, warm and cold rent, many young people live in a WG).
- u5: du-imperative gaps also accept forms with -e (*Trinke*, *Bleibe*); the pharmacy leaflet uses generic, ibuprofen-like dosing.
- u6: *Krankenpfleger/in*; the official title since 2020 is *Pflegefachmann/Pflegefachfrau*.
- u7: *bei der Bank* is given for "where?" (*in der Bank* is also common); invented fare of 74 euros.
- u8: *im Garten von meinem Bruder* (spoken) instead of the genitive; five-column adjective table; the reading previews endings after *ein* and possessives.
- In reading lessons, choice questions are written in German (taught text), unlike the B1 model, where they are in English.

## German B2
- u1: five-column adjective tables; case names (Nominativ…) are marked as explanations and translated.
- u2 p3: *in Frage kommen* (a tip says *infrage* is also correct); rough equivalents under the heading "Simpler form".
- u3 p4: check the physics wording of the heat-pump reading.

- u4 p4, u1 p4: fictional town Hallberg and fictional people.
- u5 p4: reading about Clara Schumann; facts checked (1819 Leipzig, first concert at nine, married 1840 with court permission, eight children, Brahms 1853, Frankfurt from 1878, died 1896, 100-mark note).
- u6: German verb hints in brackets; *wärst* and *wärest* accepted.
- u7: two *zwar … aber* word-order items rely on the capital letter of the first tile; "Was mich überzeugt hat, ___ die Kameraarbeit" offers *ist* (*war* also correct but not offered); the test has 12 questions.
- u8 p2: modal particles (*eben*, *doch*): check each item has one answer; the test gap "dir ___ schon dreimal erklärt" accepts *doch* and *ja*.

# Goal books

## English A2, travel
- Invented places: Brackwell station (u2), the town of Wexley (u5); real station names appear only on a departure board.
- UK facts stated generally: cheaper tickets at quieter times, replacement buses during engineering work, free tap water in restaurants, service of about 10 per cent, pharmacists advise on small problems, 999 and 112.
- u6: *I’ve lost…* taught as a fixed phrase before the course book's present perfect unit.
- u2: in word order, only question order is accepted ("Is this the right train for Glasgow?").
- Some wrong options are clearly wrong on purpose (e.g. "small and green the"); more plausible ones may be better.

## English B1, work
- u2 p1: on the phone *this is* is taught and *I am* rejected (a phone convention, not grammar).
- u4 test: *rose* against *raised* and *rised*; check it fits B1.
- u5 test: "anything nice ___ the weekend" accepts *at* and *over*, not American *on*.
- u6: *sorry about* is the answer, *sorry for* is mentioned as also correct; the complaint and reply readings are about 140 words each.
- Invented companies (Northfield Office Supplies, Parkside Design): check they do not exist; real cities Leeds, York and Hull in a fictional project.

## English B1, study and exams
- u1: *enroll* accepted next to *enrol*; u2: "In ___, I would like to repeat the main points" accepts *conclusion*, *summary*, *short*.
- u2: note-taking example with → and % and an invented figure; u5: fact or opinion as two-option choices.
- u3: "many universities have an official extension form" (general).
- u6: "B1 exams such as B1 Preliminary"; "in many exams you hear recordings twice and lose no marks for wrong answers": check it is accurate and general; the email-to-Alex task follows the usual B1 format but is original.
- House rules: u2 has "1st uni opened 1823" in a note option and u5 uses "Paragraph 1:" and "Paragraph 2:" labels; check against the no-numbered-lists rule.

## English A2, moving abroad
- u5 p1: registering with a GP reworded: in England no passport or proof of address is needed (it can help to bring them).
- u5 p3: the leaflet says "phone 111" for urgent help (England, Scotland and Wales).
- u5 p2 match: "You should call your GP" fits "a cough after three weeks" best but could fit others.
- u1: invented postcodes could match real addresses; u1 p1 letter-sound guide is rough.
- u2 p3: real places (Leeds, York, change at Selby) with invented times.
- u6 p2: a phrase table where "say yes" and "say no politely" appear twice; reads close to a phrase list.

## English A2, study and exams
- u2 p1: rough meanings for *how often* words; u5 p2: "I don’t think … are boring" called more natural (a style point).
- u3 p1: tip says not to write *Dear Teacher* and that UK adult learners often use the teacher's first name.
- u3, u4: invented prices for fictional schools; u4 test: check the "Lost: black umbrella … call Tom" notice reads only one way.
- u6: writing task format like A2 Key, not official; "questions often follow the order of the text" stated generally.

- u4: "many contracts can be ended with one month's notice after the minimum term" (general); invented prices.

## English A2, work
- Invented companies (Brightside Travel, Northway Hotels and others) may match real small firms; phone numbers use the UK ranges reserved for fiction, emails use example.com.
- u4, u6: a weekday with a date (Thursday 12 November) is right for 2026 only.
- u6: full-time "usually about 35 to 40 hours"; many UK CVs leave out the photo and date of birth (general).
- u2: British *oh* and *double* for numbers; simplified letter sounds.

## English B1, moving abroad
- UK facts checked and stated generally: deposit of at most five weeks' rent in England, right-to-rent checks, the 14-day cooling-off period, GP registration without proof of address in England; check the ombudsman, school start age, half-term and term-time absence wording.
- u1: no tenancy type is named (the Renters' Rights Act changes this in England); the summary says "two months' notice in writing" for this one flat.
- u1, u2: a sort code has six digits and an account number eight.
- u3 p2: a CV with short list-style lines (as real CVs look); check against the no-lists rule.
- Word-order items "Could you please confirm that you can attend?" and "Could you tell me what homework he has missed?"; u4 gap accepts *was charged* and *have been charged*.

## English B1, travel
- u3 p2: tipping customs stated generally (about 10% in UK restaurants, 15 to 20% in the USA, usually none in Japan); u3 p1: regional dishes (Cornish pasty, scone, Irish stew, Welsh cake, haggis).
- u5 p2: emergencies in the UK countryside: 999 or 112, police then Mountain Rescue, or the Coastguard on the coast (checked).

- u6 reading: idioms *my heart sank*, *You’d better come with me*; check they suit B1.

## German A2, travel
- Facts stated generally: tip of about 5 to 10 percent, *Stimmt so*, not every restaurant takes cards, allergen information on request, ID at hotel check-in, often no tickets on board local trains, the pharmacy *Notdienst* list on the door, 112.
- Real places in invented situations (building work between Hannover and Celle, *Halt in Fulda entfällt*, Bodensee, Lindau, Konstanz); invented town Lindenau is also a Leipzig district; *Hotel Lindenhof* is a common name.
- u5 accepts *offen* with *geöffnet*; u6 accepts *übel* with *schlecht*.
- Some items preview grammar above the A2 course book (*Wissen Sie, wo … ist?*, *nächste*, relative clauses with *an dem*).

## German A2, study and exams
- u6: A2 exams like Goethe A2 and telc A2 have four parts; a writing task of about 30 to 40 words on three points (check against the current formats).
- u2 p1: frequency words with rough percentages (*immer* 100 %, *oft* 60 % …) used as teaching aids.
- u3: *Liebes Team,* as the friendly greeting next to *Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren,*.
- Word-order items rely on capitals and commas (*Ich komme später, weil ich einen Termin habe.*); slightly above A2: *trotzdem*, *Wer …, kann …*.
- Dialogue titles are in English here (translated as explanations); the B1 model uses German titles.

## German A2, work

- Fixed phrases above A2 (*Ich kümmere mich sofort darum*, *Mir ist etwas dazwischengekommen*); choice questions in simple German; dialogue titles in English.

## German B1, travel
