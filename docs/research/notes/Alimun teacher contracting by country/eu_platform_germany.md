# Engaging remote language teachers as independent contractors: EU-level rules and Germany (research notes, as of 4 Nov 2026)

Not legal advice. These notes come from 24 tool calls. Primary text was available for Directive (EU) 2024/2831 (verbatim reproduction of EUR-Lex on lawplayer.com, because EUR-Lex itself blocked automated fetching) and the DRV transitional-rule page. Everything else rests on law-firm, insurer and adviser secondary sources. Where a point comes only from background knowledge and I could not cite it, it sits under "Inferences" or "Gaps" and is marked as uncited.

## Q1. EU Platform Work Directive (EU) 2024/2831: scope, presumption, transposition, algorithmic-management duties

### Takeaway
Alimun's planned model very probably makes it a "digital labour platform". It is provided online, at the request of learners, it organises paid work by individuals as an essential component, and it uses an automated matching engine. The directive covers platforms outside the EU when the work is performed in the Union. Two consequences follow. First, the rebuttable presumption of employment applies wherever "facts indicating direction and control" are found, from 2 December 2026 and only for periods after that date. Second, the algorithmic-management rules in Chapter III (no prohibited data processing, transparency, human oversight, human review, and a human making any account suspension) apply to genuine self-employed teachers too. As of early November 2026, Germany had not visibly finished transposing the directive, and the sources disagree on whether a draft bill exists.

### Cited Findings
- **Definition (Art. 2(1)(a)).** A "digital labour platform" is a person providing a service that meets all four of these conditions:
  - (i) it is provided "at least in part, at a distance by electronic means, such as by means of a website or a mobile application";
  - (ii) it is provided "at the request of a recipient of the service";
  - (iii) it "involves, as a necessary and essential component, the organisation of work performed by individuals in return for payment, irrespective of whether that work is performed online or in a certain location";
  - (iv) it "involves the use of automated monitoring systems or automated decision-making systems".

  — [Directive 2024/2831 Ch. I (verbatim EUR-Lex text via LawPlayer)](https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32024L2831/section/chapter-1)
- **The only exclusion (Art. 2(2)).** Providers "whose primary purpose is to exploit or share assets" and resale of goods by non-professionals. Online tutoring is not excluded. — [same](https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32024L2831/section/chapter-1)
- **Automated decision-making systems (Art. 2(1)(i)).** These are systems that take or support decisions affecting "their access to and the organisation of work assignments, their earnings, including the pricing of individual assignments … their working time … and their contractual status including the restriction, suspension or termination of their account". A matching engine that offers classes, sets priority and applies late-cancellation consequences falls inside this definition. — [same](https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32024L2831/section/chapter-1)
- **Territorial scope (Art. 1(3)).** The directive applies to platforms "organising platform work performed in the Union, irrespective of their place of establishment or of the law otherwise applicable". A Canadian company is caught for teachers who work from EU countries. — [same](https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32024L2831/section/chapter-1)
- **Two layers of protection (Art. 1(2)).** Employment-status rights apply to people who have, or are deemed to have, an employment relationship. The algorithmic-management data rules apply to "persons performing platform work in the Union, including those who do not have an employment contract or employment relationship". — [same](https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32024L2831/section/chapter-1)
- **Commentary on scope.** One commentary separates platforms that "organise work" (covered) from pure directories where clients find a specialist with no further algorithmic management (likely not covered). — [Lexgo / Luxembourg commentary](https://www.lexgo.lu/en/news-and-articles/13832-new-eu-directive-on-working-through-digital-labour-platforms); [Taylor Wessing, Dec 2024](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2024/12/platform-work-directive)
- **Facts over labels (Art. 4(2)).** Status is "guided primarily by the facts relating to the actual performance of work, including the use of automated monitoring systems or automated decision-making systems … irrespective of how the relationship is designated". — [Directive Ch. II](https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32024L2831/section/chapter-2)
- **The presumption (Art. 5).**
  - The relationship "shall be legally presumed to be an employment relationship where facts indicating direction and control, in accordance with national law … and with consideration to the case-law of the Court of Justice, are found". The platform must prove the contrary (Art. 5(1)).
  - It applies in all relevant administrative or judicial proceedings, but "shall not apply to proceedings which concern tax, criminal or social security matters", unless a Member State chooses to apply it there (Art. 5(3)).
  - Workers can trigger proceedings, and authorities must act on suspected misclassification (Art. 5(4)-(5)).
  - For contracts ongoing on 2 Dec 2026, the presumption covers only the period from that date (Art. 5(6)).

  — [Directive Ch. II](https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32024L2831/section/chapter-2)
- **No fixed list of indicators.** The final text has no EU-wide list of indicators; the trigger is "direction and control" under national law. Member States set the trigger and the rebuttal mechanism. — [Directive Ch. II](https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32024L2831/section/chapter-2); [Teamed, Germany page](https://www.teamed.global/eu-platform-work/germany)
- **Art. 7: banned processing.** The ban holds for all persons performing platform work, from recruitment onwards. It covers:
  - emotional or psychological state;
  - private conversations, including those with other workers;
  - data collected while the person is "not offering or performing platform work";
  - prediction of the exercise of fundamental rights;
  - inference of race, migration status, health, union membership and similar characteristics;
  - biometric identification against a database.

  It also applies to any automated system "taking or supporting decisions that affect persons performing platform work in any manner". — [Directive Ch. III](https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32024L2831/section/chapter-1) (Ch. III text retrieved from https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32024L2831/section/chapter-3)
- **Art. 8: DPIA.** Automated monitoring and decision-making counts as high-risk processing under GDPR Art. 35. The platform must seek the views of the people doing the work and give the assessment to workers' representatives. — [Directive Ch. III](https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32024L2831/section/chapter-3)
- **Art. 9: transparency to all persons performing platform work.** A written, plain-language document must cover:
  - the categories of decisions;
  - "the main parameters … and the relative importance of those main parameters", including how behaviour influences decisions;
  - the grounds for restricting, suspending or terminating an account or refusing payment.

  It must be given in concise form on the first working day, before changes are made, and on request. It must also be given to applicants before selection. Workers have a right to portability of their data, including ratings and reviews. — [Directive Ch. III](https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32024L2831/section/chapter-3)
- **Art. 10: human oversight.**
  - Impact evaluation at least every two years.
  - Enough trained human overseers with authority to override the system.
  - Art. 10(5): "Any decision to restrict, suspend or terminate the contractual relationship or the account of a person performing platform work or any other decision of equivalent detriment shall be taken by a human being."

  — [Directive Ch. III](https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32024L2831/section/chapter-3)
- **Art. 11: human review.**
  - Right to an explanation of any automated decision, and access to a designated contact person.
  - Written reasons for decisions on account suspension, refused payment or contractual status.
  - Review on request, with a written reply within two weeks; rectification or compensation if rights were infringed.
  - Art. 11(5): the article "shall not apply to persons performing platform work who are also business users as defined in … Regulation (EU) 2019/1150" (platform-to-business).

  — [Directive Ch. III](https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32024L2831/section/chapter-3)
- **Art. 12: safety and health.** This applies to "platform workers", meaning those who are or are deemed employees. It includes not using automated systems "in a manner that puts undue pressure on platform workers". — [Directive Ch. III](https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32024L2831/section/chapter-3)
- **Deadline.** Transposition is due 2 December 2026. — [EUR-Lex summary](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/EN/legal-content/summary/working-conditions-in-platform-work.html); [Teamed](https://www.teamed.global/eu-platform-work/germany)
- **Germany: no draft yet (Teamed, checked 3 Nov 2026).**
  - In April 2026 the government confirmed in parliamentary answers that BMAS was drafting a bill.
  - On 25 Sep 2026 the Bundesrat asked the government to bring a bill forward promptly and to require direct employment of couriers.
  - As of 3 Nov 2026 no Referentenentwurf had been published and no national presumption was in force.

  — [Teamed, PWD in Germany (2026)](https://www.teamed.global/eu-platform-work/germany)
- **Germany: conflicting report (DLA Piper, 12 Oct 2026).** A search snippet of a DLA Piper update says a "Platform Work Act" draft had been introduced for consultation and that a direct-employment requirement for subcontracted platform labour was being examined. I could not open the page (timeout). — [DLA Piper GENIE, Germany update](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2026/germany-moves-forward-on-transposing-the-eu-platform-work-directive). **The sources conflict.** An April 2026 tracker had Germany at the "consultation stage", with case law of the Federal Labour Court (BAG) to be built in. — [CXC tracker](https://www.cxcglobal.com/blog/cxc-comply/eu-directive-platform-work-guide/)
- **Other countries** (from search summaries of trackers; the pages were not opened and dates vary):
  - **Italy** has published draft implementing legislation for consultation.
  - **Spain** has opened public consultation on a draft law and already had a statutory presumption for delivery riders (the "Ley Rider").
  - **Czechia**'s government approved a bill on 31 Aug 2026, with 1 Jan 2027 proposed as the effective date.
  - **France** has only private members' bills (July 2026).
  - As of September 2026, one source said no Member State had a complete national law in force.

  — [CXC tracker](https://www.cxcglobal.com/blog/cxc-comply/eu-directive-platform-work-guide/); [Teamed Czechia](https://www.teamed.global/eu-platform-work/czechia); [Teamed France](https://www.teamed.global/eu-platform-work/france); [ADT Solution tracker](https://www.adtsolution.com/post/eu-platform-work-directive-transposition); [DLA Piper tracker](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2026/updated-tracker-for-the-european-platform-work-directive)

### Inferences
- **Alimun meets all four limbs of Art. 2(1)(a).** It is a website, learners request classes, organising teachers' paid work is essential to the service, and the matching engine is an automated decision-making system: it allocates classes, gives priority by hours and reliability, and removes priority after late cancellations. A school-like model (Alimun sells the class and assigns the teacher) is if anything more clearly "organisation of work" than a marketplace where learners choose a tutor. The scope cannot realistically be designed away while keeping automated matching. The practical goal is to comply with Chapter III and to avoid "direction and control" facts.
- **What Alimun must build even with genuine contractors:**
  - **(a) Art. 9 notice to teachers:** a plain-language written explanation of the matching engine, giving its parameters (eligibility, desired-hours gap, reliability record, cancellation history) and their relative weight. It goes to every teacher on day one and to applicants before selection.
  - **(b) Art. 7 data limits:** no emotion or sentiment analysis of teachers on video, no reading of private chats, no tracking while the teacher is offline, no biometric face-matching for identity checks against databases.
  - **(c) Art. 10(5):** any suspension, removal or equivalent detriment (for example blocking from offers) is taken by a named human, not automatically.
  - **(d) Art. 11:** an explanation on request, a designated contact person, written reasons for suspension or refused payment, and a review answered within 2 weeks.
  - **(e) Art. 8:** a GDPR data-protection impact assessment of the matching and reliability scoring, consulting teachers.
  - **(f) Art. 9(6):** a data-export tool covering teachers' ratings and reviews.
  - **(g) Biennial impact evaluation:** at least every two years, under Art. 10(1).
- **Timing.** Exact duties and penalties depend on each Member State's law, and many are late. The directive's text is the safe baseline to build to now.

- Recitals were not retrieved, including any on tutoring or education.

## Q2. EU cross-border basics: applicable law, social security, VAT, GDPR

### Takeaway
A choice of law in a contractor agreement does not shield Alimun if the relationship is in substance employment. Rome I Art. 8 then gives the teacher the mandatory protections of the country where they habitually work, and in practice, for remote teachers, that means their home country. Social security also follows the place where the work is physically done. VAT on a teacher's B2B invoice generally follows the customer's location. GDPR and the directive's data rules apply to teacher data.

### Cited Findings
- **Rome I Art. 8.** An employment contract is governed by the law the parties choose, but "such a choice of law may not … have the result of depriving" the employee of the protection of the law that would otherwise apply. Without a choice, the law of the habitual place of work applies: a real centre of activity (Koelzsch, C-29/10). The escape clause is rare. — [legislation.gov.uk Rome I Art. 8](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2008/593/article/8/data.htm); [Arletti Partners on remote working](https://arlettipartners.com/rome-i-regulation-jurisdiction-of-contracts-for-remote-working-abroad/)
- **Locatrans (C-485/24, Dec 2025).** The CJEU allowed the intended new habitual place of work to be considered under the escape clause. — [EAPIL, 15 Dec 2025](https://eapil.org/2025/12/15/cjeu-on-the-law-applicable-to-employment-contracts-in-locatrans-sarl-v-es/)
- **Rome I does not define "employee"**, so who is protected depends on how status is determined. — [Arletti Partners](https://arlettipartners.com/rome-i-regulation-and-labour-law-choice-of-governing-law-in-cross-border-employment-contracts/)
- **Germany's territorial principle.** It applies to a foreign company with no German branch whose staff work from home in Germany: German social security law applies, and the foreign employer carries full employer duties (contributions, payroll tax, DEÜV reporting). This is a non-authoritative forum source. The same source says that employment alone does not create a tax permanent establishment unless there is an organisational centre or fixed facility in Germany. — [DATEV community thread](https://www.datev-community.de/t5/Personalwirtschaft/Ausl%C3%A4ndisches-Unternehmen/td-p/520912); [DATEV magazin 2024](https://www.datev-magazin.de/praxis/ausland/rechtssicher-gestalten-115126)
- **Third countries.** For non-EU countries, outcomes depend on bilateral social security agreements. — [informationsportal.de](https://www.informationsportal.de/hkk/homeoffice-im-ausland-was-zu-beachten-ist/)
- **VAT on B2B services.**
  - The general rule (VAT Directive Art. 44) is that the supply takes place where the business customer is established. For an EU business customer, the supplier charges no VAT and the customer self-accounts under Art. 196.
  - The invoice shows "Reverse charge" (Art. 226(11a)) and both VAT numbers, not a 0% rate.
  - The sources were vendor guides, and the non-EU-customer case was not covered.

  — [Marosa VAT, invoices for services](https://marosavat.com/eu-vat-invoices-services/); [Marosa Germany reverse charge](https://marosavat.com/vat-manual-chapters/germany-reverse-charge)
- **Platform data rules.** Platform processing of teacher data is high-risk processing needing a DPIA (Directive Art. 8, referring to GDPR Art. 35). — [Directive Ch. III](https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32024L2831/section/chapter-3)

### Inferences
- **Choice of law will not help on status.** A "governed by the law of Ontario" (or other) clause will not stop a German teacher's court or the DRV applying German status rules. Status is decided on the facts, and German social security law applies territorially to work physically done in Germany.
- **VAT (uncited, from background knowledge of VAT Directive Art. 44 and German §3a(2) UStG).** While Alimun is a Canadian company, a German teacher's B2B service to it is supplied outside the EU, so no German VAT arises. The teacher invoices net with a "not taxable in Germany / place of supply outside EU" note. If Alimun moved to an EU state (for example Italy) and registered for VAT, EU teachers would invoice under the reverse charge and Alimun would self-account. Education exemptions for teaching supplied B2B to a school are country-specific and need checking.
- **Social security (uncited, from background knowledge of Regulation 883/2004).** Under Art. 11(3)(a), a self-employed person is insured in the Member State where they pursue the activity, which is the remote teacher's own country. Art. 13 applies only when someone works in two or more states. A genuine self-employed teacher therefore pays their own national contributions. If reclassified, the "employer" (Alimun) owes that country's employer contributions.

### Gaps
- Regulation 883/2004 Arts. 11-13 and A1 rules for self-employed people were not retrieved from primary text (search returned nothing specific).
- The VAT position for a non-EU customer, and the German exemption §4 Nr. 21 UStG for teaching, were not verified with a source.
- GDPR international-transfer issues (Canada adequacy) were not researched.

## Q3. Germany: Scheinselbständigkeit, status procedure, Herrenberg, §127 SGB IV, teachers' pension duty, VAT, employment costs, exposure

### Takeaway
Since the Herrenberg judgment (BSG, 28 Jun 2022, B 12 R 3/20 R), teachers no longer get special lenient treatment. Integration into the school's organisation weighs heavily, and many freelance school teachers have been found to be employees. The BSG followed this line in a Volkshochschule case on 5 Nov 2024 (B 12 BA 3/23 R).

§127 SGB IV is the transitional rule. It has applied since 1 Mar 2025 and, after an extension passed in March 2026, runs to 31 Dec 2027. It protects teaching contracts where both sides assumed self-employment and the teacher expressly consents. For those contracts, employee contributions start no earlier than 1 Jan 2028.

Self-employed teachers in Germany are compulsorily pension-insured themselves under §2 S.1 Nr.1 SGB VI and pay the contribution alone. If a teacher is reclassified, the employer cost is roughly 21% on top of pay.

A BMAS draft dated 26 Mar 2026 would create a new statutory category of self-employment coupled to pension insurance.

### Cited Findings
- **Herrenberg (BSG, 28.06.2022, B 12 R 3/20 R).**
  - The case concerned a music-school teacher with a freelance contract. The school provided rooms and instruments free of charge and dealt alone with the pupils. The BSG classed her as employed.
  - It dropped the earlier special criteria for teachers. The deciding factors were the actual performance of the contract and integration into the school's organisational processes, weighed in an overall assessment.
  - Lower courts: SG Stuttgart, S 12 R 5098/15; LSG Baden-Württemberg, L 13 BA 582/18.

  — [DRV: Was ist das Herrenberg-Urteil](https://www.deutsche-rentenversicherung.de/DRV/DE/Home/Herrenberg-Urteil/herrenberg-urteil-was-ist-das); [iww.de](https://www.iww.de/pfb/steuern-und-recht-aktuell/sozialversicherungspflicht-bundessozialgericht-rueckt-von-sonderrechtsprechung-fuer-lehrkraefte-ab-n162447); [ADVANT Beiten](https://www.advant-beiten.com/aktuelles/das-herrenberg-urteil-des-bundessozialgerichts-aufatmen-dank-uebergangsregelung); [sozialversicherung-kompetent](https://sozialversicherung-kompetent.de/urteile/gesetzliche-rentenversicherung/1653-herrenberg-urteil-lehrkraefte-selbststaendig-oder-beschaeftigt.html)
- **New social insurance criteria.** The social insurance umbrella bodies adjusted their criteria for teachers in 2023, and the new criteria have applied since July 2023. — [DRV transitional-rule page](https://www.deutsche-rentenversicherung.de/DRV/DE/Home/Herrenberg-Urteil/herrenberg-uebergangsregelung.html)
- **BSG 5.11.2024, B 12 BA 3/23 R.** A freelance Volkshochschule lecturer. The BSG overturned the LSG Niedersachsen-Bremen, which had found self-employment, and stressed that each case must be assessed individually. One database dates it 4 Nov 2024. — [rewis PDF](https://rewis.io/service/pdf/urteile/v32-05-11-2024-b-12-ba-323-r.pdf); [gesetze.co](https://gesetze.co/urteile/B_12_BA_3-23_R); [ADVANT Beiten](https://www.advant-beiten.com/en/news/das-herrenberg-urteil-des-bundessozialgerichts-aufatmen-dank-uebergangsregelung)
- **Indicators of employment for teachers** named by commentators: being bound by instructions, fixed working times, and a fixed place of work. — [TWW, 26 Aug 2026](https://tww.law/blog/2026/08/26/127-sgb-iv-selbststaendige-lehrkraefte-2027/)
- **§127 SGB IV (DRV official page).**
  - Applies to teaching activity (transfer of knowledge, skills and abilities, with reference to §2 S.1 Nr.1 SGB VI) at public or private educational institutions.
  - Conditions: both parties assumed self-employment at contract signing (signs include "Honorarvertrag" or "freie Mitarbeit" and no registration or contributions); the teacher consents; the activity continues unchanged.
  - Consent is given to the client, in writing or electronically (verbal is not enough), and kept in the payroll records.
  - Timing: fictitious self-employment runs from 1 Mar 2025 to 31 Dec 2027; employee insurance starts 1 Jan 2028. The rule was extended in March 2026 from end-2026.
  - During the transition no employee contributions are due, but teachers must register as self-employed and the DRV checks the self-employed pension duty.

  — [DRV, Herrenberg-Übergangsregelung](https://www.deutsche-rentenversicherung.de/DRV/DE/Home/Herrenberg-Urteil/herrenberg-uebergangsregelung.html)
- **The extension.** The Bundestag passed it on 5 Mar 2026 and the Bundesrat on 27 Mar 2026. Commentators say new consents are needed for 2027, that back-payments and criminal liability are excluded while the rule applies correctly, and that from 1 Jan 2028 contributions can be claimed only for the future. — [TWW](https://tww.law/blog/2026/08/26/127-sgb-iv-selbststaendige-lehrkraefte-2027/); [EEB Bayern](https://www.eeb-bayern.de/update-uebergangsregelung-zur-sozialversicherungspflicht-von-lehrkraeften-bis-31-12-2027-verlaengert/); [Neufang Akademie](https://online.neufang-akademie.de/artikel/7527/verlangerung-der-ubergangsregelung-127-sgb-iv-zum-herrenberg-urteil-bis-ende-2027)

- **Teachers' own pension duty (§2 S.1 Nr.1 SGB VI).**
  - Self-employed teachers are compulsorily insured unless they regularly employ an insured employee. This holds even with several clients (BSG B 12 RA 4/05 R).
  - Registration is due within 3 months (§190a SGB VI), with fines up to €2,500 (§320 SGB VI).
  - Earnings below the marginal-employment limit are exempt (§5 Abs.2 Nr.2 SGB VI).
  - A part-time language teacher's insurance was upheld by the Federal Constitutional Court.

  - A forecast decision (§7a(4a)) and a group assessment (§7a(4b), an employer-only, non-binding expert opinion for similar contracts) are available until 30 Jun 2027.

### Inferences
- **§127 covers private institutions, but Alimun is a poor fit.** The rule protects teaching at public or private educational institutions, so Alimun as a private online school may fall within it. It only helps contracts where both sides started out assuming self-employment, with documented teacher consent. It gives no protection from 1 Jan 2028, and the new §7 SGB IV regime is still a draft. A business model designed in late 2026 should not rely on it.
- **Who pays.** Under a genuine freelance contract, the German teacher pays the full 18.6% pension contribution personally. That is a real cost Alimun's rate card must absorb if it wants supply. If reclassified, Alimun as a foreign employer without a German establishment would owe about 21% employer contributions plus the employee share it failed to withhold.
- **Back-pay exposure (uncited, from background knowledge).** The standard limitation is 4 years, and 30 years for intentional evasion (§25 SGB IV). Criminal liability is possible under §266a StGB. Late-payment surcharges run at 1% per month (§24 SGB IV). These figures need verification.
- **VAT for a German teacher invoicing Canadian Alimun (uncited inference).** Likely not taxable in Germany. The Kleinunternehmer threshold mainly matters for the teacher's other, domestic clients.

## Q4. Which planned features push toward employment, and lawful alternatives

### Takeaway
Under the German overall assessment (instructions on time, place and content, integration into the organisation, entrepreneurial risk) and the directive's "direction and control" trigger, the riskiest planned features are these:
- reliability-based ranking with loss of priority as a penalty;
- long fixed 8-10-week groups combined with mandatory curriculum and platform;
- Alimun setting the pay unilaterally;
- paying for learner no-shows and standby;
- guaranteed hours ("priority to teachers below desired hours");
- a cover-pool duty.

None is automatically fatal. Together they describe integration into Alimun's organisation, which is exactly what decided Herrenberg. Mitigation means real freedom to refuse, to substitute and to price, with no sanctions beyond not being offered work. Regardless of status, the platform must also meet the Chapter III transparency and human-review duties.

### Cited Findings
- **The courts' tests.** German courts look at actual performance and integration into the institution's organisational and administrative processes, not the contract label. In Herrenberg the school provided rooms and instruments and was the only party dealing with pupils. — [iww.de](https://www.iww.de/pfb/steuern-und-recht-aktuell/sozialversicherungspflicht-bundessozialgericht-rueckt-von-sonderrechtsprechung-fuer-lehrkraefte-ab-n162447); [DRV](https://www.deutsche-rentenversicherung.de/DRV/DE/Home/Herrenberg-Urteil/herrenberg-urteil-was-ist-das)
- **Directive guidance.** Automated systems that organise work count as facts in the status assessment (Art. 4(2)). Automated decisions on access to assignments, earnings and pricing, working time and account restriction are regulated (Art. 2(1)(i)). Any "decision of equivalent detriment" must be taken by a human (Art. 10(5)). — [Directive Ch. I-III](https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32024L2831/section/chapter-2)
- **German indicators.** Commentators list instructions, fixed working times and a fixed place of work. Associations push to make the absence of a personal right to give instructions (§611a BGB) the central test, which shows that this is currently decisive and contested. — [TWW](https://tww.law/blog/2026/08/26/127-sgb-iv-selbststaendige-lehrkraefte-2027/); [Lobbyregister RV0027439](https://www.lobbyregister.bundestag.de/inhalte-der-interessenvertretung/regelungsvorhabensuche/RV0027439/501390)

### Inferences (feature-by-feature risk assessment; not legal advice; built on the cited tests)

**Higher risk:**
- **Reliability-based ranking and loss of priority for late cancellations.** This works as a disciplinary sanction (direction and control) and is an automated decision of "equivalent detriment". Lawful alternative:
  - Rank only on objective, teacher-controlled eligibility (approved levels, stated availability, language).
  - Use a neutral tie-break (rotation or randomness).
  - If cancellations are handled at all, use a contractual remedy: no fee, or a pre-agreed liquidated-damages clause that is the same for any business supplier.
  - Have a human decide any restriction, disclose the parameters (Art. 9) and offer review (Art. 11).
- **Alimun sets fixed hourly rates (€13-20) with no negotiation.** Pay set by the client is an employment indicator. Alternatives:
  - Teachers set their own rate within a published band, or bid per class.
  - Alimun's price to learners is separate, and premiums are part of the offer the teacher accepts or declines.
- **Paying for learner no-shows; standby fee for "instant class" availability.** Pay for time spent available, regardless of output, looks like wages for working time. It also shifts entrepreneurial risk away from the teacher. Alternatives:
  - Treat a no-show payment as a contractual booking or cancellation fee for a reserved slot, charged like any supplier's cancellation terms.
  - Make standby an optional, separately priced "reserved availability" service the teacher may decline, without any ranking consequence.
  - Note that this choice trades lower cost-of-risk for teachers against higher status risk.
- **Long fixed groups (8-10 weeks) with a fixed weekly schedule**, combined with mandatory Alimun curriculum, materials and video platform, and Alimun as the only party dealing with learners. This is the Herrenberg pattern of integration. Alternatives:
  - The teacher accepts each course as a discrete, fixed-scope engagement (a "course contract") and can decline renewal.
  - The curriculum is an outcome specification (CEFR level targets), with the teacher choosing methods and supplementary materials.
  - The teacher may use their own tools where feasible.
  - The teacher may be named to learners as an independent teacher.
- **"Priority to teachers below desired hours."** Managing teachers toward target hours resembles workforce planning for staff. Alternatives: drop it, or keep it as a transparent, disclosed fairness rule without any guarantee of hours.
- **Cover pool (15%) as a duty.** An obligation to be on call is an employment indicator. Alternative: cover offers go out like any other offer, the teacher is free to refuse, and there is no penalty.

**Lower risk or helpful facts to build in:**
- Teacher-published availability and approved levels, with an explicit right to refuse any offer and no penalty.
- First-to-accept allocation.
- An express right to substitute another qualified teacher; the substitute is approved only on objective qualification criteria.
- Non-exclusivity, and encouragement to work for multiple clients.
- The teacher's own equipment, internet and workspace.
- Invoicing by the teacher, or self-billing with consent.
- The teacher handles their own tax and social security and registers as self-employed. In Germany that includes the §2 Nr.1 SGB VI pension registration.
- No performance reviews in the employee sense: client feedback is shared, not used for discipline.
- No minimum hours.

**Not paying for classes Alimun cancels 48h+ ahead** is consistent with a contractor relationship: a fair notice term.

**Process:** for German teachers, consider a §7a SGB IV status procedure, or a group assessment while available (to 30 Jun 2027), on the final contract template to obtain legal certainty. Document §127 consents only for any existing German teaching contracts that qualify.
