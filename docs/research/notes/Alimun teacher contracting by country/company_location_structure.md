# Company location, owner residency and contractor payout structure for Alimun (2025-2026)

Not legal or tax advice. Research cut off at about 25 tool calls (4 Nov 2026). Many sources are secondary (accounting firms, vendor blogs); primary sources (CRA, Revenue.ie, Agenzia delle Entrate, Preply help centre) are noted where fetched.

## Q1. Candidate company locations: headline rates and costs

### Takeaway
On rates alone, Canada (CCPC small-business rate about 11-12% combined, falling to about 11.2% in Ontario and Quebec during 2026), Ireland (12.5% trading) and Estonia (0% retained / 22% on distribution) are the cheapest mainstream EU/Canada options, with UAE free zone at 0% only on "qualifying income" (which a B2C language school selling to consumers may struggle to meet). Morocco's standard rate converged to 20% in 2026; Italy is the most expensive (IRES 24% plus IRAP). But the owner's place of residence decides more than the incorporation country (see Q2).

### Cited Findings
**Canada**
- Federal small-business rate 9%; Ontario small-business rate 3.2% in 2025, combined 12.2%; Ontario general rate 11.5%, combined 26.5%; small-business limit $500,000 of active business income — [EY Ontario 2025 budget](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2025-1102-canada-ontario-budget-2025-26); [KPMG thresholds](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/ca/pdf/2026/01/ca-small-business-income-thresholds-for-2025-and-beyond.pdf)
- Ontario 2026 budget proposed cutting the CCPC small-business rate from 3.2% to 2.2% from 1 July 2026 — [PwC Ontario 2026 budget](https://www.pwc.com/ca/en/services/tax/budgets/2026/ontario.html); a less authoritative blog says the cut took effect (combined 11.2%) — [Beancount](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/22/ontario-small-business-tax-rate-cut-ccpc-guide) (verify enactment)
- British Columbia 2025: provincial 2%, combined 11% on first $500,000 — [Manning Elliott 2025 table](https://manningelliott.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/2025-Corporate-Tax.pdf)
- Quebec 2025: 3.2% provincial, combined 12.2%, with extra Quebec-specific eligibility conditions for the small business deduction — [Raymond James](https://www.raymondjames.ca/victoria/insights/2025/03/27/2025-quebec-budget-highlights); Quebec announced cut to 2.2% for years beginning after 29 April 2026 — [Beancount Quebec](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/09/05/quebec-small-business-deduction-rate-cut-2-2-percent-guide) (secondary; verify with Revenu Québec)
- Note: the RSM 2025 federal/provincial rate table is a useful single source — [RSM 2025 rates](https://rsmcanada.com/content/dam/rsm/insights/services/business-tax/1pdf/2025-federal-provincial-territorial-corporate-income-tax-rates.pdf)

**Ireland**
- Standard 12.5% rate on trading income; 25% on non-trading income — [Revenue Tax and Duty Manual Part 02](https://www.revenue.ie/en/tax-professionals/tdm/income-tax-capital-gains-tax-corporation-tax/part-02/02-02-01-20220603114618.pdf); [Deloitte Ireland highlights 2024](https://www2.deloitte.com/content/dam/Deloitte/global/Documents/Tax/dttl-tax-irelandhighlights-2024.pdf)
- Companies incorporated in Ireland on/after 1 Jan 2015 are tax resident unless a treaty makes them resident elsewhere; foreign-incorporated companies resident if central management and control in Ireland; Revenue and courts emphasise where board meetings are held — [Maples Chambers guide 2024](https://maples.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Chambers-Global-Practice-Guide-Corporate-Tax-2024.pdf)
- Companies Act 2014 s.137: at least one EEA-resident director or a bond; residence not citizenship counts (an Italian passport-holder living in Canada still needs the bond); UK residents stopped counting from 1 Jan 2021 — [RBK](https://www.rbk.ie/services/audit-assurance/corporate-compliance-governance/eea-resident-directors); [Law Society Gazette](https://www.lawsociety.ie/gazette/top-stories/company-directors-must-be-eu-resident-post-brexit-cro-warns/)
- Bond: €25,000 guarantee for at least two years, premium quoted around €1,500-2,000 (commercial provider, has interest) — [Open Forest s.137 bond](https://www.openforest.co/ie/ireland-company-incorporation/section-137-bond); exemption possible if CRO certifies a "real and continuous link" with economic activity in Ireland — [RBK](https://www.rbk.ie/services/audit-assurance/corporate-compliance-governance/eea-resident-directors)

**Italy**
- IRES 24% applies to an innovative startup's profits (no reduced rate) — [Stripe guide to innovative startups in Italy](https://stripe.com/au/resources/more/how-to-set-up-an-innovative-startup-italy)
- Startup innovativa: unlisted capital company, max 5 years old, SME, objective plus at least one subjective innovation requirement; rules amended by Law 193/2024 (annual competition law), investor deduction raised from 30% to 65% from 1 Jan 2025 — [Mamacrowd](https://mamacrowd.com/en/news/nuove-opportunita-startup-investitori-legge-annuale-mercato-concorrenza-2024); [Portolano](https://portolano.it/news/the-new-regulation-on-innovative-start-ups-in-italy)
- Startups exempt from "dummy company" minimum-income rules — [Telfa](https://www.telfa.law/?p=7958)

**Estonia**
- Distributed profits taxed 22% (22/78 of net distribution); retained profits untaxed; the 14% regular-distribution rate abolished from 2025 — [EMTA income and social taxes](https://emta.ee/en/business-client/taxes-and-payment/income-and-social-taxes); [EY July 2025](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/estonia-abolishes-temporary-defense-tax-increases-several-tax-rates)
- Planned rise to 24% for 2026 reportedly cancelled by the Riigikogu in Dec 2025; KPMG 2026 tax card lists 22% — [KPMG Estonia 2026 tax card](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/ee/pdf/tax-cards/2026%20Tax%20Card-web.pdf.coredownload.inline.pdf); [1Office](https://1office.co/blog/estonian-ou-tax-guide-2026/) (EY July 2025 had said 24% from 2026; later sources supersede)

**Morocco**
- Finance Law 2023 (Law 50-22) phased convergence 2023-2026 to: 20% on net taxable profit below MAD 100 million, 35% at/above MAD 100 million, 40% for banks/insurers, from 1 Jan 2026 — [EY Finance Law 2023](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/morocco-enacts-finance-law-2023-a-review-of-key-tax-measures); [Upsilon Consulting](https://upsilon-consulting.com/en/corporate-income-tax-in-morocco/); one source says Finance Law 2026 (Law 50-25) revised bands — [Global Law Experts](https://globallawexperts.com/?p=1466927) (conflict, unverified)
- Minimum contribution 0.25% of turnover (min MAD 3,000) — [Upsilon](https://upsilon-consulting.com/en/corporate-income-tax-calculation/)
- Foreign-exchange controls remain (no full convertibility); General Instruction IGOC 2024 from 2 Jan 2024; a 2026 instruction eases rules for SMEs/startups and lets exporters keep more export earnings in foreign currency — [US Commerce country guide](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/morocco-trade-financing); [DLA Piper on IGOC 2024](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2024/02/nouveautes-introduites-par-linstruction-de-loffice-des-changes-du-2-janvier-2024); [North Africa Post](https://northafricapost.com/?p=94999); [Office des Changes regulation](https://www.oc.gov.ma/en/regulation)

**UAE free zone**
- Qualifying Free Zone Person: 0% on qualifying income, 9% on other income; non-qualifying income must stay under the lower of 5% of revenue or AED 5m (de minimis) or QFZP status is lost for that period and reportedly the following 4 periods; substance, audited accounts and transfer pricing conditions every period — [PwC Middle East on Cabinet/Ministerial decisions 2023](https://pwc.com/m1/en/services/tax/me-tax-legal-news/2023/cabinet-ministerial-decisions-release-corporate-tax-rules-for-free-zones.html); [BDO](https://bdo.global/en-gb/insights/tax/world-wide-tax/united-arab-emirates-guidance-on-free-zone-business-issued); [Mondaq](https://mondaq.com/tax-authorities/1420716/uae-corporate-tax-who-is-considered-a-qualifying-free-zone-person)

### Inferences
- Under the UAE rules, income from selling to consumers (natural persons) outside the free zone is generally not qualifying income unless it falls in a listed qualifying activity; a B2C online school would likely pay 9% on most profit and carry full substance costs. Treat UAE as weak for Alimun unless the owner moves there (verify qualifying-activity list).
- Estonia's 0% on retained profit only helps if profits stay in the company; the owner paying himself in Canada triggers 22% plus Canadian personal tax.
- Italy is only sensible if the owner moves to Italy, where impatriati may cut personal tax (Q2).

### Gaps
- UK Ltd rates (25% main / 19% small profits) and US Delaware LLC/C-corp were not verified in this session (budget). Italian IRAP rate (generally 3.9%) not confirmed by a source here. SR&ED credit rates not researched. Casablanca Finance City current rates and offshoring incentives not researched. Company formation/annual running costs (accountant, audit, registered agent) per jurisdiction not collected.

## Q2. Owner residency trap (central management and control)

### Takeaway
While the owner lives and makes decisions in Canada, a foreign company he runs alone is very likely Canadian-resident under common law (central management and control), so it is taxed in Canada anyway, with dual-residence risk resolved by treaty. A Canadian corporation is the simplest and most honest fit while he lives in Canada. Moving abroad changes the picture (Italy has a 183-day presence test and an "ordinary management" test for companies from 2024).

### Cited Findings
- CRA: a corporation incorporated in Canada after 26 April 1965 is deemed resident (ITA s.250(4)); otherwise common-law test — "the real business is carried on where the central management and control abides"; what matters is where control is actually exercised, not what the articles say; treaties resolve dual residence and s.250(5) deems a treaty-resident-elsewhere corporation non-resident — [CRA Residency of a corporation](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/businesses-international-non-resident-taxes/residency-a-corporation.html)
- Board meetings in a country are not alone sufficient to prove residence there — [CRA](https://www.cra-arc.gc.ca/tx/nnrsdnts/bsnss/bs-rs-eng.html)
- If the foreign company is not Canadian-resident, a Canadian shareholder must file T1134 for foreign affiliates; active business income of a foreign affiliate is generally not FAPI, but filing applies regardless and penalties apply even with no tax owing — [CRA T1134 Q&A](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/information-been-moved/foreign-reporting/questions-answers-about-form-t1134.html); [RSM](https://rsmcanada.com/insights/services/business-tax-insights/best-practices-for-filing-Form-information-return.html)
- Italy 2024 reform (Legislative Decree 209/2023): individuals resident if present in Italy more than half the year (counting partial days), even without registration; companies resident if registered office, place of effective management, or principal place of ordinary management is in Italy for most of the tax period; Agenzia delle Entrate Circular 20/2024 gives guidance — [Agenzia delle Entrate Circular 20/2024 (EN)](https://agenziaentrate.gov.it/portale/documents/20143/6519685/Circolare_20_del_4_11_2024_Eng.pdf/bdacb2e0-a699-5180-719e-665210459d75); [Rödl](https://www.roedl.com/insights/tax-newsletter-italien/2024-09/italy-new-companies-criteria-tax-residence-purposes); [McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/italian-tax-reform-new-residence-definitions-for-individuals-and-corporations/)
- Italian impatriati regime from 2024: 50% of qualifying employment/self-employment income exempt (60% with a minor child), cap €600,000, requires 3 prior years of non-residence, high qualification and a minimum stay commitment (4 or 5 years — sources conflict) — [International Tax Review](https://www.internationaltaxreview.com/article/2cp2ulic5iblvdo6zsnb4/sponsored/italy-continues-reforms-by-revising-its-international-tax-principles); [Assolombarda](https://www.assolombarda.it/servizi/fisco/riforma-fiscale/i-redditi-delle-persone-fisiche/regime-impatriati); [A&P](https://arlettipartners.com/the-special-tax-regime-for-inpatriate-workers/)

### Inferences
- Estonia e-Residency OÜ, Irish Ltd, UK Ltd or UAE FZ company run solely by the owner from Canada: central management and control in Canada makes it Canadian-resident at common law. The Canada-Ireland / Canada-UK / Canada-Estonia treaties' tie-breakers then apply (CRA says the usual tie-breaker points to the place of creation, but newer treaties/MLI often use competent-authority agreement) — outcome uncertain and costly to defend. Result: Canadian tax plus foreign compliance, i.e. higher cost, not lower.
- Non-CCPC risk: a foreign-incorporated company is not a "Canadian-controlled private corporation", so even if Canadian-resident it loses the ~9-12% small-business rate and pays the general ~26.5% (inference from CCPC definition; verify).
- If the owner is not a Canadian citizen/PR and leaves Canada, the analysis restarts in his new country; living in Italy for 183+ days makes him Italian-resident and any company he manages from Italy Italian-resident.

### Gaps
- Moroccan individual and corporate residence rules not researched. Canada-Ireland, Canada-Estonia, Canada-UAE treaty tie-breaker texts not checked. Whether the owner is a Canadian citizen/PR is unknown and matters for immigration and departure tax.

## Q3. Consumer-side tax by location (VAT/GST, Stripe)

### Takeaway
Live online classes are not clearly "electronically supplied services"; since 1 Jan 2025 EU rules tax live-streamed educational events where the consumer lives, and non-EU sellers can use the non-Union OSS. Canada: a Canadian company is a normal GST/HST registrant (small-supplier threshold not verified here) and services to non-residents outside Canada are generally zero-rated; a foreign seller must register under simplified GST/HST once B2C digital sales to Canadians exceed CAD 30,000. Stripe works in Canada, Ireland and (with limits) UAE, but not Morocco.

### Cited Findings
- From 1 Jan 2025, live virtual events (incl. online classes) supplied to consumers are taxed where the consumer resides — [BDO UK](https://bdo.co.uk/en-gb/insights/tax/vat-and-indirect-taxes/new-vat-rules-for-online-entertainment-culture-and-education-in-2025); treatment of live 1:1 tutoring is disputed across secondary sources — [good-vat guide](https://good-vat.pages.dev/guides/selling-to-eu/non-union-oss-digital-services)
- Non-Union OSS: non-EU businesses can register in any one EU member state to account for B2C services VAT — [EU Your Europe OSS](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/taxation/vat/one-stop-shop/indexamp_hr.htm); [Polish MoF OSS principles](https://www.podatki.gov.pl/media/9448/zasady-rozliczania-podatku-vat-w-procedurze-nieunijnej-en.docx)
- Preply (13 May 2025): "live online tutoring is not considered a digital service", so Preply is not a deemed supplier; tutors supply lessons directly and are responsible for EU VAT; Preply's own fee to EU-VAT-registered tutors is reverse-charged — [Preply help centre](https://help.preply.com/en/articles/11364005-how-eu-vat-may-apply-to-tutoring-on-preply)
- Canada: non-resident vendors of digital services must register (simplified regime) once B2C sales to Canadian consumers exceed CAD 30,000 in 12 months, since 1 July 2021; no input tax credits under simplified registration — [Forvis Mazars](https://www.forvismazars.com/ca/en/insights/latest-news/new-gst-hst-rules-for-digital-economy-businesses); [BDO Canada](https://www.bdo.ca/en-ca/insights/tax/tax-articles/digital-sales-tax-in-canada/)
- Canada exports: Sch. VI Part V s.7 zero-rates services to non-residents unless excluded (e.g. individual in Canada when served); s.18 specifically covers instruction leading to certificates/licences — [CRA GST/HST Memorandum 4.5.3](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/4-5-3/exports-services-intangible-personal-property.html); [CRA Educational services to non-residents](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/20-8/educational-services-made-non-resident.html); [Revenu Québec](https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/businesses/consumption-taxes/gsthst-and-qst/special-cases-gsthst-and-qst/exports-outside-canada-and-shipping-outside-quebec/sales-of-services-to-non-residents/)
- Stripe: Canada and Ireland fully supported; UAE supported with some feature limits; Morocco not supported for merchant accounts (secondary lists) — [Dodo Payments 2026 list](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/stripe-supported-countries-alternatives); [Wise on Stripe international](https://wise.com/us/blog/stripe-international)

### Inferences
- Whatever the location, Alimun selling to EU consumers likely needs EU VAT handling (non-Union OSS from Canada, Union OSS from Ireland/Estonia/Italy). An EU company also has domestic VAT on its home-country customers and loses the ability to treat non-EU sales simply; a Canadian company zero-rates most foreign B2C services but must check the 2025 EU rules.
- A Moroccan company cannot use Stripe directly; this alone makes Morocco impractical as the selling entity.
- Teaching education services may be VAT-exempt in some EU states when supplied by recognised bodies or private teachers — not researched.

### Gaps
- Canadian small-supplier threshold (CAD 30,000) for domestic registrants and QST rules not verified. GST/VAT rules in India, Turkey, Egypt, Gulf, Latin America for foreign digital/education sellers not researched (other researchers may cover). Stripe fees by country not collected.

## Q4. Paying contractors abroad: withholding, reporting, rails and costs

### Takeaway
From Canada, Regulation 105 (15% withholding, plus 9% Quebec) only applies to services rendered in Canada, so teachers teaching from abroad are generally not subject to Canadian withholding (T4A-NR relates to Reg 105 payments). Payout rails: Stripe Connect cross-border payouts (reported 0.25% fee; Canadian platforms can pay to US/UK/EEA/CH regions self-serve), contractor-management platforms at about $29-49 per contractor per month, and EOR at about $599-699 per employee per month.

### Cited Findings
- Reg 105(1): payer withholds 15% of fees to a non-resident "in respect of services rendered in Canada"; only the portion allocable to Canada is caught; extra 9% for Quebec; T4A-NR required; waivers/treaty relief possible — [MLT Aikins](https://www.mltaikins.com/insights/withholding-tax-on-services-provided-in-canada-by-a-non-resident-understanding-regulation-105-of-the-income-tax-act/); [RSM Canada](https://rsmcanada.com/insights/services/business-tax-insights/regulation-105-what-to-know-about-withholding-tax-and-cra-audits.html); [Deloitte](https://www2.deloitte.com/content/dam/Deloitte/ca/Documents/tax/ca-EN-ITSL-Regulation-105-withholding-AODA.PDF)
- Stripe Connect cross-border payouts: supported platform regions include US, Canada, UK, EEA, Switzerland; self-serve payouts not supported outside listed regions; fee depends on recipient location (third-party sources report 0.25%) — [Stripe docs](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/cross-border-payouts); [Stripe support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/pay-out-to-connected-accounts-in-other-countries); [Stripe Connect pricing](https://stripe.com/it-us/connect/pricing)
- Contractor platforms: Deel $49/contractor/month; Remote $29 ($99 Plus tier with indemnity); Oyster $29 — [Costbench Deel vs Oyster](https://www.costbench.com/compare/deel-vs-oyster-hr/); [Costbench contractor software](https://costbench.com/best/contractor-management-software)
- EOR: Deel $599/employee/month; Remote $599-699 (sources conflict); Oyster $699 — [Costbench Deel vs Remote](https://costbench.com/compare/deel-vs-remote); [Costbench best EOR](https://costbench.com/best/best-eor-software/)

### Inferences
- Stripe Connect alone will not reach teachers in Morocco, Algeria, Latin America, Gulf, Australia/NZ unless those countries are in Stripe's list; Wise Business or Payoneer, or a contractor platform, fills those gaps (fees not verified here).
- Per-contractor monthly fees of $29-49 are large relative to part-time per-hour teaching income; a mix (direct agreements plus Wise for low-risk countries, platform only where needed) is cheaper.

### Gaps
- Italy, Ireland and Morocco withholding on payments to foreign contractors for services performed abroad not researched (general expectation: none on services performed abroad, but unverified). Wise Business and Payoneer fees, Ontop/Rippling pricing, and Stripe's official per-country cross-border payout fee not confirmed. Whether a Canadian payer must issue any slip for non-residents working outside Canada not confirmed.

## Q5. Contracting model: direct contractors vs platform vs EOR vs marketplace

### Takeaway
The marketplace/agent model (teacher supplies the learner; platform takes a commission), as Preply describes, shifts VAT and service liability to teachers and lowers misclassification risk because teachers set prices, choose learners and run their own business — but it changes Alimun's product (fixed classes, subscription) and must be real in substance. A contractor platform adds compliance paperwork but does not by itself cure misclassification; EOR is the safe option for any teacher who is in practice an employee (high-control, full-time, in countries like Spain, Italy, Germany).

### Cited Findings
- Preply treats the tutor as supplier of lessons to students and itself as not a deemed supplier, charging tutors a service fee (reverse-charged for EU VAT-registered tutors) — [Preply help centre](https://help.preply.com/en/articles/11364005-how-eu-vat-may-apply-to-tutoring-on-preply)
- EU Article 9a Implementing Regulation presumes platforms facilitating electronically supplied services act in their own name (deemed supplier); CJEU (Fenix) upheld its validity — [Baker McKenzie](https://insightplus.bakermckenzie.com/bm/tax/european-union-the-court-of-justice-issues-judgment-on-validity-of-deemed-reseller-rules-for-online-platforms-facilitating-electronic-supplied-services); [International Tax Review](https://www.internationaltaxreview.com/article/2ffcwtbrymcmhjrxgodts/sponsored/from-matchmaker-to-supplier-deemed-supplies-and-platform-classification-under-eu-vat)
- Remote's $99 Plus contractor tier includes misclassification indemnity — [Costbench](https://costbench.com/best/contractor-management-software)

### Inferences
- If Alimun's classes are classified as electronically supplied services (e.g. recorded/automated content, app subscription), Article 9a would make Alimun the deemed supplier anyway, so the marketplace model's VAT benefit only applies to the live-teaching part.
- Under the current product (Alimun sets prices, schedules, curriculum, subscription), Alimun is the supplier to learners and teachers are its subcontractors; misclassification risk is driven by control and dependence in each teacher's country, not by the payment rail.
- Likely lowest-cost lawful structure while the owner lives in Canada: a Canadian (provincial or federal) CCPC as the selling entity, Stripe Canada, non-Union OSS for EU consumers, direct independent-contractor agreements paid via Stripe Connect/Wise, EOR only for teachers who work like employees.

### Gaps
- EU Platform Work Directive (2024/2831) presumption of employment and its transposition deadline (Dec 2026) not researched. Country-specific misclassification tests (Spain, Italy, Germany, UK IR35-style off-payroll) not researched here.
