# Engaging remote language teachers in the UK, Ireland, Sweden, Denmark, Finland, Norway and the Netherlands (2025-2026)

Not legal advice. Research cut-off: 4 Nov 2026, about 25 tool calls. Most sources are secondary (law firms, payroll vendors, trade press). Where a primary authority page was read, it is marked "(primary)". Points marked "background, not verified this session" come from general knowledge and need a source before the report relies on them.

## Cross-cutting: what does a foreign client with no local establishment have to do? (registration, withholding, reporting, social security)

### Takeaway
If the teacher is a genuine contractor, a foreign client usually has no duty to register, withhold or report anywhere in the seven countries. The teacher self-assesses. Sweden and Finland are the exceptions to watch: their 30% / prepayment withholding rules bite on payers, and it is not settled whether they reach a payer with no local presence. If a teacher is reclassified as an employee, the picture flips. A foreign employer typically owes host-country social security, and in Sweden and Norway it also owes withholding or reporting even with no permanent establishment. In Ireland, payroll obligations arise once the teacher works there beyond short-visit exemptions. The real cost of misclassification is back employer contributions, not just tax.

### Cited Findings
- Sweden (primary): a foreign employer "ska göra skatteavdrag med 30 procent" on pay for work done in Sweden, and "ska i de flesta fall betala arbetsgivaravgifter i Sverige". If the employer has no permanent establishment, employer and employee can sign a *socialavgiftsavtal* so that the employee reports and pays the employer contributions himself — [Skatteverket, Lön från utländska arbetsgivare utan fast driftställe](https://www.skatteverket.se/privat/internationellt/lonfranutlandskaarbetsgivare.4.906b37c10bd295ff4880001233.html)
- Sweden, contractor side: a payer doesn't withhold if the recipient holds F-skatt. Otherwise 30% withholding generally applies to payment for work, and is reported in the employer declaration — [Skatteverket FAQ](https://skatteverket.se/foretag/etjansterochblanketter/svarpavanligafragor/ekonomisktarbetsgivarbegrepp.4.18e1b10334ebe8bc80002640.html); [Skatteverket, Skatteavdrag med 30 procent](https://www.skatteverket.se/foretag/arbetsgivare/arbetsgivaravgifterochskatteavdrag/skatteavdrag/skatteavdragmed30procent.4.5c1163881590be297b511bc9.html)
- Norway (primary): "All foreign employers must report information about salary and benefits and withholding tax regardless of tax liability" for work performed in Norway. Employer's national insurance contributions must be specified for work in Norway unless the recipient is outside the Norwegian scheme under the EEA Agreement or a convention, and only exceptions registered with the Tax Administration count — [Skatteetaten, Foreign employers' reporting obligation in the a-melding](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/business-and-organisation/employer/the-a-melding/the-a-melding-guide/special-groups/foreign-employers-reporting-obligation-in-the-a-melding)
- Norway: Skatteetaten has a separate a-melding category for "freelancers, contractors and fee recipients", meaning people paid outside an employment relationship *without* being self-employed (næringsdrivende). Fees to such persons are reportable. Payments to genuinely self-employed persons are not part of that category — [Skatteetaten, Freelancers, contractors and fee recipients](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/business-and-organisation/employer/the-a-melding/the-a-melding-guide/employment/type-of-employment/freelancers-contractors-and-fee-recipients/)
- Finland (primary guidance): registration in the prepayment register (ennakkoperintärekisteri) is voluntary. A payer need not withhold on work compensation paid to a registered business, but must withhold on work compensation paid to an unregistered one. A payer of *wages* must always withhold. A foreign employer with no permanent establishment in Finland is, as a rule, not obliged to withhold — [Vero, Ennakkoperintärekisteröinnin vaikutukset yritystoimintaan](https://www.vero.fi/syventavat-vero-ohjeet/ohje-hakusivu/47881/ennakkoperintarekisteroinnin-vaikutukset-yritystoimintaan5/); [Vero, Ennakonpidätyksen toimittaminen](https://vero.fi/syventavat-vero-ohjeet/ohje-hakusivu/48735/ennakonpidatyksen-toimittaminen9/)
- Denmark: a foreign employer *with* a Danish permanent establishment must withhold Danish tax and AM-bidrag and register — [skat.dk](https://skat.dk/media/iddg3szc/p246_dk_2018.pdf). Skattestyrelsen guidance says AM-bidrag is withheld only where the employer is Danish or has a Danish permanent establishment — [info.skat.dk](https://info.skat.dk/data.aspx?oid=79287). Danish practice generally treats an employee's private choice to work from home as not creating a permanent establishment — [Nordic Council report 2023](https://pub.norden.org/nord2023-024/problemomrade-1-fast-driftssted-ved-hjemmearbejde.html). BDO says a foreign employer still has to pay ATP and workplace-injury insurance contributions, roughly DKK 15,000 a year — [BDO 2023](https://www.bdo.dk/da-dk/faglig-info/depechen/depechen-artikler-2023/fjernarbejde-over-landegraenser-hvad-med-skatten). These two sources conflict on ATP.
- Denmark, contractor side: fees for personal work that are B-income fall under the B-income reporting rule in skatteindberetningsloven — [lovguiden, LBK 1059 af 21/08/2025](https://www.lovguiden.dk/loven/skatteindberetningsloven/4). Danish residents must declare foreign income themselves even if it was taxed abroad — [BDO](https://www.bdo.dk/da-dk/faglig-info/depechen/depechen-artikler-2023/husk-at-indberette-udenlandsk-indkomst-og-formue-til-skat). No source found says whether a foreign client with no Danish presence must report B-income in eIndkomst.
- Ireland: a foreign employer may have an Irish payroll obligation for workdays an employee spends in Ireland. The 30-workday exemption covers visiting employees who are resident in a DTA country. Otherwise a dispensation is needed within 30 days, or Irish payroll taxes apply from day one. PRSI is generally due unless an A1/certificate of coverage is held — [Chartered Accountants Ireland, Tax obligations for foreign employers with workers in Ireland](https://www.charteredaccountants.ie/Accountancy-Ireland/Home/AI-Articles/tax-obligations-for-foreign-employers-with-workers-in-ireland); [Revenue TDM Part 42-04-65](https://revenue.ie:443/en/tax-professionals/tdm/income-tax-capital-gains-tax-corporation-tax/part-42/42-04-65.pdf)
- EU/EEA social security: employer contribution duties under the coordination rules point to Regulation 987/2009 art. 21. Under it, an employer with no establishment in the competent state must still fulfil that state's contribution obligations, or agree that the employee does it on the employer's behalf — [HMRC NIM33120](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/national-insurance-manual/nim33120). A teacher who works only from home in one country is normally insured there, under the lex loci laboris rule in art. 11(3)(a) of Reg. 883/2004 — [lawandmore.eu](https://lawandmore.eu/cross-border-remote-work-eu-framework-agreement-telework/).

### Inferences
- Alimun is a Canadian company with no EU/EEA/UK establishment. For a genuine contractor in each country, the likely position is: no registration, no withholding, no reporting, and the teacher self-assesses. The open questions are the Danish B-income reporting duty and the Swedish/Finnish payer withholding rules, which the sources don't clearly apply to a payer abroad. Collecting each teacher's local business registration (F-skatt certificate, Finnish prepayment-register extract, Danish CVR, UK UTR or Irish self-assessment confirmation, Norwegian ENK/org.nr, Dutch KvK) is cheap and removes most of the Swedish and Finnish withholding risk. Inference.
- If a teacher is reclassified, a foreign employer still owes host-country employer social security under Reg. 987/2009 art. 21, and in Norway and Sweden it also owes reporting. Back contributions plus penalties are the main exposure. Inference.
- Approximate employer social-security add-ons if Alimun employed teachers directly or via an EOR: UK 15% above £5,000; Ireland 8.9-11.25% (11.40% from Oct 2026); Sweden about 31.42%; Norway 14.1% (zone 1); Finland about 20%; Denmark low (ATP and insurance only); Netherlands about 18-20%. Only the UK and Irish figures were verified this session (see below). The others are background, not verified.

### Gaps
- No primary source found on whether Danish eIndkomst B-income reporting binds a foreign payer with no Danish presence.
- No primary source found on whether Sweden's 30% withholding for payments to recipients without F-skatt reaches a payer with no Swedish establishment. Skatteverket's foreign-payer page covers employees, not contractors.
- The conflict over Danish ATP for a foreign employer without a permanent establishment is unresolved.
- Exact Swedish, Norwegian, Finnish, Danish and Dutch employer contribution rates for 2026 were not verified this session.

## UK: status tests, IR35, worker rights, Employment Rights Act 2025, employment cost

### Takeaway
UK law has three statuses: employee, worker and self-employed. "Worker" status (minimum wage, holiday pay) is the realistic risk for platform teachers. The off-payroll (IR35) rules don't bind a client with no UK presence, and small companies are exempt anyway. The cheapest compliant route is a genuine self-employed contract with real freedom to accept or refuse work. If worker status looks likely, an alternative is to build 12.07% holiday pay into the rate and stay above the National Minimum Wage, as MyTutor does for its schools tutors. Direct employment adds 15% employer NICs above £5,000 a year.

### Cited Findings
- Off-payroll working: Finance Bill 2020 excluded "wholly overseas organisations with no UK presence" from the client duties. In that case the contractor's PSC decides IR35 itself. Small companies are also exempt. A client that sets up a UK presence (and isn't small) must start issuing status determination statements. If the worker isn't chargeable to UK tax or NICs, the rules don't apply — [Markel/Qdos](https://uk.markel.com/about-us/news-and-insights/ir35-private-sector-what-happens-if-the-client-is-overseas); [Qdos, clarification regarding overseas clients](https://qdoscontractor.com/ir35/off-payroll-working/clarification-regarding-overseas-clients)
- IR35 only applies where the individual works through an intermediary (a PSC). A sole-trader teacher invoicing in their own name is outside IR35 entirely; their status is decided under the general employment-status tests — background, not verified this session (see HMRC ESM manual).
- Employer NICs: 15% above a £5,000 secondary threshold from 6 April 2025 (previously 13.8% above £9,100). Employment Allowance rose to £10,500 and the £100,000 eligibility cap was removed — [ICAEW](https://www.icaew.com/insights/tax-news/2024/oct-2024/national-insurance-increases-for-employers-announced-in-budget); [GOV.UK impact assessment](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/employer-national-insurance-contributions-and-employment-allowance-changes-impact-assessment/employer-national-insurance-contributions-nics-and-employment-allowance-changes-screening-equality-impact-assessment). Sources conflict on how long the £5,000 threshold lasts: to April 2028 or frozen to April 2031 — [PayFit](https://payfit.com/blog/secondary-threshold).
- Employment Rights Act 2025: Royal Assent 18 December 2025, phased in over 2026-2027 — [NHS Employers](https://www.nhsemployers.org/articles/employment-rights-act-2025). Reported milestones: SSP changes and the Fair Work Agency in April 2026; a longer tribunal time limit in October 2026; a six-month unfair-dismissal qualifying period from January 2027; guaranteed-hours rules for zero-hours staff in 2027 — [Employment Hero](https://employmenthero.com/uk/blog/employment-rights-act-2025-timeline/); [Mondaq/Withers](https://www.mondaq.com/uk/employee-rights-labour-relations/1749200/employment-rights-act-2025-what-is-the-current-timeline-for-implementation). Single worker status is not in the Act; it was left for consultation — background, not verified this session.
- Online-teacher precedent: the IWW TEFL Workers Union brought an Employment Tribunal claim for online teachers of The Overseas Teacher (DaDaABC "Free Talk"), seeking worker status, holiday pay, NMW and sick pay — [IWW press release](https://iww.org.uk/news/taking-the-overseas-teacher-to-employment-tribunal/). Trade press reported a result in the teachers' favour, but the outcome was not verified against a judgment — [EL Gazette](https://elgazette.com/?p=4893).
- Related tribunal decisions: an SQA exam invigilator was held to be a "worker" and awarded holiday pay (2024-25) — [Shanks v SQA](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6825fd14a4c1a40fde4e6445/Mrs_J_Shanks_v_Scottish_Qualifications_Authority_-_8001728.2024_-_Judgment.pdf). Goldsmiths conceded worker status to hourly lecturers after a claim was issued — [Leigh Day](https://www.leighday.co.uk/news/news/2021-news/university-lecturers-win-their-claim-for-worker-status-against-goldsmiths-university-of-london/).
- Market practice: MyTutor treats its schools tutors as workers, with holiday pay built into the hourly rate, and its private-marketplace tutors as self-employed with no holiday pay — [MyTutor help](https://help.mytutor.co.uk/en/articles/6359349-how-do-i-get-paid).
- Uber BV v Aslam [2021] UKSC 5 held that the written contract is not the starting point. Control over pay, contract terms, task allocation (including penalties for declining) and performance ratings pointed to worker status. Pimlico Plumbers v Smith [2018] UKSC 29 held that a restricted right of substitution is consistent with personal service — background, not verified this session.

### Inferences
- If teachers are workers, the rate card (€13-20/h, about £11-17) has to be checked against the NMW for every hour worked, including prep and admin time if those count, and 5.6 weeks' holiday pay must be added. At the bottom of the range this is tight. Inference.
- Paying for no-show classes and premiums doesn't create employment by itself. The features that look like Uber are: removing priority after late cancellation (a penalty for declining), algorithmic allocation on reliability scores, fixed curriculum and platform, and an 8-10 week group commitment. Inference.
- The UK EOR price is about $599 per employee per month at Deel and $599-699 at Remote (see the EOR section). On top come 15% NICs, 3% auto-enrolment pension and holiday pay. For a part-time teacher on €13-20/h, the fixed EOR fee alone can exceed the gross pay. Inference.

### Gaps
- No verified final judgment in the Overseas Teacher claim.
- The current text of HMRC's ESM guidance and the CEST tool was not reviewed this session.
- 2026-27 NMW rates were not retrieved.

## Ireland: Karshan test, Code of Practice, PRSI, incorporating in Ireland

### Takeaway
Since Revenue v Karshan (Domino's) [2023] IESC 24, Irish status turns on a five-question framework, now built into the revised Code of Practice (late 2024). A genuine contractor resident in Ireland self-assesses income tax and PRSI. A Canadian client with no Irish presence has no Irish duties in respect of a genuine contractor. If Alimun moves to Ireland it becomes an Irish employer for any employee: employer PRSI is 8.9% or 11.25% in 2026 (9.15% / 11.40% from 1 Oct 2026). Relevant Contracts Tax is not relevant to teaching.

### Cited Findings
- Karshan: in October 2023 the Supreme Court held that Domino's delivery drivers were employees, reversing the Court of Appeal and upholding Revenue's position — [Revenue press release](https://www.revenue.ie/ga/corporate/press-office/press-releases/2023/pr-102723-update-v-domino.aspx); [Matheson](https://www.matheson.com/insights/detail/to-be-an-employee-or-not-to-be-an-employee). The court set out a five-step framework applied to each worker's facts — [Chartered Accountants Ireland](https://www.charteredaccountants.ie/News/revenue-updates-guidance-following-judgment-on-employed-versus-self-employed-status).
- The commonly summarised five questions are: (1) does the contract involve an exchange of wage or other remuneration for work; (2) is the worker agreeing to provide their own services, not those of others; (3) does the employer exercise sufficient control over the work; (4) if so, do the terms and the factual matrix as a whole point to employment or self-employment; (5) does any legislative regime require the test to be adjusted. Mutuality of obligation is no longer a separate threshold requirement — based on the [Revenue Code of Practice PDF](https://www.revenue.ie/en/self-assessment-and-self-employment/documents/code-of-practice-on-employment-status.pdf) and [Lewis Silkin 2025](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/04/02/guidance-on-the-updated-code-of-practice-on-determining-employment-status). The wording of the questions was not re-read verbatim this session.
- The revised Code of Practice (Revenue, DSP and WRC) reflects the five-step test, and the WRC uses it. Sources differ on the publication date: Oct, Nov or Dec 2024 — [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/04/02/guidance-on-the-updated-code-of-practice-on-determining-employment-status); [Arthur Cox](https://www.arthurcox.com/insights/updated-code-of-practice-on-determining-employment-status-to-be-published-following-dominos-pizza-case/). The WRC told the Public Accounts Committee that a review of the code is "ongoing" — [Irish Examiner](https://www.irishexaminer.com/news/arid-41355703.html).
- Employer PRSI 2026: 11.25% standard and 9.00% reduced for weekly pay up to €552 (threshold raised from €527 on 1 Jan 2026). The rate applies to the whole week's pay, so the threshold works as a cliff edge. From 1 Oct 2026 the rates are 11.40% / 9.15%, and the employee rate 4.35% — [Sage IE](https://www.sage.com/en-ie/blog/payroll-changes-irish-employers/); [Saffery](https://www.saffery.com/ie/?p=1245). The sources are secondary and one outlier gives a €496 threshold — [Teamed](https://www.teamed.global/country-hiring-guides/ireland/tax-and-payroll).
- Irish minimum wage €14.15/h from 1 Jan 2026 — [Sage IE](https://www.sage.com/en-ie/blog/minimum-wage-ireland/).
- Foreign employers with staff working in Ireland: payroll obligation for Irish workdays, subject to the 30-workday and dispensation rules — [Chartered Accountants Ireland](https://www.charteredaccountants.ie/Accountancy-Ireland/Home/AI-Articles/tax-obligations-for-foreign-employers-with-workers-in-ireland).
- RCT applies to construction, forestry and meat processing, so not to teaching; VAT on private language tuition may fall under the education exemption — background, not verified this session.

### Inferences
- Under Karshan, the decisive Irish factor is control (question 3), followed by the overall picture. The Alimun features that weigh towards employment are: Alimun sets curriculum and materials, the platform allocates work algorithmically, reliability affects priority, and there is a multi-week commitment. Inference.
- An Irish teacher on €13-20/h at, say, 15 hours a week earns about €195-300 a week, well under €552. If employed, employer PRSI would be 9% (9.15% from Oct 2026). Pay at €13/h is below the Irish €14.15 minimum wage if the relationship is employment. Inference from cited rates.
- Incorporating in Ireland would make Alimun an Irish "employer" for any reclassified Irish teacher with no further registration step. For teachers abroad, Reg. 987/2009 art. 21 obligations would still follow the teacher's country of residence. Inference.

### Gaps
- Revenue's specific guidance on Irish residents paid by foreign clients as contractors (Form 11 self-assessment, Class S PRSI 4.1-4.2%) was not retrieved this session.
- The VAT treatment of the teacher's own services to a foreign B2B client was not researched.

## Sweden: F-skatt, client obligations, labour market

### Takeaway
Ask Swedish teachers to hold F-skatt (or FA-skatt) and keep a dated register extract on file. With that, no withholding or contributions are due from the payer. A teacher without F-skatt is risky: Swedish payers must withhold 30%, and a foreign employer of a reclassified employee owes 30% withholding plus arbetsgivaravgifter, which can be shifted to the employee through a socialavgiftsavtal. Sweden has no statutory minimum wage. Pay floors come from collective agreements, which do not bind a non-member.

### Cited Findings
- No withholding if the recipient has F-skatt; 30% withholding otherwise; a foreign company can apply for F-skatt — [Skatteverket, Ändrade skatteregler vid arbete i Sverige (2021 reform deck)](https://www.skatteverket.se/download/18.96cca41179bad4b1aa1930/1708607931130/%C3%84ndrade%20skatteregler%20vid%20arbete%20i%20Sverige_Del%202%20F%C3%B6rdjupning.pdf). Check the recipient's status with a signed and dated register extract — [Skatteverket](https://skatteverket.se/privat/internationellt/anlitautlandskforetagare.4.6efe6285127ab4f1d258000101.html?q=f-skatt).
- A foreign employer without a Swedish permanent establishment must withhold 30% and in most cases pay arbetsgivaravgifter, or use a socialavgiftsavtal — [Skatteverket (primary)](https://www.skatteverket.se/privat/internationellt/lonfranutlandskaarbetsgivare.4.906b37c10bd295ff4880001233.html).
- The European Commission has started infringement proceedings against Sweden over the F-skatt requirement for foreign contractors — [Forvis Mazars](https://www.forvismazars.com/se/sv/insikter/nyheter-artiklar/artiklar-fraan-forvis-mazars-nyhetsbrev/kommissionen-vaecker-talan-mot-sverige). Its current status was not verified.
- No statutory minimum wage. About 90% of workers are covered by collective agreements, none of which are generally applicable or extended — [Eurofound](https://www.eurofound.europa.eu/en/countries/sweden/minimum-wage).
- F-skatt is an administrative tax status, not a labour-law ruling. Swedish courts decide employment status on an overall assessment, so F-skatt does not prevent reclassification under labour law — background, not verified this session.

### Inferences
- Requiring F-skatt is the cheapest compliant route. Registration costs the teacher nothing. Inference.
- €13-20/h is low by Swedish standards for teachers. That matters less legally (no statutory floor) than commercially. Inference.

### Gaps
- No Swedish case on online teachers found. No source found on how Swedish online-tutoring companies (for example homework-help firms) engage tutors. Background: they commonly use hourly employment (timanställning) rather than contractors.
- The 2026 arbetsgivaravgifter rate was not verified.

## Denmark: CVR, B-income, reporting

### Takeaway
A Danish freelancer paid by a foreign client declares the income as B-income or business income through self-assessment. No source showed a duty on a foreign client with no Danish presence to report in eIndkomst or withhold. If a Danish teacher is reclassified as an employee of a foreign employer with no permanent establishment, there is no AM-bidrag or A-skat withholding by the employer according to Skattestyrelsen. BDO says ATP and workplace-injury insurance are still owed, about DKK 15k a year.

### Cited Findings
- See the Cross-cutting section: [skatteindberetningsloven](https://www.lovguiden.dk/loven/skatteindberetningsloven/4); [info.skat.dk](https://info.skat.dk/data.aspx?oid=79287); [BDO 2023](https://www.bdo.dk/da-dk/faglig-info/depechen/depechen-artikler-2023/fjernarbejde-over-landegraenser-hvad-med-skatten); [Nordic Council](https://pub.norden.org/nord2023-024/problemomrade-1-fast-driftssted-ved-hjemmearbejde.html).
- Late reporting of foreign income by the taxpayer carries a surcharge of DKK 100 per day, capped at DKK 2,500 a year — [BDO](https://www.bdo.dk/da-dk/faglig-info/depechen/depechen-artikler-2023/husk-at-indberette-udenlandsk-indkomst-og-formue-til-skat).

### Inferences
- Cheapest route: a contractor with a CVR number (free to register; VAT registration only above DKK 50,000 turnover). Background, not verified.
- Denmark has no statutory minimum wage and relies on collective agreements. Background, not verified.

### Gaps
- Whether a foreign client must report B-income.
- Whether a foreign employer with no permanent establishment owes ATP (sources conflict).
- No Danish online-teacher cases found.

## Finland: prepayment register, YEL

### Takeaway
Ask Finnish teachers to be in the ennakkoperintärekisteri (prepayment register). A payer then doesn't withhold on work compensation, and a foreign payer with no Finnish permanent establishment generally has no withholding duty anyway. Self-employed people arrange their own YEL pension insurance once their assessed earnings pass the statutory threshold.

### Cited Findings
- Prepayment register rules, voluntary registration, no withholding for registered payees, wages always withheld — [Vero](https://www.vero.fi/syventavat-vero-ohjeet/ohje-hakusivu/47881/ennakkoperintarekisteroinnin-vaikutukset-yritystoimintaan5/).
- A foreign employer with no Finnish permanent establishment is as a rule not obliged to withhold — [Vero, Ennakonpidätyksen toimittaminen](https://vero.fi/syventavat-vero-ohjeet/ohje-hakusivu/48735/ennakonpidatyksen-toimittaminen9/).
- YEL (self-employed pension) is mandatory above an earnings threshold (about €9,000-9,400 a year in 2025-26) — background, not verified this session.

### Inferences
- If a Finnish teacher were reclassified, the foreign employer would owe Finnish employer social insurance (TyEL pension and others) under Reg. 987/2009 art. 21, even without withholding tax. Inference.
- Finland has generally applicable collective agreements (yleissitovuus), so minimum pay could apply if the relationship is employment. Background, not verified.

### Gaps
- Current YEL threshold and contribution rates.
- Which collective agreement would cover private language teaching.
- No Finnish cases found.

## Norway: EEA, reporting

### Takeaway
Norway is the strictest on reporting. Foreign employers must file the a-melding for work performed in Norway "regardless of tax liability". A genuinely self-employed Norwegian teacher (ENK) invoicing a foreign client looks outside the employer reporting duties. But a person paid fees without being næringsdrivende is a reportable "fee recipient", so the teacher should hold an ENK registration.

### Cited Findings
- Foreign employer a-melding duties and employer NI contributions — [Skatteetaten (primary)](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/business-and-organisation/employer/the-a-melding/the-a-melding-guide/special-groups/foreign-employers-reporting-obligation-in-the-a-melding).
- The "freelancers, contractors and fee recipients" category covers people paid outside employment without being self-employed — [Skatteetaten](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/business-and-organisation/employer/the-a-melding/the-a-melding-guide/employment/type-of-employment/freelancers-contractors-and-fee-recipients/).
- Assignments carried out in Norway by a foreign business must be registered in the assignment and employee register (RF-1199). This is about foreign businesses working in Norway, not about a foreign client paying a Norwegian freelancer — [same Skatteetaten page](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/business-and-organisation/employer/the-a-melding/the-a-melding-guide/special-groups/foreign-employers-reporting-obligation-in-the-a-melding).

### Inferences
- If an occasional Norwegian teacher has no ENK, Alimun may be a payer of reportable fees. Requiring an ENK/org.nr reduces this. Unclear. Inference.
- Norway is EEA, not EU. Reg. 883/2004 applies through the EEA Agreement, but the EU Platform Work Directive does not apply unless incorporated into the EEA. Background, not verified.

### Gaps
- Whether a foreign client with no Norwegian presence must report fees paid to a Norwegian non-self-employed person.
- Norwegian employer NI rate for 2026 (14.1% zone 1, background).
- No Norwegian online-teacher cases found.

## Netherlands: Wet DBA, Deliveroo, VBAR, platform work

### Takeaway
This is the highest-risk country for Alimun's model. The Belastingdienst resumed enforcement of Wet DBA on 1 Jan 2025 (soft first year, fines possible from 2026). Deliveroo (HR 24 Mar 2023) holds that a right of substitution and freedom to refuse work don't rule out employment, and that embeddedness in the business counts. The VBAR act, adopted by the Senate on 16 June 2026 and expected in force by 31 Dec 2026, creates a labour-law presumption of employment below about €38/h (excl. VAT). Alimun's €13-20/h is far below that. The Platform Work Directive transposition (deadline 2 Dec 2026, Dutch bill delayed) adds a second presumption.

### Cited Findings
- Wet DBA enforcement restarted on 1 Jan 2025. Additional wage-tax assessments for false self-employment reach back no earlier than 1 Jan 2025, and no fines were imposed in 2025 — [ZZP Nederland](https://www.zzp-nederland.nl/nieuws/2025-geen-boetes-bij-handhaving-schijnzelfstandigheid-wet-dba); [MKB Servicedesk](https://www.mkbservicedesk.nl/nieuws/ondernemersnieuws/handhaving-zzp-wet-voor-veel-ondernemers-nog-erg-onduidelijk). 2025 was described as a transition year with fines possible from 2026 — [Freelance.nl, 2026](https://www.freelance.nl/community/wet-dba-2025).
- Deliveroo, HR 24 March 2023 (ECLI:NL:HR:2023:443): all circumstances decide; freedom to be substituted does not exclude an employment contract; "inbedding" (embeddedness in the organisation) is a relevant factor — [NJB](https://www.njb.nl/nieuws/hoge-raad-bezorgers-deliveroo-zijn-geen-zelfstandigen/); [Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.nl/insights/themas/legal/hoge-raad-in-deliveroo-arrest-bezorgers-hebben-een-arbeidsovereenkomst/). Belastingdienst assessment framework — [Afwegingskader dienstbetrekking](https://download.belastingdienst.nl/belastingdienst/docs/afwegingskader_dienstbetrekking_lh6301z1pl.pdf).
- VBAR: labour-law presumption of an employment contract for work below an hourly rate of €38 (earlier drafts said €36), indexed to the minimum wage. The Senate adopted the bill on 16 June 2026; entry into force is expected by 31 Dec 2026 with no transitional rules. The presumption applies in labour law, not directly in tax or social security, and is rebuttable — [SRA, June 2026](https://www.sra.nl/nieuws/259001/2026/06/rechtsvermoeden-arbeidsovereenkomst-bij-uurtarief-tot-38); [Salaris van Morgen, Apr 2026](https://www.salarisvanmorgen.nl/2026/04/16/rechtsvermoeden-arbeidsovereenkomst-op-basis-van-uurtarief-in-debat-over-vbar/); [PwC](https://www.pwc.nl/nl/actueel-en-publicaties/belastingnieuws/loonbelasting-en-sociale-verzekeringen/wetvbarnaartweedekamer.html). Exact dates and the threshold should be checked in the Staatsblad.
- Platform Work Act: draft bill published 29 June 2026, consultation closed 24 Aug 2026. The Directive covers any platform organising work in the EU regardless of where it is established, and creates a rebuttable presumption where facts indicate direction and control, with the burden of proof on the platform — [Global Workplace Insider, Sept 2026](https://www.globalworkplaceinsider.com/2026/09/the-platform-work-act-implementing-the-eu-platform-work-directive-in-the-netherlands/); [Houthoff](https://www.houthoff.com/actueel/nieuws/wetsvoorstel-voor-digitaal-arbeidsplatformwerk-ter-internetconsultatie-het-voorgestelde-kader/). The Dutch government says the 2 Dec 2026 deadline won't be met — [JD Supra tracker](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/eu-platform-work-directive-which-4838342/).
- Model contracts (modelovereenkomsten) were the Wet DBA safe harbour. Under strict enforcement they give limited comfort if practice diverges from the contract — [MKB Servicedesk, Wat is de wet DBA](https://www.mkbservicedesk.nl/starten-zzp/dba-var/wat-is-de-wet-dba).

### Inferences
- A Dutch teacher habitually working from home in NL would, under Rome I art. 8, keep the protection of mandatory Dutch employment law even under a Canadian-law contract. The VBAR presumption would then arguably apply to a claim against Alimun. Inference, not verified.
- On tax and social security, a foreign client with no Dutch permanent establishment does not withhold wage tax. If reclassified, it would owe Dutch employee-insurance premiums as a foreign employer without a PE. Background, not verified.
- At €13-20/h, a Dutch teacher is presumed to be an employee from VBAR's entry into force. Lawful options are a higher Dutch rate (≥€38/h, which is costly), employment through an EOR, or not engaging NL-resident teachers. Inference.

The Directive's definition (services provided at the request of a recipient, at least partly by electronic means, involving organisation of work by individuals) likely does — background, not verified.

## Employment alternative: EOR pricing

### Takeaway
List prices for Employer of Record services are about $599 per employee per month at Deel. Remote is $599 billed annually or $699 month-to-month. Negotiated rates can drop to roughly $400-500 at 20+ heads. That is on top of salary and employer contributions, which makes EOR uneconomic for part-time teachers earning €13-20/h.

### Cited Findings
- Deel EOR $599 per employee per month; contractor management about $49 per contractor per month — [erpresearch](https://erpresearch.com/erp-add-ons/payroll/deel/pricing); [Pin.com](https://www.pin.com/blog/deel-pricing/).
- Remote $699 month-to-month, $599 billed annually — [Costbench](https://costbench.com/compare/deel-vs-remote). Reported negotiated rates $400-500 at 20+ headcount (unverified third-party claim) — [Costbench](https://costbench.com/compare/deel-vs-remote).

### Inferences
- Example: a teacher doing 40 hours a month at €17 earns €680 gross. A $599 EOR fee (about €550) nearly doubles the cost before employer contributions. EOR only makes sense for teachers on high hours, or for the Netherlands if Alimun wants NL teachers at all. Inference.

### Gaps

## Platform features that raise reclassification risk, and lawful design alternatives

### Takeaway
All the tests discussed focus on control, integration and the reality of the relationship: Uber (UK), Karshan question 3 (IE), Deliveroo embeddedness and VBAR (NL), and the Platform Work Directive's direction-and-control presumption (EU). The riskiest Alimun features are reliability-based priority, loss of priority after cancelling, algorithmic offering of work, the platform setting the pay, mandatory curriculum and materials, and multi-week group commitments.

### Cited Findings
- Deliveroo: substitution and freedom to refuse do not override embeddedness and the overall picture — [NJB](https://www.njb.nl/nieuws/hoge-raad-bezorgers-deliveroo-zijn-geen-zelfstandigen/).
- The PWD presumption is triggered by facts indicating direction and control, with the burden on the platform; reality, including algorithmic allocation, monitoring and control, prevails over labels — [Global Workplace Insider](https://www.globalworkplaceinsider.com/2026/09/the-platform-work-act-implementing-the-eu-platform-work-directive-in-the-netherlands/); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/hun/legal-updates/from-gig-to-guarantee-how-the-eu-is-transforming-platform-work).
- Karshan question 3 (sufficient control) — [Revenue Code of Practice](https://www.revenue.ie/en/self-assessment-and-self-employment/documents/code-of-practice-on-employment-status.pdf).

### Inferences (design options, not legal conclusions)
- Priority penalties for late cancellation work like Uber's sanctions for declining. Alternatives: a contractual cancellation fee or forfeiture of that class's fee (a commercial remedy, not a status penalty), or plain first-come-first-served with no reliability ranking.
- Rates set unilaterally by the platform signal control. Alternative: let teachers set or bid a rate within a band, or offer rate tiers they choose.
- Mandatory curriculum and materials point towards control and integration. Alternative: specify outcomes (CEFR level and syllabus coverage) and give the teacher freedom of method. Offer materials as optional resources.
- Algorithmic monitoring and ratings point towards control. Alternative: use quality data only for the learner's choice, not for discipline.
- Exclusivity and non-competes: avoid them. Allow teachers to teach elsewhere and to build their own brand.
- Genuine substitution: let a teacher send a qualified substitute they pay themselves, with Alimun's approval limited to qualifications (Pimlico shows that a restricted right weakens the argument).
- Business indicators to collect: a local business registration (F-skatt, CVR, ENK, KvK, prepayment register, UK self-assessment), the teacher's own equipment, invoices issued by the teacher, multiple clients.
- Per country: the Netherlands at under €38/h is presumed employment under VBAR whatever the design. Design alone cannot solve the Netherlands at this rate card. Inference.

### Gaps
- No court decisions on online language-school platforms specifically, beyond the UK IWW claim.
- No Nordic or Irish rulings on online teachers found.
