# Competitor pricing, tiers and teacher pay for live online language classes (2025-2026)

Research date: 26 Oct 2026. Exchange rate used throughout: 1 EUR = 1.1537 USD (ECB reference rate of 16 Sep 2026, republished by Banca d'Italia; the latest one found), so USD 1 = EUR 0.867 — [Banca d'Italia](https://www.bancaditalia.it/compiti/operazioni-cambi/cambio/cambi_rif_20260916). BRL, MAD, EGP and INR rates were not sourced, so those prices are left in their own currency. "Per hour" figures are my arithmetic from the cited per-lesson price and lesson length, and they are marked (calc). Most competitor prices come from third-party review blogs, because many companies hide prices or show them only to logged-in or geolocated users. These are flagged.

## Q1. How each competitor prices live classes (model, length, group size, price per class and per hour, regional prices, discounts, trials, cancellation)

### Takeaway
Group live classes cost the learner about EUR 2.60-10 per class: Cambly Groups "from $3", EF English Live about $3-4.60 by third-party estimate, Lingoda EUR 5.99-9.49 for 60 minutes. Private 1:1 lessons cost about EUR 15-35 per hour on subscription schools (Lingoda, Cambly, Novakid) and about EUR 16 per hour on average on marketplaces (Preply). The cheapest per hour are Cambly Groups, EF English Live's bundled group classes and Engoo's 25-minute 1:1 lessons on large annual plans (about EUR 9 per hour). Nearly everyone uses a subscription with discounts for volume or longer terms (20-50%), plus a cheap or free trial.

### Inferences
- The live group market clusters at EUR 6-10 per learner-hour (Lingoda) with a low-price tail of about EUR 2.60-4 per class (Cambly Groups "from $3", EF English Live bundles, Lingoda USD promos). An Alimun price of about EUR 3 per lesson would sit at the floor of the market rather than below it. Whether it is cheaper per hour depends on lesson length: EUR 3 for 60 minutes would be clearly the cheapest group hour found, while EUR 3 for 30 minutes (EUR 6/h) is in line with Lingoda's 40-class plan (EUR 5.99/h).
- Volume and commitment discounts are universal and steep: Lingoda's group per-class price falls 37% from 5 to 40 classes, Cambly annual is 30-54% below monthly, Novakid -25%, Engoo up to 50%. The "headline" low price is almost always the largest or annual plan.
- Regional pricing is real and large where it is visible: Cambly Brazil groups were R$37-46 a month, and Goethe charges about 9x more per hour in the US than in India. EF and Open English hide prices behind sales calls, which allows per-country pricing.
- The common tier axis is teacher/format (group, private, premium/native teacher), multiplied by volume (lessons per week) and term (monthly, quarterly, annual). Examples: Cambly Groups / Private+ / Pro and Novakid Standard / Premium.

### Gaps
- Verbling, Speexx, Learnship, Dante Alighieri, Talkpal and Tandem paid tutors: no pricing found within the search budget.
- Cambly Groups: lesson length, group size and monthly plan price were not shown in the results. The $3 is a "from" price.
- Prices in MAD, EGP and INR (other than the Goethe India example) were not found. Lingoda's page offered EUR, GBP and USD only.
- Most prices are third-party snapshots. Lingoda (fetched 26 Oct 2026), the Cambly Kids/Groups pages and the Engoo plans page are the only official-page figures.

## Q2. Teacher pay

### Takeaway
Platform-employed teachers on the large schools are paid about EUR 8.80-12 per teaching hour (Cambly about $10.20/h, Lingoda about EUR 11-12 per 60-minute class, Engoo about $5 per 25 minutes for native speakers). Marketplaces let tutors set their own rates but take 15-33% commission (Preply's falls from 33% to 18% with hours taught, and Preply keeps 100% of trial lessons). "Above market" for Alimun therefore means more than about EUR 12-15 per hour.

### Cited Findings
- **Cambly:** paid per minute, about $10.20/h for adults (calc: EUR 8.84) and about $12/h for Cambly Kids (EUR 10.40), paid weekly via PayPal — [teast.co](https://teast.co/teach-english-online); [TEFL Hero](https://teflhero.com/blog/cambly-review/)
- **Lingoda:** EUR 11 ($12.50) per hour for English classes, independent contractors on zero-hours terms. One teacher earned about EUR 1,500-2,000/month at peak, but "lesson volume has reduced significantly" — [The TEFL Academy](https://www.theteflacademy.com/blog/teaching-on-lingoda-what-you-need-to-know/). An Indeed reviewer with 20 years' experience cites "12EUR/lesson" and calls it low — [Indeed Lingoda reviews](https://www.indeed.com/cmp/Lingoda-Gmbh/reviews?fcountry=ALL&ftopic=paybenefits). Other ranges: $8-13/h — [tefl.org Lingoda](https://www.tefl.org/teach-english-online/lingoda/). Glassdoor shows about $25/h, but from only 2 reports, which is weak — [Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Hourly-Pay/Lingoda-Teacher-Hourly-Pay-E946674_D_KO8,15.htm)
- **Engoo:** GBP 2-7.20 ($2.80-10) per hour range. A teacher reports $5 per 25 minutes or $10 per 50 minutes for native speakers, less for non-native — [tefl.org list](https://www.tefl.org/teach-english-online/); [onlineteacherdude](https://onlineteacherdude.com/non-chinese-esl-companies/)
- **Novakid:** the figures conflict. One source says $8 base per 25-minute class ($16 per 50 minutes); another says about GBP 20/$25 per hour. The most recent source says Novakid publishes no rate and the circulating figures are unverified teacher reports — [TEFL Institute ZA](https://teflinstitute.com/za/blog/novakid-application/); [The TEFL Academy Novakid](https://www.theteflacademy.com/blog/teaching-on-novakid-what-you-need-to-know/)
- **Preply:** tutors set their own rate. Commission is 33% on the first 20 hours, falling in stages to 18% after 400 hours (breakpoints differ by source). Preply takes 100% of every trial lesson with a new student — [TutorLingua](https://tutorlingua.co/blog/preply-commission-explained-2026); [supatutor](https://supatutor.in/how-does-preply-pay-teachers/)
- **italki:** sources disagree. Some say 21% on single lessons (previously 15%), falling to 19/17/15% on 5/10/15-lesson packages, with 30% on group classes; others say a flat 15% — [supatutor](https://supatutor.in/preply-vs-italki/); [Teacher and Me](https://teacher-and-me.com/blog/preply-italki-commission)

### Inferences
- **Teacher cost share at Lingoda (calc, estimate):** a group class earns EUR 5.99-9.49 per learner. With 3-5 learners, that is about EUR 18-47 of revenue per class-hour. A teacher paid about EUR 11-12 takes roughly 25-65% of class revenue. Lingoda's 3-5 group size is what makes the low per-learner price workable.
- **Teacher pay at about EUR 3 per lesson (calc, estimate):** if Alimun pays a teacher above market (say EUR 15 per hour), a 60-minute group needs at least 5 paying learners at EUR 3 just to cover teacher pay, before payment fees, platform costs and no-shows. A 30-minute lesson at EUR 3 needs 2.5 learners. This is arithmetic, not a sourced benchmark.

### Gaps
- No current public data on Berlitz, EF or Open English teacher pay was found within the budget.
- No Lingoda pay data for teachers of languages other than English was found.

## Q3. Take rates, gross margins, CAC and churn

### Takeaway
Hard financials are scarce for private players. Preply raised $150M at a $1.2B valuation and is EBITDA-positive. Duolingo's gross margin is about 72% (app-led, not live classes). Education app benchmarks show heavy churn: monthly plans keep a median of about 14-26% of subscribers at 6 months and 6-14% at 1 year. No reliable CAC benchmark was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Preply:** a $150M Series D led by WestCap (announced Jan 2026), $1.2B valuation and $299M total raised. EBITDA-positive over the prior 12 months — [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/01/21/preply-raises-150m-enhance-human-led-language-learning-ai/); [Preply blog](https://preply.com/en/blog/preply-raises-150-million-to-shape-the-future-of-education-through-human-led-ai-enhanced-learning/). The latest revenue figure found is $50M for 2021 (old) — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/preply/). The take rate is effectively the 18-33% commission plus 100% of trials (see Q2).
- **Duolingo (comparison, app-led):** FY2025 revenue $1.037B (+39%), of which $873.4M from subscriptions. Gross margin was 72.2% in FY2025, down from 72.8%, attributed partly to AI costs such as Video Call — [Duolingo 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000162828026012494/duol-20251231.htm). Q3 2025 gross margin was 72.5% — [Duolingo Q3 2025 letter](https://investors.duolingo.com/static-files/c9bf5861-b19d-4396-b060-c0dbeefd34f5)
- **Lingoda:** raised $68M in 2021 — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/04/15/lingoda-an-on-demand-online-language-school-with-live-instructors-and-zoom-classrooms-raises-67m) (old; no recent financials found).
- **Education app retention (RevenueCat 2026):** 6-month retention median is 14-26% for monthly plans (top quartile 30-50%). Year-1 retention median is 6-14% for monthly (top quartile 11-25%) and 20-40% for yearly (top quartile 32-59%) — [RevenueCat Education 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-education). Education annual renewal is about 24% — [RevenueCat renewal rates](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/average-subscription-renewal-rates-by-app-category)
- Across categories, about 72% of annual subscriptions are cancelled in year 1, and 35% of annual cancellations happen in month 1. Billing failures cause 31% of cancellations on Google Play — [RocketShip HQ summary of RevenueCat 2025](https://www.rocketshiphq.com/revenuecat-state-of-subscription-apps-2025-summary/)
- Language apps: 10.5% monthly churn. The top cancellation driver is loss of motivation (40%). Methodology unclear, directional only — [RetentionCheck](https://retentioncheck.com/churn-benchmarks/language-learning-apps)

### Inferences
- Live-class businesses carry teacher cost as cost of goods sold, so their gross margins should sit well below Duolingo's 72%. A marketplace's take rate of 18-33% is effectively its gross revenue. This is an inference; no live-class gross margin was found.
- High early churn on monthly plans argues for commitment discounts (quarterly or annual) and for fixed weekly class times, as Cambly's "lessons per week" plans and Lingoda's Sprint do.

### Gaps
- No figures were found for Babbel 2024/2025 revenue, Berlitz financials, Busuu/Chegg segment data, or CAC for language subscriptions.

## Q4. Very cheap entry pricing (EUR 1-5 per lesson): who does it, and does it work?

### Takeaway
Several players advertise lessons in the EUR 2.60-5 range, but always on a group format, a 25-30-minute lesson, a large annual or high-volume plan, or as a promotion. These include Cambly Groups "from $3", Engoo $4.34 per 25 minutes on 40 lessons a month annually, Lingoda USD promos at about $4.30-4.50 on 40 classes, and EF English Live group classes bundled at about $3-4.60. Cheap trials ($1 Cambly trial, Lingoda 7-day free trial) are the norm. No public data was found on conversion, churn or perceived quality at these price points.

### Cited Findings
- Cambly Groups "starting at only $3 per lesson" — [Cambly Groups](https://www.cambly.com/groups)
- Engoo $4.34 per 25-minute lesson on 40/month annual, with "up to 50% off" — [Engoo plans](https://engoo.com/plans)
- Lingoda: "the promo is the norm, not the exception", and regular pricing applies from the third month. This is a low-entry-then-step-up pattern — [Spanish Fluency Club](https://www.spanish-fluency-club.com/blog/lingoda-spanish-review)
- Lingoda teachers report that lesson volume has fallen, which limits earnings even at a fixed rate — [The TEFL Academy](https://www.theteflacademy.com/blog/teaching-on-lingoda-what-you-need-to-know/)
- Goethe India: about EUR 2.38 per learner-hour (calc) in groups of 10+. This shows that very low per-hour prices exist in low-PPP markets through large groups — [Deutschable](https://deutschable.com/is-the-goethe-institut-worth-it/)
- Cambly $1 trial lesson (unconfirmed) — [AmazingTalker blog](https://en.amazingtalker.com/blog/en/other/90286/)

### Inferences
- Competitors reach a EUR 3-level price in one of three ways: (a) group size (cost spread across learners), (b) short lessons (25-30 minutes), or (c) promotions, annual prepayment or high volume. Alimun's EUR 3 lesson would need at least one of these to be sustainable while paying teachers above about EUR 12/h (see the Q2 arithmetic).
- A promo-then-regular pattern (Lingoda) suggests a low entry price is used to acquire customers, not as the steady-state price.

### Gaps
- No studies or company disclosures were found on conversion, churn or quality perception for EUR 1-5 lessons. Talkpal and other low-cost regional players were not covered.

## Q5. Tier counts and structures; what customers pick

### Takeaway
Live-class competitors typically show 3-4 volume tiers (Lingoda 5/12/20/40 classes, with the 12-class plan marked "POPULAR") or 3 format tiers (Cambly Groups / Private+ / Pro), multiplied by 3 commitment terms (monthly, quarterly, annual). Simon-Kucher's work supports the idea that 3 options steer buyers to the middle. No primary data on the share choosing each tier was found.

### Cited Findings
- Lingoda: 4 volume tiers per format (5/12/20/40). The 12-class plan carries the "POPULAR" label in both group and private — [Lingoda pricing](https://www.lingoda.com/en/pricing)
- Cambly: 3 adult tiers (Small Groups, Private+, Pro), each with monthly, quarterly and annual terms — [inglesreview.com.br](https://inglesreview.com.br/cambly-vale-a-pena/); [Cambly English](https://www.cambly.com/english). Kids: 4 frequency tiers (2/3/5/7 lessons a week) times 3 terms — [Cambly Kids](https://www.cambly.com/kids/plans)
- Novakid: 2 quality tiers (Standard, Premium), each with 1, 3, 6 and 12-month terms — [worthebuck](https://worthebuck.com/blog/novakid-review/)
- Simon-Kucher: good-better-best relies on the decoy effect, and buyers gravitate to the middle option — [Simon-Kucher GBB](https://www.simon-kucher.com/en/insights/rise-and-fall-good-better-best-packaging). With 2 options consumers skew cheap; adding a premium option shifts them to the middle — [Simon-Kucher 2014 newsletter](https://www.simon-kucher.com/sites/default/files/consumer_insights_newsletter_fall_winter_2014_simon-kucher.pdf)

### Inferences
- A common pattern is 3 visible tiers with a highlighted middle tier, plus a term discount (monthly vs annual), plus optionally a separate private add-on. This matches what competitors show. Four volume tiers (Lingoda) also work when the axis is classes per month.
