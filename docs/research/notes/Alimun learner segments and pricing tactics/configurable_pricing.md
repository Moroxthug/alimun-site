# Configurable ("build your own plan") and modular pricing for live classes

Research date: 26 Oct 2026. About 24 tool calls. Competitor prices change often and differ by country, currency and promotion; every figure below is a snapshot and should be re-checked before use. Where the search snippet and the live page disagreed, both are noted.

## Who uses configurators and modular pricing, and how they show the "from" price and the result

### Takeaway
The online language competitors closest to Alimun (Lingoda, Cambly, Preply) use short configurators with two or three dimensions: class type (group or private), volume (classes or lessons per week or per cycle) and commitment length. They show the result as a **price per class or lesson** next to a plan total, and they steer with a single "POPULAR" or "BEST VALUE" label. None of the pages fetched used a free-text "from €X" headline on the pricing page itself. Meal kits (HelloFresh) use the same two-axis grid (people × meals) with the per-serving price falling as the box grows.

### Cited Findings
- **Lingoda (pricing page fetched Oct 2026):** the learner picks the language (English, Business English, French, German, Italian, Spanish), then Group or Private. Group is the default view and is described as small groups of 3-5 students. Each type has four tiers (5, 12, 20, 40 classes) showing a per-class price, a plan total with a struck-through original price and a "SAVE 20%" badge. The 12-class tier is marked "POPULAR". Prices are in EUR by default (GBP and USD available). Plans renew every 4 weeks, not monthly. — [Lingoda pricing](https://www.lingoda.com/en/pricing/)
- Lingoda charges more for some languages: one third-party source says the 5-class group package is $79.99 for English, French and Spanish, with Italian and German higher. Third-party figures conflict and some are dated. — [BaseLang vs Lingoda](https://baselang.com/compare/baselang-vs-lingoda/); [Langoly review](https://langoly.com/lingoda-review/)
- **Cambly (subscribe page fetched Oct 2026):** this is an explicit "Build your own plan" page. The learner picks the plan type (Small groups, Private+, Pro), then lessons per week (1, 2, 3, 5 or 10, each 30 minutes), then plan length (monthly, 3 months, yearly). The price is shown **per lesson**: $18.75 monthly, $12.12 for 3 months ("30% off") and $8.65 yearly ("50% off"). The only steering labels are the discount percentages. — [Cambly subscribe](https://www.cambly.com/en/subscribe)
  - Conflict: a search snippet of the same URL showed a different version, with "30 minutes to 5 hours per week", "up to 1 hour per day" and monthly prices of $109, $98 and $82. Older third-party reviews describe a minutes-per-day × days-per-week grid (for example 30 min/day at $76, $109 or $146 for 2, 3 or 5 days). This suggests Cambly has changed its plan structure over time or varies it by market. — [Cambly subscribe](https://www.cambly.com/en/subscribe); [EduReviewer](https://edureviewer.com/blog/cambly-cost/)
- **Cambly small groups:** 1, 2 ("BEST VALUE") or 4 lessons per week, priced per lesson at $5.50 monthly, $3.55 for 3 months and $2.54 yearly. Group size and how groups are formed are not stated. — [Cambly groups](https://www.cambly.com/en/subscribe/groups). (A search snippet showed $5.25, $4.52 and $3.39, which again conflicts.)
- **Preply:** a per-tutor subscription built from lessons per 28-day cycle. The learner can change the number of lessons or the lesson length at any time, can buy top-up lessons inside a cycle, and can pause once per cycle for up to 20 days. — [Preply help: how the subscription works](https://help.preply.com/en/articles/4966680-how-does-my-preply-subscription-work); [Preply help: extra lessons](https://help.preply.com/en/articles/15432937-how-to-add-extra-lessons-to-your-subscription)
- **HelloFresh (US):** 15 weekly plan combinations (2, 4 or 6 people × 2 to 6 meals). For example, the 2-person plans run from about $45.96 for 2 meals to $119.88 for 6, plus $10.99 shipping per week. The plan page shows the per-serving cost before the customer commits, and the per-serving price falls as the number of meals rises (for example $12.49 per serving at the minimum, $10.99 above it, in an older snapshot). — [NBC News Select](https://nbcnews.com/select/shopping/hello-fresh-review-rcna595893); [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/finance/how-much-does-hello-fresh-cost)
- HelloFresh Australia publishes a per-serving grid (for example $14.99 for 2 people × 2 meals and $8.99 for 4 people × 6 meals) and states that "the larger the box, the cheaper the price per serving". — [HelloFresh AU meal plans](https://www.hellofresh.com.au/meal-kits/meal-plans?q=vffb5co)
- **ClassPass:** a monthly credit plan in which each class costs a variable number of credits. Credit cost depends on demand, not only clock time: popular instructors, full classes and waitlists cost more, and off-peak slots usually cost less unless demand spikes. — [ClassPass blog: Myth Busters](https://classpass.com/blog/classpass-myth-busters-credits-and-pricing/)

### Inferences
- The market pattern (Lingoda, Cambly, HelloFresh) is **2-3 dimensions plus commitment**, with the result shown as a unit price (per class or per serving) and a total. Alimun's proposed five or six dimensions (focus, group size, frequency, schedule, teacher type, and possibly commitment) is more than any comparable competitor found shows at once.
- Alimun's language-dependent teacher cost (€13-€20 per hour) has a precedent: Lingoda already prices Italian and German higher than English, French and Spanish.

### Gaps
- Berlitz, Verbling, Peloton, Kumon, music-school and mobile-operator configurators were not researched within the tool budget.
- No conversion data (completion rate or conversion uplift) for any competitor configurator was found. Companies do not publish it.

## Pricing dimensions: group size, off-peak, intensity, specialisation, seniority, commitment

### Takeaway
Group size and commitment length are the levers most often used and documented. Semi-private (2-3 learners) is typically 20-50% cheaper per learner than private, and commitment discounts in this market are steep (30-50% at Cambly). Off-peak pricing is common in gyms but carries a risk of training customers to wait for deals. Little public evidence was found on specialisation premiums or teacher-seniority tiers in online language schools, apart from Lingoda's separate "Business English" language and Cambly's "Pro" plan type.

### Cited Findings
- **Semi-private discount per student versus private (mixed providers and currencies, illustrative only):**
  - Penobscot School (languages): private $46.50/h; semi-private (2-3 students) $36.50/h per person, about 21% off. — [Penobscot](https://www.penobscot.us/?p=416)
  - Italian Cultural Institute Sydney: 1 student $90/h; 2 students $140 ($70 each); 3 students $165 ($55 each), about 22% and 39% off. — [IIC Sydney](https://iicsydney.esteri.it/en/?p=395)
  - Nichibei Japanese school: ¥7,000 one-to-one; ¥4,000 each for 2 students; ¥3,500 each for 3 students. — [Nichibei](https://www.nichibei.ac.jp/jli/program/private.php)
  - Vancouver Academy of Music: private from $86/h; semi-private for 2 from $48/h per student. — [VAM](https://vancouveracademyofmusic.com/private-semi-private-small-group-classes/)
  - A translators' forum describes a pair rate of about 1.5× the individual rate (about 25% off per student). This is anecdotal. — [ProZ forum](https://guj.proz.com/forum/teaching_and_learning_languages/31750-rates_for_private_lesson_vs_semi_private.html)
- **Group size at scale:** Lingoda's group classes hold 3-5 students; Cambly small-group lessons cost about 30% of its private per-lesson price on the monthly plan ($5.50 versus $18.75). — [Lingoda pricing](https://www.lingoda.com/en/pricing/); [Cambly groups](https://www.cambly.com/en/subscribe/groups); [Cambly subscribe](https://www.cambly.com/en/subscribe)
- **Commitment length:** Cambly shows 30% off for 3 months and 50% off for a year on both private and group plans. Lingoda shows a "SAVE 20%" badge, with rolling 4-week plans that can be cancelled or paused. — [Cambly subscribe](https://www.cambly.com/en/subscribe); [Lingoda pricing](https://www.lingoda.com/en/pricing/)
- **Intensity (volume):** HelloFresh lowers the per-serving price as meals per week rise. Lingoda's per-class price falls across its 5, 12, 20 and 40 tiers. — [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/finance/how-much-does-hello-fresh-cost); [Lingoda pricing](https://www.lingoda.com/en/pricing/)
- **Off-peak:**
  - Gyms sell off-peak tiers that limit access to quiet windows (example: $30-35/month versus $50 for standard access). This is vendor content. — [Rework: peak hour management](https://resources.rework.com/vi/libraries/gym-fitness-growth/peak-hour-management)
  - A studio-software vendor argues that constant midday discounting "teaches members to devalue your service and wait for a deal". It suggests a small premium on the 6-7pm slots that fill first instead (a hypothetical example). — [Vibefam](https://vibefam.com/how-fitness-studios-can-monetize-off-peak-hours-without-discounting/)
  - ClassPass prices by demand rather than strictly by time slot. — [ClassPass blog](https://classpass.com/blog/classpass-myth-busters-credits-and-pricing/)
  - Health Club Management describes yield management as a way to fill off-peak capacity whose fixed costs continue. The article is from 2016 and may be dated. — [Health Club Management](https://www.healthclubmanagement.co.uk/health-club-management-features/Yield-management-Revenue-booster/30725)
- **Specialisation:** Lingoda lists "Business English" as its own language option. — [Lingoda pricing](https://www.lingoda.com/en/pricing/)
- **Teacher type:** Cambly separates "Private+" and "Pro" plan types. — [Cambly subscribe](https://www.cambly.com/en/subscribe)

### Inferences
These are my own calculations and not sourced figures, so treat them as estimates.
- **Break-even per learner** for a 60-minute class at the teacher's hourly rate, before Stripe fees, platform costs and VAT:

  | Language (teacher €/h) | 1 learner | 2 learners | 3 learners | 4 learners | 6 learners | 8 learners |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | English/Spanish (€13) | 13.00 | 6.50 | 4.33 | 3.25 | 2.17 | 1.63 |
  | German (€20) | 20.00 | 10.00 | 6.67 | 5.00 | 3.33 | 2.50 |

  A sustainable price needs a margin on top for overhead, payment fees and VAT. For example, if VAT is 20% and overhead is 30-40% of revenue, the price is roughly 2× the teacher cost per learner.
- **Pricing on the minimum seats:** because empty seats are the risk, the safest model prices each group-size band on its **minimum** seat count (for example price the "4-6" band as if 4 attend). Learners in fuller classes then generate the margin.
- **Translating the semi-private discounts:** applying the observed 20-50% semi-private discounts to Alimun, a 2-3 learner class could plausibly be priced at 55-75% of the private per-learner price. That still covers the teacher, because 2 × 55% = 110% of the private price.
- **Off-peak:** for a live online school with teachers paid hourly, off-peak demand is mostly a matching problem (finding enough learners for one slot) rather than an idle-capacity problem. The gym logic (empty studios cost money anyway) applies only partly. Off-peak is more useful as a way to fill groups than as a discount lever.

### Gaps
- No published data was found on exam-prep or medical/professional premiums at online language schools (for example Goethe or telc exam courses, medical German), or on how teacher seniority tiers are priced. This needs a dedicated competitor scan.
- No elasticity data was found for learners at different group sizes.

## Credit/token models versus fixed plans, breakage, expiring credits and rollover

### Takeaway
Credits are the industry norm (Lingoda class credits, Preply lessons per cycle, ClassPass credits, Cambly weekly minutes). The competitors earn breakage by letting unused credits expire at renewal or at cancellation, but this produces visible customer complaints and legal risk in some EU countries. Germany's highest civil court treats the forfeiture of prepaid balances in standard terms as invalid, at least for telecom prepaid credit. A softer model (limited rollover, credits usable while subscribed, clear reminders) is the defensible middle ground.

### Cited Findings
- **Lingoda:** unused class credits roll over. When the subscription ends they remain visible but cannot be used after the last payment cycle, and renewing restores them. Pausing is allowed for up to 4 weeks once per cycle. On monthly plans a class can be cancelled 3 days ahead for a credit refund. — [Lingoda pricing](https://www.lingoda.com/en/pricing/)
- A Lingoda review says a class cancelled less than 3 days ahead loses its credit. — [Freaking Nomads](https://freakingnomads.com/lingoda-review/)
- **Preply:** lessons not scheduled before the renewal date expire. Lessons already booked are protected. On cancellation, unbooked lessons expire at the end of the 28-day cycle. — [Preply help](https://help.preply.com/en/articles/4966680-how-does-my-preply-subscription-work)
- An App Store reviewer complained that Preply's expiry rule is not clearly shown and that there is no reminder. Preply replied that the rule is mentioned at checkout. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/jp/app/preply-%E8%A8%80%E8%AA%9E%E5%AD%A6%E7%BF%92%E3%82%A2%E3%83%97%E3%83%AA-%E8%8B%B1%E8%AA%9E-%E9%9F%93%E5%9B%BD%E8%AA%9E-%E5%A4%96%E5%9B%BD%E8%AA%9E%E5%8B%89%E5%BC%B7/id1352790442)
- **Cambly:** unused minutes expire. One review says minutes reset weekly (Monday 3:00 AM local time). — [learnenglish.life review](https://www.learnenglish.life/reviews/cambly/)
- **ClassPass (help centre updated Jan 2026):** unused credits expire the day before renewal. Purchased credits can roll over, capped at the size of the next plan. Trial credits do not roll over. Cancelling forfeits the remaining credits at the end of the cycle. — [ClassPass help](https://help.classpass.com/hc/en-us/articles/209367426)
  - Some country terms (for example Argentina) say credits expire immediately on cancellation with no refund. — [ClassPass terms ARG](https://classpass.com/terms/arg)
- **Germany:** according to the consumer advice centre, BGH III ZR 157/10 (9 June 2011) made standard-term clauses that forfeit prepaid balances invalid. The repayment claim is subject only to the general 3-year limitation period. OLG München (29 U 2294/06) struck down a 365-day expiry clause, while noting that not every time limit is automatically an unreasonable disadvantage. These rulings concern telecom prepaid credit and self-paid balances; whether they apply to bonus credit is open. — [Verbraucherzentrale](https://www.verbraucherzentrale.de/wissen/digitale-welt/mobilfunk-und-festnetz/prepaidrestguthaben-nach-vertragsende-auszahlen-lassen-so-gehts-23828); [Computerwoche](https://www.computerwoche.de/article/2638685/gericht-untersagt-verfall-von-prepaid-guthaben-fuer-handys.html)
- **E-money and gift cards:** a 2011 Osborne Clarke analysis says the Second E-Money Directive (2009/110/EC) widened redemption rights and threatened gift-card breakage models. It is dated (2011) and UK-focused. — [Osborne Clarke](https://marketinglaw.osborneclarke.com/advertising-regulation/the-death-of-breakage-for-gift-cards/)

### Inferences
- **Plan or credit balance:** a subscription sold as "N classes per 4 weeks" is legally closer to a periodic service subscription than to a prepaid stored-value balance. Forfeiting unused classes at renewal is common (Preply, ClassPass), but a German or Austrian court could test it under unfair-terms rules (BGB §307 and the Unfair Contract Terms Directive). Fair, clear rollover reduces both legal risk and complaints. This is an inference, not legal advice.
- **Revenue risk:** breakage revenue was not quantified by any source found. Building the margin on breakage is risky; building it on group fill is safer.
- **Recommended policy (estimate-based design):**
  - Roll over unused classes for one cycle, capped at one cycle's allowance (as ClassPass does).
  - Keep classes usable while the subscription is active (as Lingoda does).
  - Send expiry reminders, which addresses the Preply complaint pattern.
  - Allow a pause once per cycle.

### Gaps
- No current EU-wide rule on expiry of subscription class credits was found. France, Spain and Italy were not checked.
- The EU Digital Fairness Act proposal (expected to cover subscriptions) was not researched.
- No breakage percentages were found for Lingoda, Preply or ClassPass.

## Choice overload, defaults and the number of options

### Takeaway
The evidence that "more options lowers purchase" is mixed. One meta-analysis found an average effect of about zero, and a later one found real effects under specific conditions: complex choice sets, difficult tasks, uncertain preferences, and a goal of buying quickly. A first-time language learner configuring focus × group size × frequency × time × teacher hits most of those conditions. A configurator with a sensible pre-filled default and progressive steps is therefore safer than an open grid.

### Cited Findings
- **Scheibehenne, Greifeneder & Todd (2010, Journal of Consumer Research):** 63 conditions from 50 experiments (about 5,000 participants). The mean effect of assortment size on choice overload was about zero, with considerable variance between studies, and the authors could not identify conditions that reliably produce overload. This is older than 2022 but foundational. — [Scheibehenne et al. 2010 PDF](https://abcwest.cogs.indiana.edu/pmwiki/pdf/scheibehenne.options.2010.pdf); [Phys.org summary](https://phys.org/news/2010-01-choices.pdf)
- **Chernev, Böckenholt & Goodman (2015, Journal of Consumer Psychology):** 99 observations (N = 7,202). It identified four moderators: choice-set complexity, decision-task difficulty, preference uncertainty and decision goal. Once these are accounted for, the effect of assortment size on choice overload is significant. — [Kellogg summary](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2015/when-product-assortment-leads-to-choice-overload-a-conceptual/)
- A 2022 working paper argues that existing studies probably underestimate choice overload. — [arXiv 2212.03931](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.03931)
- **The jam study:** Scheibehenne's team tried to replicate the jam and chocolate studies and found no sign of the "choice is bad" effect. The difference with the original results is unexplained. — [Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/pop-psych/201602/is-choice-overload-real-thing)
  - The widely quoted figures from Iyengar & Lepper (2000) (24 versus 6 jams, 3% versus 30% purchase) were not verified against the primary paper in this session.
- **Decoys and "recommended" labels:** practitioner sources recommend 3 tiers with the middle one marked "Recommended" or "Most popular". The claimed split (about 20% cheapest, 60-70% middle, 10-20% top) has no cited source and should not be relied on. One source warns that a decoy must be comparable on the same dimensions or it causes deferral instead. These are practitioner blogs, not controlled studies. — [Atticus Li, Medium](https://medium.com/@atticusli/the-decoy-effect-in-plan-selection-how-a-third-option-changes-everything-0f5cc35eeff0); [Fungies](https://fungies.io/saas-pricing-psychology-guide)
- **In practice:** Lingoda marks one tier "POPULAR" and Cambly marks one group frequency "BEST VALUE". — [Lingoda pricing](https://www.lingoda.com/en/pricing/); [Cambly groups](https://www.cambly.com/en/subscribe/groups)

### Inferences
- **Steps, not a grid:** use sequential steps with one decision per step, each step having 3-4 options, and pre-fill a recommended configuration. An example default: General focus, 4-6 learners, 2 classes a week, evening. The price updates live.
- **Advanced choices last:** put cost-only choices (teacher type, off-peak) behind an optional "adjust" step, so most visitors accept the default. This follows the Chernev moderators: reduce complexity and preference uncertainty.
- **Show both prices:** show the per-class price (the market convention) and the 4-week total, as Lingoda does.

### Gaps
- No controlled study was found on multi-attribute configurators for services specifically, or on the optimal number of steps.
- Mass-customisation research (for example on product configurators) was not searched.

## Operations: matching learners into groups, minimum enrolment, and under-filled classes

### Takeaway
Bricks-and-mortar language schools handle small groups openly in their terms. The common patterns are:
- fewer hours for the same fee when the group is under the minimum (for example 1 student gets 50% of the hours as one-to-one);
- converting the course to a mini-group;
- merging courses of a similar level;
- cancelling with a full refund.

Online schools seem to run small classes anyway. Lingoda users report classes running with 1-2 students, but this is anecdotal. A configurator that lets learners choose group size, time and focus fragments demand, so the number of open combinations should be limited and the under-fill rule stated at checkout.

### Cited Findings
- **Sprachschule Aktiv Ingolstadt:** minimum of 4 participants. Below that, the course is cut to 20 hours (10 lessons) or 16 hours (8 lessons) for the same fee. The school may also cancel, reschedule or convert the course to a mini-group, with no price reduction or right of cancellation for the customer. — [Sprachschule Aktiv terms](https://www.sprachschule-aktiv.de/ingolstadt/en/legal-terms/)
- **Instituto Mediterráneo Sol (Granada):** minimum of 3 per class. A single student receives 50% of the contracted lessons as one-to-one lessons; two students receive 75% as semi-private lessons. The student never pays the difference. — [Instituto Mediterráneo Sol PDF](https://www.languagecourse.net/pdf/instituto_mediterraneo_sol_grenade__1748_en_46205.pdf)
- **inlingua Como:** a possible reduction in hours is disclosed in the booking confirmation. A reviewer complained mainly about late notice of the reduction. — [Language International](https://www.languageinternational.com/school/inlingua-como-66077)
- **Carl-Schurz-Haus:** minimum of 4 participants. The school may shorten the course or combine small courses of a similar level. — [Carl-Schurz-Haus](https://www.carl-schurz-haus.de/en/english-courses/general-course-info.html)
- **Salzburg university language centre:** cancels under-subscribed courses with a full refund. — [PLUS Language Centre](https://www.plus.ac.at/language-centre/courses-at-the-language-centre/important-information/attendance-waiting-list-cancellation/?lang=en)
- **Lingoda:** no official policy for one-student group classes was found. One reviewer says classes are not cancelled when they are the only student, and another describes a group class with only two students. — [All Language Resources](https://www.alllanguageresources.com/?p=7198); [Language Advisor](https://languageadvisor.net/learning-a-language-online-my-experience-with-lingoda/)
- **Cambly** does not publish how its groups are formed or what happens if there are too few students. — [Cambly groups](https://www.cambly.com/en/subscribe/groups)

### Inferences
- **Too many combinations:** 5 focuses × 4 group sizes × several frequencies × 2 time bands × teacher types × 5 languages gives hundreds of cells, and most will never fill at an early-stage school.
- **Bookable time slots instead of open choices:** let learners choose from a published timetable of existing or forming groups per language and level (as Lingoda and ClassPass do), rather than specifying time and size freely. Learners can then express a preference (for example "evening, 4-6") and are placed into the nearest forming group.
- **Under-fill rule** (based on the Granada and Ingolstadt models), stated at checkout:
  - If a 4-6 group has only 2-3 learners, it runs as a semi-private class at the same price but shorter (for example 45 instead of 60 minutes), or at full length at Alimun's cost when the margin allows.
  - If it has only 1 learner, the class becomes a shorter one-to-one, or it is merged into another group of the same level.
  - The learner never pays more.
  - The learner is notified at least 48 hours in advance; reviews show that late notice is the main source of complaints.
- **Teacher cost:** because teachers are paid hourly per class, shortening an under-filled class (Granada model) keeps the per-class teacher cost proportional to the revenue.

### Gaps
- No data was found on online-school fill rates or how often small groups are merged.
- Lingoda's official help-centre policy on under-filled groups was not found.

## "From €X per class" advertising: EU and Canadian law

### Takeaway
**EU:** a "from" price in an advert that names the service and its price is an "invitation to purchase" under the UCPD (Ving Sverige, CJEU 2011). An entry-level price is allowed, but it must be genuinely obtainable, include taxes and unavoidable charges, and the advert must give or link to the main characteristics. Whether a given "from" price misleads is decided case by case. The Price Indication Directive (including its 30-day prior-price rule) covers goods, not services, but showing a fake "struck-through" price is still caught by the UCPD.

**UK regulator practice (ASA, 2025)** requires a "significant proportion" of the product to be actually available at the "from" price.

**Canada** (federal drip-pricing rule since 2022, and Quebec CPA s.224(c)) requires the advertised price to include all mandatory fees apart from taxes.

### Cited Findings
- **CJEU, Ving Sverige (C-122/10, 12 May 2011):** a commercial communication is an invitation to purchase once it gives the product and price in a way that lets the consumer make a transactional decision. A real opportunity to buy in the same communication is not required. The price requirement can be met by stating an **entry-level price** (the lowest price at which the product can be bought). The advert may give only some main characteristics and refer to a website for the rest. Whether an entry-level price misleads is for the national court to decide. The case concerned a newspaper ad "New York from SEK 7 820". This is pre-2022 but still the leading case. — [IPPT summary](https://www.ippt.eu/items/ippt20110512-cjeu-konsumentombudsman-v-ving); [Osborne Clarke](https://marketinglaw.osborneclarke.com/advertising-regulation/was-swedish-ad-an-invitation-to-purchase/); [Lexploria summary](https://app.lexploria.com/document/8346-judgment-of-the-court-second-chamber-of-12-may-2011-konsumentombudsmannen-v-ving-sverige-ab/summary)
- **Price Indication Directive:**
  - It applies only to "products" defined as movable goods, not services. — [EuroCommerce 2022 recommendations](https://www.eurocommerce.eu/app/uploads/2022/10/2022.10.14-EuroCommerce-Recommendations-Price-Indication-Directive.pdf)
  - The Article 6a prior-price (30-day lowest price) Notice was adopted in Dec 2021 and applies from 28 May 2022. — [European Commission: PID](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/consumer-protection-law/unfair-commercial-practices-law/price-indication-directive_en)
  - Member States interpret Article 6a differently. — [EuroCommerce joint paper 2024](https://www.eurocommerce.eu/app/uploads/2024/03/20240229-pid-joint-paper-ee-eurocommerce-ire-final.pdf)
- **UK ASA (late 2025, hotel rulings):** "from" price claims require that a significant proportion of the rooms is available at the advertised price, across a range of dates if the ad does not name dates. — [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/11/24/asa-acts-on-misleading-hotel-pricing-102lvpo)
- **UK ASA (July 2025):** where prices change with demand, CAP guidance allows a "from" price with wording that prices may change and when they were last updated. — [Browne Jacobson](https://www.brownejacobson.com/insights/asa-monthly-insights-july-2025-fourteen-rulings-you-need-to-know)
- **Canada (federal):** since 23 June 2022, drip pricing is an express deceptive marketing practice under the Competition Act (civil s.74.01(1.1), criminal s.52(1.3)). A price that cannot be obtained because of mandatory fixed fees, other than government charges such as sales tax, is misleading. Civil penalties for a first violation are up to CAD $10M or 3× the benefit. — [Competition Bureau news release](https://www.canada.ca/en/competition-bureau/news/2022/06/important-amendments-to-the-competition-act-come-into-effect.html); [Miller Thomson](https://www.millerthomson.com/en/insights/competition-antitrust-foreign-investment/drip-pricing-canada-competition-act/)
- **Quebec:** CPA s.224(c) forbids charging more than the advertised price. The advertised price must include all amounts owed except taxes (QST and GST may be excluded). ### Inferences
These are not legal advice.
- **What the "from" price must be:** "From €X per class" must be:
  - the real lowest per-class price a consumer can buy (for example the largest group, cheapest language, off-peak slot, longest commitment);
  - inclusive of VAT for EU consumers (UCPD Art. 7(4)(c));
  - genuinely available, meaning there are open groups at that price, by analogy with the ASA's "significant proportion" test;
  - stated with the conditions next to it, for example "from €X per class in groups of 7-8 with a 12-week plan".
- **Commitment-based "from" prices:** if the lowest price requires an annual commitment (as Cambly's $8.65 does), saying so beside the price reduces the risk of a misleading-omission finding.
- **No invented reference prices:** struck-through "was" prices, as on Lingoda's page, must reflect real prior prices. Even though the PID's 30-day rule targets goods, a fake reference price is a misleading action under the UCPD. Alimun's own rule against invented numbers already points the same way.
- **Canada and Quebec:** the configurator's displayed total must include every mandatory fee (any platform, booking or payment fee). Only taxes may be added at checkout.
