# Morocco: tax, consumer law, data protection and contractor rules for a Canadian online education company (2025-2026)

Not legal advice. Research done 11 Nov 2026. Sources marked "commercial blog" are consultancies selling services; primary texts (DGI, CGI, Law 31-08, CNDP deliberations) are preferred where found.

## 1. VAT on remote digital services supplied by non-residents to Moroccan consumers

### Takeaway
Yes. The 2024 Finance Law brought remote "dematerialised" services by non-residents into Moroccan VAT (CGI art. 88 and new art. 115 bis). The 2025 Finance Law refined it, and decree 2-25-862 made it operational. Since 11 June 2026 a non-resident without a Moroccan establishment selling to non-VAT-registered Moroccan customers must register on the DGI's "Taxation on digital services" platform, file quarterly and pay 20% VAT. Online courses and e-learning are named as in scope, and no threshold is published. Alimun's app subscriptions and live online classes sold to Moroccan consumers are very likely covered.

### Cited Findings
- **2024 Finance Law:** extended art. 88-2° CGI territoriality rules to services supplied at a distance "de manière dématérialisée" by non-resident companies to clients in Morocco. It applies to operations from 1 July 2024 and adds art. 115 bis obligations, including keeping a register of services for 10 years where no tax representative is accredited. — [Deloitte Avocats, LF 2024](https://blog.avocats.deloitte.fr/maroc-principales-dispositions-de-la-loi-de-finances-pour-2024/); [LPA Law Smart News LF 2024](https://www.lpalaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/2024.01.03-Smart-News-Les-principales-dispositions-de-la-Loi-de-Finances-2024.pdf)
- **2025 Finance Law (law 60-24), DGI official summary:**
  - it removes from scope services supplied at a distance to a client who is only occasionally resident in Morocco, since that client pays VAT in their home country;
  - it defines criteria for establishing Moroccan tax residence, so non-residents can identify the art. 115 bis clients;
  - it switches the turnover return from monthly to quarterly, filed via the electronic platform.

  — [DGI / Ministère des Finances, Note synthétique LF 2025, section III-1](https://www.finances.gov.ma/Publication/dgi/2024/synthetique-mesures-fiscaleslF2025.pdf)
- **Residence indicators (art. 88-2° as amended):** the client is treated as Moroccan-domiciled if any one of these applies: a Moroccan invoicing address, a bank card issued by a Moroccan institution, a Moroccan IP address, or the Moroccan dialling code. — [Upsilon Consulting, VAT digital services Morocco 2026 (commercial blog, Apr 2026)](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/en/vat-digital-services-morocco/). I did not read this list in the CGI text itself, so verify it there.
- **Official DGI communiqué (2026):** applies to any non-resident with no establishment in Morocco that supplies remote dematerialised services to non-VAT-registered clients having their seat, establishment or tax domicile in Morocco. The SIMPL e-service "Taxation on digital services" on www.tax.gov.ma allows the formalities from 11 June 2026 (art. 28 of decree 2-25-862 of 27 Nov 2025, BO 11 Dec 2025 Arabic / 18 Dec 2025 French). The obligations are:
  1. register and obtain a tax identifier (IF);
  2. before the end of the first month of each quarter, file the return of turnover made in Morocco in the previous quarter;
  3. record VAT payments made;
  4. keep, and provide on request, a register of the remote services sold in Morocco.

  A DGI guide is under Téléservices SIMPL / Taxation on digital services. Support: tsn_support@tax.gov.ma. — [DGI communiqué "TVA numérique" 2026 (PDF)](https://www.tax.gov.ma/wps/wcm/connect/2e558204-7f73-4639-8b97-7b34205192cb/Communiqu%C3%A9+_TVA+num%C3%A9rique++_2026-FR.pdf?MOD=AJPERES&amp=&CACHEID=ROOTWORKSPACE-2e558204-7f73-4639-8b97-7b34205192cb-pUBFEgk)
- **Rate and scope:** 20% standard rate, with no reduced rate for these services. The scope listed includes SaaS subscriptions, online sale of intangibles such as app downloads, online courses and digital licences, streaming, advertising and cloud. B2B sales to VAT-registered Moroccan clients use reverse charge instead. Article dated 3 Aug 2026. — [Le360](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/netflix-google-meta-airbnb-le-nouveau-dispositif-de-tva-face-au-defi-de-son-application_F6Q6MRBR7BGLBB6UTNKSIYHFWQ/)
- **E-learning named in scope:** e-learning and online training platforms are listed as covered, with no education exemption mentioned. — [We Process (commercial blog, Mar 2026)](https://we-process.com/article-115-bis-cgi-maroc-tva-prestataires-numeriques-etrangers/); [Upsilon](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/en/vat-digital-services-morocco/)
- **Payment, no deduction, 10-year register:** the provider pays the tax without any right to deduct input VAT, and the register is kept for ten years. — [Hespress (search summary)](https://fr.hespress.com/375570-tva-sur-les-fournitures-transfrontieres-de-services-et-de-biens-incorporels-comment-la-dgi-compte-t-elle-proceder.html)
- **First filing deadline:** the first turnover return was due by 2 November 2026. This comes from a Hespress headline that the search tool summarised; I did not verify it in the communiqué. — [Hespress](https://fr.hespress.com/494552-dgi-le-2-novembre-2026-dernier-delai-pour-plusieurs-obligations-fiscales.html)
- **Alternative to the platform:** a non-resident may instead accredit a tax representative domiciled in Morocco (art. 115). — [Upsilon](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/en/vat-digital-services-morocco/)
- **B2B:** where the Moroccan client is VAT-registered, it self-assesses 20% (reverse charge). A client outside the VAT scope (for example a public body) withholds the VAT on each payment (art. 117-III). — [Upsilon](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/en/vat-digital-services-morocco/)
- **Decree adoption:** the government council adopted decree 2-25-862 in Nov 2025. — [Le Nouvelliste, 20 Nov 2025](https://www.lenouvelliste.ma/tva-numerique-le-maroc-encadre-les-prestataires-etrangers/)

### Inferences
- Alimun (Canadian, no Moroccan establishment) selling subscriptions or classes to Moroccan consumers must register on the DGI "Taxation on digital services" platform. It must then collect 20% VAT within its tax-inclusive MAD price, file quarterly by the end of the month after each quarter (January, April, July, October), and keep a 10-year sales register. If sales began on or after 11 June 2026, Q3 2026 was due by about 2 Nov 2026 (end of October fell on a weekend or holiday). Alimun should check whether it has a missed filing.
- Tax-inclusive price arithmetic: 20% VAT is 1/6 of the gross price.
- Customer location evidence: store at least two of billing address, card BIN country, IP and phone code, so the company can show which sales are Moroccan.
- Teachers' live classes are probably in scope as "remote dematerialised services". I found no official text excluding live, human-taught online lessons; the EU distinction between live teaching and automated e-services does not appear in the Moroccan sources found.

### Gaps
- **No threshold:** none is published in any source found. The rule appears to apply from the first dirham.
- **App stores:** I found no source on whether Apple or Google, as merchant of record, collect Moroccan VAT on in-app purchases, which would relieve the developer. Check Apple and Google tax pages for Morocco.
- **Stripe Tax:** I did not check whether Stripe Tax supports Moroccan registration and collection.
- **Payment and penalties:** the DGI guide, which sets payment currency and method from abroad and the penalties for late registration or filing, was not read. The penalty amounts were not found.
- **Exact CGI wording:** the official CGI 2026 text of art. 88-2° (residence indicators) was not read directly.
- **2026 Finance Law:** one secondary source (Nexora) mentions further tightening in the 2026 Finance Law, but I could not verify this.

## 2. Other taxes on foreign providers (withholding on payments abroad, stamp duty)

### Takeaway
Payments to non-residents for services made by Moroccan businesses suffer a 10% income/corporate tax withholding (CGI art. 15 and 45), subject to tax treaties. Consumers paying by card do not withhold, so this matters only for B2B sales, such as companies buying lessons for staff.

### Cited Findings
- **10% withholding:** payments by a Moroccan client to a non-resident for services bear a 10% withholding at source (CGI art. 15 and 45), except as provided by law or by a double-tax treaty. Without a tax representative, VAT due by the non-resident falls on the Moroccan client. — [AAFIR Audit & Consulting (firm page, undated)](https://aafir.ma/ras-de-la-tva-applicable-aux-personnes-non-residentes/)
- **B2B self-assessment:** reverse charge or withholding of VAT applies where the Moroccan client is a business (art. 115 and 117-III). — [Upsilon](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/en/vat-digital-services-morocco/)

### Inferences
- For B2C card sales, no withholding applies.
- For B2B invoices to Moroccan companies, expect a 10% deduction plus reverse-charge VAT. The Canada-Morocco tax treaty may reduce or remove the 10%; not verified.

### Gaps
- **Canada-Morocco treaty:** the treaty's article on services and technical fees was not checked.
- **Stamp duty:** no source found on any stamp duty for foreign digital providers.
- **Digital services tax:** no source found on a separate DST.

## 3. Consumer law: Law 31-08 (distance selling, withdrawal, subscriptions, language, prices)

### Takeaway
Law 31-08 applies to anyone offering services to Moroccan consumers by electronic means. It requires detailed pre-contract information, express acceptance of terms, and a durable-medium confirmation. Withdrawal runs 7 days from acceptance of the offer for services, or 30 days if the confirmation is missing. The right is lost once a service has begun with the consumer's agreement before day 7. Refunds are due within 15 days. Prices must be the global price including VAT, and in dirhams. Fixed-term subscriptions require a written renewal reminder at least one month before the opt-out window. No 2024-2026 amendment was found.

### Cited Findings
All findings in this section cite [Law 31-08 French text (ONSSA copy)](https://www.onssa.gov.ma/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Reglementation/C.Reglementation-Connexe/4.%20Divers/LOI.31-08.FR.pdf) unless another source is named.
- **Art. 26 (scope):** the distance-selling chapter applies to any natural or legal person offering by electronic means the supply of a service to the consumer. The supplier is fully liable for performance even when other providers, such as teachers, perform it. Art. 44 makes the chapter "d'ordre public", so a contract cannot opt out of it.
- **Art. 29 (pre-contract information):** the offer must state:
  - the main characteristics of the service;
  - the supplier's name, phone, email and physical address and registered office;
  - the existence of the art. 36 withdrawal right;
  - payment and performance terms;
  - how long the offer and price are valid;
  - the minimum duration, for continuous or periodic supply.

  Before the contract, the supplier must recap the consumer's choices and let them confirm or change them.
- **Art. 30:** contract terms must be easily accessible from the home page and expressly accepted before the order is confirmed.
- **Art. 32 (confirmation):** the confirmation, on paper or another durable medium, must include:
  - the art. 3, 5 and 29 information;
  - a complaints address;
  - withdrawal conditions;
  - termination conditions where the contract is open-ended or longer than 1 year.

  Withdrawal and complaints must be possible free of charge.
- **Art. 36 (withdrawal):** 7 days, or 30 days if the supplier fails to confirm the art. 29 and 32 information in writing. No reason or penalty is needed. For services the period runs from acceptance of the offer.
- **Art. 37 (refund):** due without delay and at the latest within 15 days, after which legal interest accrues. A commentator states 30 days ([adala.ai](https://adala.ai/blog/protection-consommateur-maroc-loi-31-08/)); the ONSSA text says 15, so use 15.
- **Art. 38 (exceptions):** unless otherwise agreed, there is no withdrawal for:
  1. services whose performance began, with the consumer's agreement, before the end of the 7 clear days;
  2. supplies whose price depends on financial-market fluctuations;
  3. personalised goods;
  4. audio, video or software unsealed by the consumer;
  5. newspapers and periodicals.
- **Art. 3 and 5 (prices):** the price must be the global price payable, including VAT, all other taxes and compulsory extras. Prices of services must be expressed in dirhams. — Law 31-08; [Almostahlik price-display fact sheet](https://almostahlik.ma/pdf/Fiche%20Pratique%20Affichage%20prix.pdf)
- **Art. 7 (fixed-term subscriptions):** the supplier must remind the consumer in writing, by a means proving receipt, at least one month before the window in which they can refuse tacit renewal. If it does not, the consumer may terminate at any time from the renewal date without penalty.
- **Art. 9:** written clauses must be clear and understandable; ambiguity is interpreted in the consumer's favour.
- **Art. 34:** the burden of proving that information was given and consent obtained lies on the supplier.
- **Implementing decree 2-12-503 (BO 11 Sep 2013):** sets out how information must be given. Sources say instructions and manuals must be in Arabic at least, and labels may add foreign languages. — [WIPO Lex, Law 31-08 and related texts](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/fr/legislation/details/19773); [Aujourd'hui le Maroc](https://aujourdhui.ma/societe/protection-du-consommateur-fini-le-vide-juridique-105681)

### Inferences
- To keep the waiver valid, checkout should get express consent to start immediately (an art. 38-1 waiver acknowledgement, a checkbox) for live classes and app access, since the withdrawal right is lost only if performance began with the consumer's agreement. Without that consent, a 7-day full refund window applies from purchase.
- Send a confirmation email containing all art. 29 and 32 items. Otherwise the withdrawal window becomes 30 days.
- Monthly auto-renewing subscriptions (indefinite duration) are not squarely within art. 7, which covers fixed-term contracts. Annual plans with tacit renewal need the one-month-ahead written reminder. Art. 32 also requires termination conditions for open-ended contracts.
- Display MAD prices that include 20% VAT.
- Arabic: offering an Arabic version of key terms and pre-contract information is the prudent course. A French-only contract is common in practice, but I found no source confirming that it satisfies the law.

### Gaps
- **Language rule:** I found no authoritative text on the required language of an online contract for services. The decree's Arabic rule is confirmed only for instructions and labelling.
- **Amendments:** I found no 2024-2026 amendment of Law 31-08 or e-commerce reform. None was found, but the search was not exhaustive.
- **Sanctions:** the fine amounts for breaching the distance-selling rules were not extracted.

## 4. Data protection: Law 09-08 and the CNDP

### Takeaway
Law 09-08 applies to a controller established in Morocco, or to one not established there that uses processing means located in Morocco. In that second case the controller must notify the CNDP of a representative in Morocco. Transfers abroad are allowed without specific authorisation only to countries on the CNDP's adequacy list, and still require notification. The list (deliberation 236-2015) includes Canada and the EU/EEA states but not the US. Transfers to the US need CNDP authorisation or a derogation such as express consent. No 2025-2026 reform has been adopted.

### Cited Findings
- **Scope and representative:** art. 2 covers controllers established in Morocco and those not established there but using means located in Morocco (transit excluded). The latter must notify the CNDP of a representative in Morocco. — [Upsilon, Loi 09-08 et entreprises étrangères (commercial blog, Apr 2026)](https://upsilon-consulting.com/loi-09-08-entreprises-etrangeres-maroc/)
- **Formalities and timing:** formalities are a prior declaration or authorisation depending on the processing. The CNDP has 2 months to decide (extendable once), and silence means approval. Transfers to non-adequate countries need prior authorisation. Fines run from MAD 10,000 to 100,000 for processing without declaration or authorisation, doubled for legal entities. — [Upsilon](https://upsilon-consulting.com/loi-09-08-entreprises-etrangeres-maroc/)
- **CNDP deliberation 236-2015 (18 Dec 2015, amending 465-2013):** subject to notifying the CNDP of any transfer under the appropriate regime, the following countries offer sufficient protection: Germany, Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, **Canada**, Cyprus, Denmark, Spain, Estonia, Finland, France, Greece, Hungary, Ireland, Iceland, Italy, Latvia, Liechtenstein, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Malta, Norway, Netherlands, Poland, Portugal, Czech Republic, Romania, United Kingdom, Slovakia, Slovenia, Sweden and Switzerland. The **US is not listed.** — [CNDP deliberation 236-2015 (PDF)](https://www.cndp.ma/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/deliberation-n-236-2015-18-12-2015.pdf)
- **Transfers to non-listed countries (arts. 43-44):** they require the data subject's express consent, an art. 44 necessity case, or a CNDP authorisation based on guarantees such as contractual clauses. Hosting data in foreign cloud services counts as a transfer. — [Upsilon](https://upsilon-consulting.com/loi-09-08-entreprises-etrangeres-maroc/)
- **CNDP notification procedure (July 2025):** the CNDP published an updated procedure, which includes a summary impact assessment when a transfer is involved. — [CNDP Procédure de notification des traitements, 24 Jul 2025](https://www.cndp.ma/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/CNDP_Procedure-de-Notification-des-traitements_20250724.pdf)
- **Reform:** commentary in 2026 says 09-08 needs revision for AI and blockchain, but there is no adopted reform. — [TelQuel, 10 Apr 2026](https://telquel.ma/2026/04/10/donnees-souverainete-et-competitivite-ou-se-situe-le-maroc_1983878)

### Inferences
- Alimun has no Moroccan establishment and its servers sit in the EU (Supabase eu-west-1), Canada or the US. Whether it "uses means located in Morocco" is arguable: Moroccan teachers process learner data, and there may be cookies on Moroccan devices. If it does, it needs a CNDP representative and notification.
- Supabase in Ireland and Canada are on the adequacy list. Transfers to US processors (for example Vercel, Daily.co, Resend, Stripe) would need consent, authorisation or a derogation. Explicit consent at sign-up is the cheapest derogation.
- Practical steps:
  - appoint a Moroccan representative if pursuing compliance;
  - file the notification or declaration via cndp.ma;
  - add a 09-08 section to the privacy notice (CNDP receipt number, rights of access, rectification and objection);
  - get express consent for transfers to the US.

### Gaps
- **Scope for Alimun:** no CNDP guidance or case was found applying art. 2 to a purely foreign online service with no Moroccan servers.
- **Later list changes:** whether the adequacy list changed after 2015 was not confirmed, but no later deliberation was found.
- **Teachers' processing:** whether Moroccan teachers processing learner data creates an "establishment" or "means" is unresolved.

## 5. Teachers in Morocco: auto-entrepreneur status, 30% rule, CPU, foreign currency

### Takeaway
Under the auto-entrepreneur regime, teachers pay 1% of cash turnover on services, with a MAD 200,000 cap. Turnover above MAD 80,000 a year from a single client is subject to a 30% withholding "operated by the client". No source addresses a foreign client with no Moroccan presence, and a Canadian company has no mechanism to withhold Moroccan tax. Teachers face an Office des Changes duty to repatriate service-export proceeds, with a 90-day deadline reported for individuals.

### Cited Findings
- **2026 regime (DGI guide):** caps are MAD 200,000 for services and MAD 500,000 for commerce, industry and crafts, on cash received. Rates are 1% (services) and 0.5% (commerce), as a final tax. Exceeding the cap two consecutive years moves the taxpayer to the real regime unless they opt for the simplified regime or the CPU. Turnover from one client above MAD 80,000 a year bears a 30% withholding at source deducted by that client. Registration in the RNAE and social coverage are conditions. Article dated 19 Aug 2026. — [Le Matin](https://lematin.ma/economie/auto-entrepreneur-ce-que-prevoit-le-regime-fiscal-en-2026/361537)
- **Effect of the 30% withholding:** it is final and replaces the 1% on the excess. "Formation" (training) is listed among services. The source has no date but appears to be 2026. — [Beancount.io (commercial blog)](https://beancount.io/fr/blog/2026/09/15/morocco-auto-entrepreneur-revenue-caps-2026-cpu-cnss-crossing-guide)
- **Legal basis:** CGI art. 73-II-G-8° (2023 Finance Law). Worked example: MAD 90,000 billed to one client means a 30% withholding on the MAD 10,000 excess, or MAD 3,000. — [Captain Legal (commercial blog)](https://www.captain.legal/ma/blog/auto-entrepreneur-sarl-seuil-80000-dh/)
- **Foreign clients:** no source addresses whether the rule applies to foreign clients. A forum question on exactly this went unanswered. Service export is allowed for auto-entrepreneurs. — [search summary of LesMRE guide](https://www.lesmre.com/fr/guides/auto-entrepreneur-maroc-mre-2026)
- **Private lessons:** "cours particuliers" is listed as a common auto-entrepreneur service. Ineligible activities are set by the arrêté of 3 July 2015 (regulated professions are excluded). The official activity list is on ae.gov.ma. — [Humantal guide](https://humantal.ma/ressources/statut-auto-entrepreneur-maroc-guide-complet); [Upsilon AE guide](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/2021/10/auto-entrepreneur/)
- **CPU:** caps are MAD 500,000 (services) and MAD 2,000,000 (commerce), with higher rates; the source gives no figures. — [Beancount.io](https://beancount.io/fr/blog/2026/09/15/morocco-auto-entrepreneur-revenue-caps-2026-cpu-cnss-crossing-guide)
- **Office des Changes:** IGOC art. 396 (2013 version) requires any service exporter to collect and repatriate export proceeds and justify them. A 90-day repatriation deadline applies, and individuals' online-service remuneration must be repatriated within 90 days of performance. A dedicated unit has controlled individuals since 2018. The fnh article is older; flag as pre-2023. — [Office des Changes, rapatriement exportations de services](https://www.oc.gov.ma/fr/personnes-morales/rapatriement-du-produit-des-exportations-de-services); [FNH](https://fnh.ma/article/actualite-economique/office-des-changes-le-rapatriement-des-remunerations-doit-etre-realise-dans-un-delai-de-90-jours)
- **IGOC 2026:** a new General Instruction on Exchange Operations was published for 2026, so article numbers may have changed. — [FNH, IGOC 2026](https://fnh.ma/article/actualite-financiere-maroc/office-des-changes-igoc-2026)

### Inferences
- Moroccan withholding obligations bind payers within Moroccan tax jurisdiction. A Canadian company with no Moroccan presence probably cannot and need not withhold. The teacher, though, may still be expected to self-declare the tax on the excess above MAD 80,000. This is unverified; ask the DGI or a Moroccan fiscaliste.
- Practical step: keep each Morocco-based teacher's annual pay visible. A teacher earning over MAD 80,000 from Alimun has a tax issue, and one over MAD 200,000 hits the cap. Point them to their accountant rather than withholding.
- Teachers should receive payment into a Moroccan bank account, which brings foreign currency in and satisfies repatriation. Payouts to foreign accounts or wallets that are not repatriated risk Office des Changes sanctions.
- Paying people as contractors who work exclusively and continuously for one client also carries a misclassification risk under Moroccan labour and CNSS rules. This was not researched.

### Gaps
- **30% rule for foreign clients:** I found no official DGI position. The CGI art. 73 / 156-157 text was not read.
- **Activity code:** the specific code for language teaching or online tutoring on ae.gov.ma was not confirmed.
- **IGOC 2026 rules:** the rules for individuals receiving service income from abroad (card, PayPal, Payoneer, Wise) were not read.
- **CPU rate:** the CPU rate for teaching was not found.

## 6. Licensing for language courses or exam preparation; mentioning official exams

### Takeaway
Morocco's private education law 06-00 explicitly covers language teaching, tutoring and distance or correspondence teaching. A provider operating in Morocco would normally need authorisation. Whether this reaches a foreign company teaching online from abroad is untested. No source covered the rules on naming IELTS or Goethe exams.

### Cited Findings
- **Law 06-00:** the private school law's art. 1 includes "l'enseignement de langue et l'organisation de cours d'appui" and "l'enseignement à distance et par correspondance". It replaced law 15-86. — [Law 06-00 text, Université Laval (CEFAN)](https://www.axl.cefan.ulaval.ca/afrique/maroc-loi-06-2000.htm)
- **Distance-learning decree:** a decree on distance learning (2.20.474) was adopted by the government council in July 2021 under framework law 51-17. This is older than 2023 and the final BO text was not confirmed. — [SNRT News](https://snrtnews.com/fr/article/enseignement-%C3%A0-distance-le-projet-de-loi-adopt%C3%A9)
- **Licence number disclosure:** a cyber-merchant whose activity is subject to licensing must state the licence number, date and issuing authority in the offer (art. 29). — [Law 31-08](https://www.onssa.gov.ma/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Reglementation/C.Reglementation-Connexe/4.%20Divers/LOI.31-08.FR.pdf)

### Inferences
- Risk is low but not zero. Law 06-00 targets establishments in Morocco. A Canadian online provider is not an "établissement" in Morocco, but using Moroccan-resident teachers and Moroccan marketing could draw attention. Avoid presenting Alimun as a Moroccan school or as issuing recognised diplomas.
- Exam names: describe preparation "in the format of" IELTS, Goethe and similar, and do not imply affiliation or certification. This is standard trademark practice and not Morocco-specific.

### Gaps
- **Law 06-00 for foreign providers:** no source on its application to foreign online providers, or on ministry practice regarding online language schools.
- **Decree 2.20.474:** final text not found.
- **Exam names:** no Morocco-specific rule found on referencing official exams. The exam owners' trademark and brand guidelines (British Council/IDP, Goethe-Institut) were not checked.
