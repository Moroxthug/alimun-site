# Let teacher cost set Alimun's price floor

Alimun can be the cheapest live option per class hour in Europe, Canada and most of Latin America while paying teachers above the online market, but only for small groups, and only if the flat €20/hour minimum becomes a per-language rate card: €13 for English and Spanish, €15 for French, €16 for Italian, and €20 kept for German. Teacher pay and payment fees, not purchasing power, set the price floor. A 60-minute group of four learners costs about €14.50 to run in English and €21.70 in German wherever the learners live. So purchasing-power bands compress to three: Band A at 100%, Band B at 85% and Band C at 75% of the single EU price, where Band C is the floor. On that basis, an English or Spanish group hour costs **€6.88 in the EU** (Lingoda's group classes cost €7.33-9.49), **C$10.63 in Canada** (Alliance Française charges C$15-20) and **56 MAD in Morocco** (British Council charges 83-103 MAD). At four learners per six-seat class, the contribution margin is about 21-42% before overheads and acquisition. The mission breaks in three places. German is not cheaper than Goethe-Institut's large in-person groups in Morocco or India. Private lessons are not cheaper than marketplace tutors. And a €3 lesson cannot pay a teacher above market unless five or more paying learners share the hour. Morocco (German, then English), Canada outside Quebec (French exam prep) and Brazil and Colombia (English, then German for nurses) are the strongest first markets. Exchange rates, local digital-services taxes and the Canadian sales-tax treatment need re-checking before launch.

## German for Morocco, French exam prep for Canada, English and German for Brazil and Colombia

Morocco's strongest paid demand is German. It is deadline-driven: Ausbildung visas generally require B1 and nursing commonly B2. About **23,000 Moroccans held German work or skilled-work residence titles in April 2026**, and Germany's work titles for non-EU nationals rose from 133,000 in 2023 to 205,000 in 2025 ([Le Brief](https://www.lebrief.ma/immigration-professionnelle-23-000-marocains-actifs-en-allemagne-100156547/); [ijob.ma](https://ijob.ma/immigration/ausbildung-allemagne-2026-formation-professionnelle-visa-marocains-b1-remuneration/)). Morocco is also the fifth-largest source of study migrants in Germany, with 37,000 ([Morocco World News](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2026/08/335475/37000-study-migrants-rank-morocco-5th-among-germanys-source-countries/)). English comes second. Demand is broad and centred on IELTS, but about 80% of Morocco's offshoring work faces France ([Le360](https://en.le360.ma/economy/50000-jobs-at-risk-as-frances-telemarketing-ban-hits-moroccan-call-centers_SOVLIMMTGFBEZARM74W323MAS4/)). Italian is a niche. About 1,500 Italian student visas went to Moroccans in 2024, and work permits for Moroccans under the decreto flussi now wait on extra labour-inspectorate checks ([InfoMigrants](https://www.infomigrants.net/en/post/67369/20-italian-academies-open-their-doors-to-moroccan-students); [Ministero del Lavoro](https://www.lavoro.gov.it/documenti-e-norme/normativa/Circolare-8047-del-16102025)). Lead Morocco with German B1/B2 exam tracks and IELTS-style English, and run Italian as a small cohort.

In Canada, French for immigration is the paying market. French-category Express Entry draws issued about **42,000 invitations in 2025 and 40,500 by 22 July 2026**, at CRS cut-offs of roughly 380-430, well below general draws ([Moving2Canada](https://moving2canada.com/news-and-features/news/immigration/latest-french-draw-november-28-2025/); [immigration.ca](https://immigration.ca/canada-invites-5000-french-speaking-candidates-express-entry-crs-399-july-22-2026/)). Who can be sold to depends on the province. Quebec's Francisation Québec is free to almost every resident aged 16 or over, served more than 90,000 people in 2024-25, and had only about 6,000 people waiting in February 2026 ([Québec.ca](https://www.quebec.ca/nouvelles/actualites/details/troisieme-annee-doperation-francisation-quebec-trouve-son-rythme-de-croisiere-69703)). Outside Quebec, temporary residents and citizens are generally excluded from the free LINC/CLIC programmes ([Welcomeaide](https://welcomeaide.com/es/blog/linc-language-classes-newcomers-guide)). So the targets are TEF/TCF prep for people abroad and for temporary residents outside Quebec, with only exam prep sold inside Quebec. English (CELPIP/IELTS) is second, and its pool is shrinking as temporary-resident arrivals fall from a 673,650 target in 2025 to 385,000 in 2026 ([IRCC](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/transparency/committees/cimm-dec-04-2025/temporary-resident-target.html)). No data was found on the size of Spanish demand in Canada. Treat it as a hobby add-on, not a launch product.

In South America, English has the broadest need in Brazil, Colombia and Ecuador, which score below Costa Rica's 516 on EF EPI 2025. Argentina (575) is a market for advanced English rather than beginners ([Delfino.cr](https://delfino.cr/2025/11/ef-epi-2025-evidencia-caida-del-ingles-en-costa-rica-estudiantes-con-el-nivel-mas-bajo-del-pais); [Mercado](https://mercado.com.ar/noticias/argentina-alcanza-el-puesto-26-en-el-indice-de-proficiencia-en-ingles-de-ef-education-first)). German has a defined pipeline: the Bundesagentur für Arbeit has recruited nurses from **Brazil since 2019 and Colombia since 2022**, with language preparation in the home country ([Nordkurier](https://www.nordkurier.de/politik/deutschland-wirbt-um-pflegekraefte-aus-lateinamerika-1763393)). About 6.9 million Venezuelans live elsewhere in the region, mainly in Colombia, Peru, Brazil, Chile and Ecuador ([R4V/ReliefWeb](https://reliefweb.int/report/colombia/venezuelan-refugees-and-migrants-region-november-2025)). Market to them in those host countries, not in Venezuela.

Beyond the named markets, German has the deepest pools in India and Turkey. India has about 59,000 students in Germany and leads Opportunity Card issuance with more than 3,700. Turkey leads skilled-visa and recognition counts ([DAAD](https://www.daad.de/en/press-releases/erneut-hohe-zahl-an-internationalen-studierenden-in-deutschland/); [VISAGUARD.Berlin](https://www.visaguard.berlin/en/post/opportunity-map-first-official-figures-show-great-interest-over-11-000-visas-since-launch); [Bundestag hib](https://www.bundestag.de/presse/hib/kurzmeldungen-1053332)). Next come the Western Balkans, where the Westbalkanregelung quota doubled to 50,000 a year ([Bundesagentur für Arbeit](https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/presse/2026-07-fuenf-jahre-fachkraefteeinwanderungsgesetz)), followed by Tunisia and the Philippines (nurses) and Egypt. For Italian, the best fits are Albania, India and Pakistan (students), Egypt and Bangladesh (flussi lists), and the worldwide B1-for-citizenship exam market ([Infoimmigrazione](https://www.infoimmigrazione.com/decreto-flussi-2026-2028/); [The Local](https://www.thelocal.it/20241014/how-to-organise-your-language-test-for-italian-citizenship)). The B1 market is mostly EU residents and spouses paying in euros. English is weakest where local supply is cheapest, in South Asia, the Philippines and Nigeria. It fits Turkey and adult learners in the EU better, but no sourced data backs those last two. Iran and Syria cannot be served through Stripe.

At the bottom, the rule would pay a Filipino, Indian or Egyptian teacher €2-3 an hour, below what Cambly and Lingoda pay the same people. At the top, Germany's integration-course floor has stayed at €42.23 per 45-minute unit since August 2022, and **93.9% of freelance integration teachers are paid exactly that floor** ([Bundestag 21/3404](https://dserver.bundestag.de/btd/21/034/2103404.pdf)). Plus 20%, that is €67.57 an hour, roughly three times what a group class can bear. A rule that needs both a floor and a cap is really a per-language rate. The market an online teacher actually competes in is the online-platform market, not their local school market.

The recommended rate card pays everyone teaching the same language the same rate, wherever they live. It is set at about the online market plus 20%. **English and Spanish get €13/h**: Lingoda's €11 plus 20%, about 1.4 times Cambly's $10.20, and about 1.5-9 times local school pay in every non-EU market sourced. **French gets €15/h.** That is at the French salaried gross rate. It sits below French freelance rates, so recruit French teachers mainly outside France. **Italian gets €16/h**, the Preply Italian midpoint of about €13.85 plus 20% ([ThinkInItalian](https://www.thinkinitalian.com/italian-language-classes-prices/)). **German stays at €20/h.** Online German tutors list at about €28-33 and net roughly €19-27 after Preply's 18-33% commission ([Preply](https://preply.com/en/online/german-tutors?ref=hackernoon.com); [Languatalk](https://languatalk.com/blog/how-much-do-German-tutors-charge/)), so €20 is above every local market outside Germany but cannot buy Germany-resident integration teachers. Compared with the flat €20, the rate card saves 35% on English and Spanish, 25% on French, 20% on Italian and nothing on German.

Paying a flat €20 for every language does not work at competitive prices. At the recommended EU English price, a four-learner class earns €22.05 net. Its contribution falls from **€7.59 (34%) at €13 to €0.38 (2%) at €20**. In Morocco it falls from €4.53 to minus €2.68. The non-rate terms matter as much as the rate. Lingoda reportedly pays only 50% for no-shows, while Cambly and Preply leave waiting time and trials unpaid ([tefl.org](https://www.tefl.org/teach-english-online/lingoda/); [lilachbullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/are-online-tutoring-jobs-legitimate-pay/)). Alimun should pay 100% for any scheduled class, include no-shows, and publish fixed weekly cohort slots. Both cost little and stand out more than an extra euro an hour.

## Three bands, three group tiers, and no €3 lesson

The unit cost of a class is almost the same everywhere, which is why bands compress. Video on Daily costs $0.004 per participant-minute, or about **€0.21 per participant-hour** ([Daily](https://www.daily.co/pricing/video-sdk/)). A 60-minute English class with four learners and a €13 teacher therefore costs €13 × 1.03 (payout allowance) + 5 × €0.21 = **€14.46**. A German class costs €20.60 + €1.07 = **€21.67**. To cover that with a 25% margin, a non-EU English seat must net about €4.80 an hour. That takes a price of about €5.20 an hour after card and currency fees, roughly 75% of the EU shelf price. Purchasing-power data would push discounts much lower. The 2025 World Bank price level is 0.80 of US prices in Germany, 0.42 in Morocco and 0.23 in India ([World Bank WDI](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/MAR;CAN;BRA;COL;PER;CHL;ARG;ECU;MEX;IND;TUR;EGY;TUN;PHL;ALB;XKX;UZB;PAK;DEU;FRA;ITA;ESP/indicator/PA.NUS.PPP?format=json&date=2021:2025&per_page=500)), and a pure-PPP band would push India to about 30% of the EU price. Below 75%, each seat sold loses money unless groups fill up. Steam's "multi-variable" method blends purchasing power with the local price of comparable goods and keeps a minimum price floor ([Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/pricing)). That is the right pattern here, with teacher cost as the floor.

That gives three bands. **Band A (100%)** covers the EU and EEA at one euro price, plus the UK, Switzerland, the US and Canada. **Band B (85%)** covers Brazil, Mexico, Chile, Peru, Argentina (priced in USD), Turkey (priced in EUR) and Albania, which have price levels of 0.41-0.54 and middle incomes. **Band C (75%, the floor)** covers Morocco, Colombia, Ecuador, Kosovo, Tunisia, the Philippines, Uzbekistan, Egypt, India and Pakistan. A fourth or fifth band would not go lower, because Band C already sits on cost. Private lessons get only two prices, full price in Band A and 85% elsewhere. A private hour has a single payer, so it cannot be discounted further without dropping below cost.

Tiers should vary in one dimension at a time. Use three group volumes, Light (4 classes a month), Regular (8) and Intensive (12). Add one exam cohort (12 classes, up to 8 learners, launching at 6) and two private packs (4 or 8 hours). Offer a single commitment discount of 10% for three months prepaid. This mirrors the market: Lingoda shows four volume tiers and Cambly three format tiers, each across monthly, quarterly and annual terms. Simon-Kucher finds that a third, premium option shifts buyers toward the middle ([Lingoda](https://www.lingoda.com/en/pricing); [Simon-Kucher](https://www.simon-kucher.com/en/insights/rise-and-fall-good-better-best-packaging)). Annual plans keep more subscribers in their first year than monthly plans (20-40% vs 6-14% for education apps) ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-education)). Even so, hold back annual plans until cohort churn is measured, because a live school owes the classes it presells. The site's design rules ban badges and pills, so highlight Regular through layout and a sentence, not a "Popular" tag.

A standing €3 lesson does not work. A €3 seat nets about €2.81 outside the EU after international card and FX fees. Covering a €13 English teacher plus video would then take **5.2 paying learners per hour**, or 6.1 in the EU after VAT, before any margin. For German it takes about eight. Competitors that advertise about €3 do so with short lessons, 40-lesson or annual plans, or promotions: Cambly Groups "from $3", Engoo's $4.34 per 25 minutes, and EF's bundles ([Cambly](https://www.cambly.com/groups); [Engoo](https://engoo.com/plans)). Use €3 as a trial price instead. Charge €3 (or the local equivalent) for 14 days with up to three group classes, credit it to the first month, and place trial learners only into classes that are already running. The extra cost of a trial seat in an existing class is about €0.21 of video per hour. A card on file also fixes the learner's price band at signup. RevenueCat finds that trials of 17 days or more convert at 42.5%, against 25.5% for trials of four days or less. It also finds that monthly plans gain nothing from trials longer than about two weeks ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/free-trial-length.md)).

## Unit economics: 21-42% contribution at four learners, 36-53% at five

The model runs on stated assumptions, and each one can be checked. Classes are 60 minutes, with up to 6 learners in groups and up to 8 in exam cohorts. Base fill is 4 paid learners per group (67%); the sensitivity cases use 5, and 5-7 for exam cohorts. Teacher pay follows the rate card, plus 3% for payouts. That allowance is an estimate. Stripe charges 0.25% per cross-border Connect payout to self-serve countries, and Wise or Payoneer are needed for Morocco and Latin America. A $29-a-month contractor platform such as Remote or Oyster would add about 5% for a teacher doing 40 hours a month ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/cross-border-payouts); [Rivermate](https://rivermate.com/es/blog/precio-remoto-com)). Video is €0.21 per participant-hour.

Payment fees follow Stripe Ireland's pricing. EEA cards cost 1.5% + €0.25. International cards cost 3.15% + €0.25 plus 2% currency conversion. Pix costs 2% + 1.5% international + 2% conversion. Billing (0.7%) and Tax (0.5%) apply to every charge ([Stripe IE](https://stripe.com/ie/pricing); [Stripe local methods](https://stripe.com/ie/pricing/local-payment-methods)).

Taxes differ by market. EU VAT is assumed at 20% inside the price. Live online teaching to EU consumers has been taxed at the customer's rate since 2025, and the actual rate varies by member state ([BDO](https://www.bdo.co.uk/en-gb/insights/tax/vat-and-indirect-taxes/new-vat-rules-for-online-entertainment-culture-and-education-in-2025)). Canadian prices are shown before GST/HST/QST, which is added at checkout once Alimun passes C$30,000 of sales ([Stripe Canada guide](https://stripe.com/ie/resources/more/canada-vat)). Colombia's 19% VAT on foreign digital services is included in the price ([vatcalc](https://vatcalc.com/?p=6605)). For Brazil, a 10% provision covers the new CBS on non-resident digital suppliers. That figure is an estimate, because the sources disagree on the start date and the rate ([vatcalc](https://www.vatcalc.com/brazil/brazil-vat-non-resident-digital-services-providers-marketplaces-2026/)). Morocco, India and Turkey are modelled with no local tax. No foreign-supplier regime was confirmed in the notes for them, which is a gap.

Exchange rates are ECB reference rates of 23 October 2026 for CAD (1.5665), BRL (5.6485), INR (106.272), USD (1.1193) and MXN (21.0226). MAD is taken as 11.0 and COP as 3,549.8 from an aggregator, because the ECB does not publish them ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/eurofxref/eurofxref-daily.xml); [open.er-api](https://open.er-api.com/v6/latest/EUR)). Earlier notes used BRL 6.2, COP 4,600 and CAD 1.61. Re-check all rates on launch day.

The arithmetic runs like this for the EU English Regular plan. The €55 price includes €9.17 of VAT and €1.73 of fees (2.7% × 55 + 0.25). That leaves €44.10, or €5.51 per seat-hour over 8 classes. Four seats make €22.05 per class. After the €14.46 cost, €7.59 is left, a 34% margin. Each learner contributes €15.17 a month (7.59 ÷ 4 × 8).

Exam cohorts (12 classes, up to 8 learners) earn less per seat and need six learners. At fill 6, the EU German cohort at €87 contributes €12.85 per class (37%), Morocco German at 719 MAD contributes €8.38 (27%), and India German at ₹6,999 contributes €8.61 (28%). At fill 5, those margins drop to 14-25%. Private lessons are thin at €2.74-6.08 per hour (15-31%). An EU English 8-pack at €185 nets €18.62 an hour against a €13.82 cost, a 26% margin. A German 8-pack at €255 nets €25.67 against €21.03 (18%). A Moroccan English 8-pack at 1,729 MAD nets €18.37 against €13.82 (25%).

Fill rate is the biggest lever. With three learners, almost every group product falls to 0-18% contribution, and Band C products go negative. So the scheduler needs a hard rule: open a class at four bookings and merge classes that fall below three.

Churn and acquisition set how much can be spent on marketing. No live-tutoring churn benchmark was found. Consumer e-learning averages about 4.9% monthly churn, about 1.1 points of it from failed payments. Language apps report about 10.5% ([Focus Digital](https://focus-digital.co/average-churn-rate-subscription-services/); [RetentionCheck](https://retentioncheck.com/churn-benchmarks/language-learning-apps)). With an assumed 10% monthly churn, an EU English learner is worth about €152 in contribution and a Moroccan English learner about €91. At 20% churn, those fall to €76 and €45. Keeping lifetime value at three times acquisition cost allows roughly €25-50 per paying learner in the EU and €15-30 in Morocco. Facebook ad impressions cost $8-11 per thousand in Germany, $11-14.5 in Canada, $2.5-4 in Brazil and $1-1.8 in India ([Adligator](https://adligator.com/blog/meta-ads-cpm-by-country-benchmarks)). Cheaper reach in Band B and C roughly offsets their thinner margins, but no education cost-per-acquisition data exists for these countries. All figures above are contribution before support, software, homework feedback, salaries and marketing.

## One global platform, regional price books, and the payment card as proof of country

The large players run one global brand with localised sites and per-country prices. That includes Preply, italki, Cambly, Lingoda and Duolingo, which raised prices only in selected markets after A/B tests ([Substack on Duolingo Q1 2025](https://sergeycyw.substack.com/p/duolingo-q1-2025-earnings-analysis)). Open English is the regional-brand exception. It built a Latin America-only brand on founder-fronted local TV ([Refresh Miami](https://refreshmiami.com/qa-with-andres-moreno-miamitech-og-on-our-citys-0-to-1-evolution-and-how-we-can-go-from-1-to-100/)). Alimun should stay global. It already has the `ar de es fr it ma pt zh` locale folders, one Stripe account and one EU OSS registration. Regional brands would multiply entities, tax registrations and SEO properties. What does need to be regional is the cohort calendar. Live classes force time-zone groups anyway, and the band price book can sit behind one brand.

The pricing engine should be built in five layers. Inputs come first: an EUR anchor price for each language family, format and tier, set by owner decision. Teacher rates per language sit in a dated table. Band multipliers are A 1.00, B 0.85 and C 0.75, with private at 0.85 outside A. A floor function sets the minimum net per seat-hour as (teacher rate × 1.03 + video × (fill + 1)) ÷ planned fill ÷ (1 − target margin). With planned fill 4 and a 25% target, the floor is about €4.80 for English and €7.20 for German. The local tax rate per country records whether Alimun collects it. Exchange rates come from the ECB daily feed, plus an aggregator for MAD and COP.

The computation is simple. The non-EU price before tax is the EU VAT-inclusive price × the band multiplier, never below the floor. It is then converted, local tax is added where collected, and the result is rounded to local charm points: whole euros, C$x5/x9, R$x9,90, MAD x9, COP x,900, ₹x99. Stripe can then hold hand-set local prices in `currency_options`, which Adaptive Pricing respects. Adaptive Pricing itself only converts currency, applies no purchasing-power discount, and does not list Argentina ([Stripe Adaptive Pricing](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/currencies/localize-prices/adaptive-pricing.md?payment-ui=stripe-hosted)).

Rebase prices quarterly. Re-round a local price when currency moves push its euro value more than about 7% off the band target (a threshold chosen by judgement), and review all anchors and teacher rates together each January. Morocco's dirham is pegged to a basket and its inflation was under 1% in 2025. Turkey's was 34.9%, Argentina's 41.9% and Egypt's 20.4% ([IMF DataMapper](https://www.imf.org/external/datamapper/api/v1/PCPIPCH/MAR/CAN/BRA/COL/PER/CHL/ARG/ECU/MEX/IND/TUR/EGY/TUN/PHL/ALB/UVK/UZB/PAK/DEU/FRA/ITA/ESP?periods=2024,2025,2026)). So price Turkey and Tunisia in EUR, and Argentina and Egypt in USD. Existing subscribers keep their price for six months after any rebase.

The data model needs eight tables:

| Table | Holds |
|---|---|
| `country_band` | country, band, currency, tax rate, whether tax is collected, valid-from |
| `price_book` | language family, format, tier, band, currency, minor-unit amount, Stripe price ID, valid-from/to |
| `teacher_rate` | language, EUR/h, exam supplement, valid-from |
| `fx_rate` | date, currency, rate |
| `subscription_band` | user, band, card country, IP country at signup, locked-until |
| `band_change_log` | changes to a learner's band |
| `class_economics` | per class: seats sold, net revenue, teacher cost |
| `trial` | trial bookings |

Mixed-band classes are fine. A German B1 evening class can hold Moroccan, Tunisian and EU learners. That is why the confirmation rule should test the class's total net seat revenue against teacher cost, not a fill count alone.

Anti-arbitrage relies on the payment instrument, not the IP address. The page shows prices from the IP country, using the `x-vercel-ip-country` header. Checkout honours a band only if the card's issuing country belongs to that band, or if a local-only method proves residence, such as Pix for Brazil. Otherwise the learner is re-quoted at the card country's band. Stripe Radar can enforce this directly. It can compare `:card_country:` with `:ip_country:`, flag `:is_anonymous_ip:`, and check band metadata written by Alimun. Missing attributes need `is_missing()` for strict rules ([Stripe Radar rules](https://docs.stripe.com/radar/rules/reference)). The band then locks to the subscription. A later card from a higher band moves it to that band at the next renewal, and voluntary band changes are allowed at most every 90 days, copying Steam's three-month store-country rule ([Steam Support](https://help.steampowered.com/faqs/view/2B3F-DAEF-846B-A0E8)). Diaspora learners pay by their card's country, which is simple and defensible. A Moroccan with a French card pays the EU price, and a Canadian abroad pays the Canadian price. The €3 card-on-file trial makes every learner pass this check before taking a seat.

EU law sets one firm limit, and the design keeps inside it. Regulation 2018/302 restricts treating EU customers differently by nationality, residence or location. Stripe's own Radar documentation warns EU businesses against blocking payments from customers in other member states ([Stripe Radar rules](https://docs.stripe.com/radar/rules/reference); [Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/geo-blocking-regulation-what-does-it-mean-for-your-business/)). So the whole EU and EEA gets one VAT-inclusive euro price. Card-country rules apply only to sort non-EU learners into bands, and no EU card is ever blocked or re-priced by member state. Whether live teaching even counts as an "electronically supplied service" under the regulation is unsettled, so do not rely on that argument. Legal confirmation is still worth getting, because the notes summarise secondary sources and the Commission's Q&A page returned a 404.

## Payment rails, free competitors and German teacher costs shape the launch more than price

Morocco's foreign-payment allowance caps total demand per learner. Residents may spend **20,000 MAD a year** on foreign e-commerce, and only with an internationally enabled card. The allowance was raised from 15,000 MAD in 2026 ([Office des Changes IGOC 2026](https://www.oc.gov.ma/sites/default/files/reglementation/pdf/2026-01/IGOC%202026.pdf); [Médias24](https://medias24.com/2026/01/05/operations-de-change-ce-qui-change-dans-la-nouvelle-instruction-1606301/)). A year of English Regular (5,388 MAD) uses 27% of it, and a year of German exam cohort (8,628 MAD) uses 43%, shared with all of the learner's other online spending abroad. Meanwhile 86% of Moroccan card transactions are still cash withdrawals ([EcoActu](https://ecoactu.ma/226-millions-de-cartes-bancaires-en-circulation/)). Expect card friction. Plan a local payment route, such as CMI through a local acquirer or bank transfer for quarterly prepay, if checkout conversion lags. Avoid annual prepayment there.

Brazil now has a native subscription rail. Since April 2026, Stripe accounts outside Brazil can collect recurring **Pix Automático**. Each charge triggers a pre-debit notice and a three-day wait and can take up to seven days to settle. Customers pay 3.5% IOF by default unless the merchant absorbs it, and Pix disputes cannot be contested ([Stripe Pix docs](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/pix); [Stripe changelog](https://docs.stripe.com/changelog/dahlia/2026-04-22/pix-recurring-payments-support)). The subscription logic must therefore tolerate a `processing` state. Absorbing the IOF is worth an A/B test, since Pix already took 42% of Brazilian e-commerce value in 2025 ([Poder360](https://www.poder360.com.br/poder-economia/pix-chega-a-42-dos-pagamentos-on-line-no-brasil/)).

India's rules make renewals fragile. Recurring card charges need an e-mandate and a 24-hour pre-debit notice. Every charge above ₹15,000 needs fresh 3DS ([Stripe RBI FAQ](https://support.stripe.com/questions/rbi-e-mandate-regulations-faqs)). Every Alimun group plan stays below ₹9,000, but a German private 8-pack (about ₹23,000) would not, so sell private only in 4-packs in India. Argentina adds a 30% income-tax prepayment on foreign card spend. It is creditable, and education software is reportedly exempt ([EY](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2025-0160-argentina-eliminates-the-impuesto-pais)). Mexico's OXXO cash vouchers cannot carry a subscription ([Stripe OXXO](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/oxxo)), which argues for prepaid three-month packs there. Egypt's bank card caps eased in 2024-25, and foreign-use fees fell from 5% to 3% ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/egyptians-can-splurge-abroad-again-as-banks-ease-card-curbs-1.2025008)). Vendor data suggests cross-border cards are approved 20-30 points less often than local ones in Mexico and Latin America ([Nuvei](https://www.nuvei.com/posts/evaluating-payment-strategies-for-the-mexican-market-direct-acquiring-versus-cross-border-models)). For Colombia, Peru and Chile, dLocal-type local acquiring (cards 2.99-3.49%, Pix 0.99% on dLocal Go) is the fallback if approvals disappoint ([dLocal Go](https://dlocalgo.com/en/coverage)).

Quebec's free Francisation means Alimun should not sell general French there. It also pays full-time students a weekly allowance, reported at $230 ([vieauqc](https://vieauqc.com/en/guides/provincial/mifi-francisation)). Sell TEF/TCF/TEFaQ prep in Quebec instead, and offer evening hours the free system does not.

Germany-based German teachers cost roughly three times Alimun's rate. The BAMF floor equals €56.31 per 60 minutes. The GEW union argues €54-60 would match salaried pay. The DVV reports that provider reimbursement was cut about 10% in October 2025, so the floor is unlikely to rise soon ([Bundestag 21/3404](https://dserver.bundestag.de/btd/21/034/2103404.pdf); [DVV factsheet](https://www.volkshochschule.de/themen/integration/index.php.media/51893/2026-03-Factsheet-Finanzierungsluecken-bei-Integrations-und-Berufssprachkursen.pdf)). So recruit German teachers who live outside Germany: Goethe-trained teachers in Morocco, Tunisia, Turkey, the Balkans, India and Latin America, for whom €20 is well above local pay. Keep any Germany-based staff to private German, where the price can carry it.

Contracting is a hidden cost. Employer-of-record service runs $599-699 per teacher per month, and misclassification of teachers across the EU and Canada was not researched ([eorhq](https://eorhq.com/guides/oyster-pricing/)). Get advice before the first ten contractors. Two tax questions also need an adviser: whether any member state's education VAT exemption could apply to Alimun, and whether Colombia's and Brazil's digital-services taxes cover live human teaching. If Colombia's 19% does not apply, the same COP price would carry a much wider margin.

## Conclusion

The mission is achievable, but only once "cheap" is anchored to cost rather than to purchasing power. Because teacher pay is a global online market, a fair rate costs Alimun roughly the same in Casablanca as in Cologne. The real price discrimination therefore happens between Band A's VAT-inclusive price and everyone else's tax-exclusive price, not through deep PPP discounts. That reframes the business around one controllable number, average learners per class. Four learners make every market viable, five make it healthy, and three make most of it lose money. Scheduling, cohort design and trial placement are therefore pricing decisions as much as operations.

The less obvious implication concerns where the cost advantage comes from. Alimun can only win against institutes that pay teachers well and run small groups: Alliance Française, the British Council, Lingoda and Berlitz. It cannot win against large-group public or cultural offers: Goethe in India and Morocco, Francisation Québec, and British Council Egypt. The lead products should therefore be the deadline-driven exam tracks, German B1/B2 for Moroccan and Latin American nurses and trainees and TEF/TCF for Canada. There, a fixed weekly cohort and a sub-exam-fee monthly price matter more to the learner than the last euro per hour.
