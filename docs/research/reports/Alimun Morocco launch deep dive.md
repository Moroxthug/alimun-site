# Register for VAT, then sell German first

Alimun can sell to Moroccan learners from Canada without a Moroccan company, but it must do three things before the first Moroccan sale: register on the tax authority's (DGI) platform for foreign digital services and charge **20% Moroccan VAT inside its MAD prices**, rebuild checkout to meet **Law 31-08** (7-day withdrawal with an explicit "start now" consent, a durable confirmation, tax-inclusive MAD prices, renewal reminders), and add a **Law 09-08** layer to sign-up (express consent for transfers to US processors). On payments, keep **Stripe Canada** as the only web route at launch. Teach learners to pay with their bank's reusable virtual card under the 20,000 MAD yearly e-commerce allowance, push upfront packs, invoice companies by bank transfer, and add **dLocal** (Moroccan cards plus Wafacash cash) only once logged declines show it is needed. VAT costs more than the plan assumed: the pricing models set aside 5% for local taxes, but 20% VAT takes one sixth of every tax-inclusive dirham. That cuts about **11.7 points** from every Moroccan margin and turns the founding level packs into near-break-even products. The recommended response is to raise Moroccan English prices about 14% (they still sit far below the British Council) and German prices about 7% (to stay close to Goethe-Institut), while keeping the app at 59 MAD and the trial at 29 MAD. To win learners, lead with German for Ausbildung and nursing candidates outside Rabat and Casablanca, tie every room to a named Goethe exam session, and close two or three recruiter or employer deals before paid advertising. This report is research, not legal or tax advice. Every legal step below should be confirmed by a Moroccan accountant (fiscaliste) and lawyer, and the open questions for them are listed at the end.

A note on the evidence. Statements with a link come from the research notes. Statements marked "inference" are the analyst's reasoning from those sources. Money figures show their arithmetic and use the pricing spec's rate of 11 MAD per euro. Alimun has processed no payments yet, so no Moroccan sale has been made and nothing was ever due on a Moroccan VAT return.

## Moroccan VAT applies from the first dirham, and 59 MAD becomes 49.17 MAD

### The rule and what registration involves

Morocco brought services supplied "de manière dématérialisée" by non-resident companies into its VAT through the 2024 Finance Law, with operations in scope from 1 July 2024 and new obligations under article 115 bis of the tax code ([Deloitte Avocats](https://blog.avocats.deloitte.fr/maroc-principales-dispositions-de-la-loi-de-finances-pour-2024/)). The 2025 Finance Law moved the return from monthly to quarterly and excluded clients who are only occasionally resident in Morocco ([DGI note on the 2025 Finance Law](https://www.finances.gov.ma/Publication/dgi/2024/synthetique-mesures-fiscaleslF2025.pdf)). Decree 2-25-862 made it operational. Since **11 June 2026**, any non-resident with no establishment in Morocco that sells remote digital services to non-VAT-registered Moroccan clients registers through the SIMPL e-service "Taxation on digital services" on tax.gov.ma, obtains a tax identifier (IF), files a quarterly turnover return before the end of the first month of the next quarter, records its VAT payments, and keeps a register of its Moroccan sales ([DGI communiqué, 2026](https://www.tax.gov.ma/wps/wcm/connect/2e558204-7f73-4639-8b97-7b34205192cb/Communiqu%C3%A9+_TVA+num%C3%A9rique++_2026-FR.pdf?MOD=AJPERES&amp=&CACHEID=ROOTWORKSPACE-2e558204-7f73-4639-8b97-7b34205192cb-pUBFEgk)). The register must be kept for **ten years**, and the provider pays without any right to deduct input VAT ([Hespress](https://fr.hespress.com/375570-tva-sur-les-fournitures-transfrontieres-de-services-et-de-biens-incorporels-comment-la-dgi-compte-t-elle-proceder.html)). The rate is the standard **20%**, and the published scope names SaaS subscriptions, app downloads and **online courses** ([Le360](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/netflix-google-meta-airbnb-le-nouveau-dispositif-de-tva-face-au-defi-de-son-application_F6Q6MRBR7BGLBB6UTNKSIYHFWQ/)). Commercial advisers list e-learning and online training platforms as covered, with no education exemption ([We Process](https://we-process.com/article-115-bis-cgi-maroc-tva-prestataires-numeriques-etrangers/); [Upsilon](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/en/vat-digital-services-morocco/)). No source gives a registration threshold, so the rule appears to apply from the first dirham. Instead of registering directly, a non-resident may accredit a tax representative domiciled in Morocco ([Upsilon](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/en/vat-digital-services-morocco/)).

Two points remain uncertain and go to the accountant. First, no Moroccan source separates live, teacher-led online classes from automated e-services, as EU law does. The prudent reading (inference) is that both the app and the live classes are in scope. Second, one adviser lists four indicators, any one of which makes a client Moroccan for this purpose: a Moroccan billing address, a card issued in Morocco, a Moroccan IP address or the +212 dialling code ([Upsilon](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/en/vat-digital-services-morocco/)). That list was not checked against the tax code itself. A press report says the first quarterly return under the new system was due on 2 November 2026 ([Hespress](https://fr.hespress.com/494552-dgi-le-2-novembre-2026-dernier-delai-pour-plusieurs-obligations-fiscales.html)). Alimun had no Moroccan sales, so it had nothing to declare. The practical sequence is to **register before the first Moroccan sale**, then file every quarter from the first quarter with sales. The adviser should confirm when registration must happen, whether nil returns are needed once registered, and how payment from abroad works, since the DGI guide on payment currency and penalties was not read.

Alimun's checkout already reads the card's issuing country before charging, and Vercel supplies the visitor's IP country. Storing both, plus the billing country and phone prefix, on every payment row gives the location evidence the register needs (inference). Business clients are different. A VAT-registered Moroccan company self-assesses the VAT under reverse charge, and a Moroccan payer of services to a non-resident normally withholds **10% income tax** unless a tax treaty says otherwise ([Upsilon](https://www.upsilon-consulting.com/en/vat-digital-services-morocco/); [AAFIR](https://aafir.ma/ras-de-la-tva-applicable-aux-personnes-non-residentes/)). On a company room at €79 per learner a month, a 10% withholding leaves €79 × 0.9 = **€71.10**, unless the Canada–Morocco treaty reduces it. That treaty was not checked.

The app stores take most of this off Alimun's hands for in-app sales. Apple adjusted Moroccan proceeds for the 20% VAT from 27 August 2026 and states that it collects and remits Moroccan tax ([Morocco World News](https://moroccoworldnews.com/2026/08/336622/apple-applies-moroccos-20-vat-across-app-store-proceeds-and-prices); [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/27/apple-announces-app-store-price-changes-in-four-countries/)). No source confirmed that Google Play does the same for Morocco.

### What 20% inside the price does to each Moroccan price

The pricing spec deducts "VAT (or a 5% provision for local taxes outside the EU)" before computing margins ([docs/LAUNCH_PRICING_SPEC.md](../../LAUNCH_PRICING_SPEC.md), section 5). With prices that include tax, 20% VAT equals P × 20/120, or **one sixth of the gross price**. Alimun keeps P ÷ 1.2 = 0.8333 × P, against the 0.95 × P the models assumed. The gap is 0.95 − 0.8333 = **0.1167 × P on every Moroccan sale**.

### Recommended Moroccan prices: English up 14%, German up 7%

Holding the same MAD contribution per learner as the model needs prices about 0.95 × 1.2 = **1.14 times higher**. Applied blindly, that would push German above Goethe-Institut. The constraint is competitive, not arithmetic. Goethe Morocco charged 3,000 MAD for 60 units of 45 minutes, or 45 hours, in 2024-25 ([Goethe flyer](https://goethe.de/resources/files/pdf329/kursflyer_arbeit_final.pdf)). That is 3,000 ÷ 45 = **66.7 MAD per hour**. The British Council charges 3,100 MAD for 30 hours of adult English, or **103 MAD per hour** ([British Council Maroc](https://www.britishcouncil.ma/en/english/adults/classes)). Alimun's English is far below that ceiling, while its German already sits on Goethe's.

The recommendation (inference) is to raise English by about 14% and German by about 7%, rounding to the spec's "…9" rule, and to keep the app, trial, help class and voice minutes unchanged. The app's margin stays near 68%, and its web price should match the store price. MAD rows in the price book are used only for Morocco, so this changes nothing for other Band B countries priced in EUR or USD.

To see what the change buys, split the spec's English level-pack costs into a price-linked part (5% rewards plus about 3% card fees, an estimate) and a fixed part. The fixed part is (0.95 − 0.08 − 0.38) × 669 = 327.81 MAD per learner a month at 8 learners. The model's contribution was 0.38 × 669 = **254.22 MAD**. With VAT and no price change, it falls to 669 × (0.8333 − 0.08) − 327.81 = **176.17 MAD** (26.3%). At 769 MAD it recovers to 769 × 0.7533 − 327.81 = **251.50 MAD** (32.7%). For German at 8 learners the fixed part is 0.51 × 829 = 422.79 MAD. Contribution drops from 298.44 MAD in the model to 201.72 MAD with VAT absorbed, and recovers to 889 × 0.7533 − 422.79 = **246.92 MAD** (27.8%) at the proposed price. German stays thinner than the model. The way to close that gap is fuller rooms: the spec's margins rise about 10 points between 8 and 12 learners. Raising prices further would cost the Goethe comparison. No customer has paid yet, so this is the cheapest moment to change prices.

## Moroccan learners pay by bank virtual card, so make upfront packs the default

### The allowance and the cards

Since 1 January 2026, resident Moroccans have an e-commerce allowance of **20,000 MAD a year** for online purchases from foreign suppliers, up from 15,000 MAD ([Office des Changes](https://www.oc.gov.ma/fr/actualites/publication-de-l-instruction-generale-des-operations-de-change-2026-0); [Le360](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/reglementation-des-changes-dotation-pour-voyages-e-commerce-export-import-ce-qui-change-des-janvier_BPDQLTUSQFE5PNTNKVZO3UMNA4/)). A consultancy summary of the 2026 instruction lists "online training from foreign providers" and SaaS subscriptions as eligible. It says the allowance sits on a nominative international card that the bank caps technically, renews every calendar year and does not carry over ([Upsilon](https://upsilon-consulting.com/commerce-electronique-change-maroc/)). That is a secondary reading; the instruction's own articles were not read. A German learner paying the proposed 889 MAD a month all year spends 889 × 12 = 10,668 MAD, which is **53% of the allowance**. Four upfront packs at 2,499 MAD come to 9,996 MAD, or 50%. It fits, but a learner who also buys other things online abroad can run out late in the year.

The practical card in 2026 is a virtual card generated in the bank's app. Attijariwafa offers one valid up to a year and capped at 50,000 MAD. CIH Virtual runs up to 12 months, with a "supplier card" locked to one merchant at a monthly cap. Bank of Africa's BMCE e-card lasts a day or a month, Société Générale's 1 or 7 days, and Banque Populaire's L'Instant expires within an hour if unused ([Wafir](https://wafir.ma/fr/guide/cartes-virtuelles-paiement-en-ligne-maroc-2026)). 3-D Secure is mandatory ([Wafir](https://wafir.ma/fr/guide/cartes-virtuelles-paiement-en-ligne-maroc-2026)). The same guide warns that merchant anti-fraud rules sometimes reject virtual cards for "expensive training". Many Moroccan cards also carry limits on international online spending ([Kolonell](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/morocco-online-payment-gateway-comparison-2026)).

Recurring charges are where this breaks (inference). The first payment passes 3-D Secure, but the second monthly charge of a 12-week pack fails on any card that has expired (SG, BMCE, BP) or whose cap fits only one charge. That is why upfront packs should be the default offer for Moroccan cards. It also means Alimun needs a learner guide (in French, Arabic and Darija) to creating a reusable card with a monthly cap above the plan price, a reminder before each charge, and a dunning email with a "pay with a new card" link. The pricing spec already plans the decline explanation and the upfront preference ([docs/PRICING_ENGINE_SPEC.md](../../PRICING_ENGINE_SPEC.md), section 13).

## Morocco's consumer law adds four things to Alimun's EU withdrawal build

Law 31-08 applies to anyone offering services to Moroccan consumers electronically. It is a matter of public order, so terms cannot contract out of it, and the supplier answers for performance even when teachers deliver it ([Law 31-08, arts. 26 and 44](https://www.onssa.gov.ma/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Reglementation/C.Reglementation-Connexe/4.%20Divers/LOI.31-08.FR.pdf)). The withdrawal period is **7 days from acceptance of the offer** for services. It becomes **30 days** if the supplier fails to confirm the required information in writing (art. 36). Refunds are due within **15 days** (art. 37). The right is lost for a service that began, with the consumer's agreement, before the 7 days ended, "unless otherwise agreed" (art. 38). Prices must be the global price including VAT and expressed in dirhams (arts. 3 and 5). For a fixed-term contract with tacit renewal, the supplier must remind the consumer in writing, by a means proving receipt, at least one month before the window to refuse renewal. Otherwise the consumer may terminate at any time after renewal without penalty (art. 7). The supplier carries the burden of proving that information was given and consent obtained (art. 34). No amendment from 2024-2026 was found.

Alimun has built the EU version: a two-step online withdrawal function in Settings, an email receipt in the learner's language with date and time, owner notification and admin refunds. Its policy is 14 days, with classes already taken deducted if the learner asked for an early start ([docs/PRICING_ENGINE_SPEC.md](../../PRICING_ENGINE_SPEC.md); [docs/LAUNCH_PRICING_SPEC.md](../../LAUNCH_PRICING_SPEC.md)). That policy is **more generous than Morocco's**. Article 38 allows the parties to agree more, so the simplest course (inference) is to apply the EU policy to Moroccan learners too: one rule, one function, one email. The gaps lie elsewhere.

| Law 31-08 requirement | What Alimun has | What to add |
|---|---|---|
| Express agreement that the service starts before day 7 (art. 38) | Nothing at checkout yet; the new checkout is still being built | A checkbox at checkout for classes and app access: "Start my access now. I understand that I can still withdraw within 14 days and pay only for classes already taken." Store the timestamp and wording as proof (art. 34) |
| Pre-contract information (art. 29) and a recap before ordering, with terms accessible from the home page and expressly accepted (art. 30) | Prices page; Terms page | The supplier's legal name, physical address, phone and email, the minimum duration of each product, how long the price is valid, and the withdrawal right on the order summary, plus a terms-acceptance checkbox |
| Durable-medium confirmation with the art. 29 items, a complaints address, withdrawal conditions and termination conditions (art. 32) | Withdrawal receipt email only | An order-confirmation email (with a PDF copy) carrying all of these. Without it the window becomes 30 days |
| Refund within 15 days (art. 37) | Admin refunds "within 14 days" | Keep the 14-day internal target, which meets both laws |
| Global MAD price including tax (arts. 3, 5) | MAD prices tax-inclusive in the price book | Add "TTC" or "VAT included" next to MAD prices and on receipts |
| Written renewal reminder at least one month ahead for fixed-term contracts (art. 7) | None | A reminder 35 days before renewal of the yearly app plan (569 MAD) and before any pack that rolls into the next pack, sent by email with delivery logged |
| Clear language; ambiguity read in the consumer's favour (art. 9) | Site in nine languages, including Arabic and Darija | Publish the key terms and order summary in Arabic and French |

Monthly plans that continue until cancelled are not squarely within article 7, which covers fixed-term contracts. Sending the same reminder costs nothing and removes the argument (inference). Arabic is required for instructions and labelling under decree 2-12-503 ([WIPO Lex](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/fr/legislation/details/19773)). No source settled whether a French-only online services contract is enough, so publish both. The withdrawal paragraph of the Terms is a locked block, so the owner must approve any edit to it.

## Data protection: Canada and Ireland are fine, US processors need consent

Law 09-08 covers controllers established in Morocco, and foreign controllers that use processing means located in Morocco. The latter must notify the data protection authority (CNDP) of a Moroccan representative. Processing without the required declaration costs **MAD 10,000 to 100,000**, doubled for companies ([Upsilon](https://upsilon-consulting.com/loi-09-08-entreprises-etrangeres-maroc/)). Transfers abroad need no authorisation to countries on the CNDP's list, though they must still be notified. That list includes **Canada, Ireland and the rest of the EU, but not the United States** ([CNDP deliberation 236-2015](https://www.cndp.ma/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/deliberation-n-236-2015-18-12-2015.pdf)). Transfers elsewhere need the person's express consent, a necessity case or a CNDP authorisation, and hosting in a foreign cloud counts as a transfer ([Upsilon](https://upsilon-consulting.com/loi-09-08-entreprises-etrangeres-maroc/)). The CNDP updated its notification procedure in July 2025 ([CNDP](https://www.cndp.ma/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/CNDP_Procedure-de-Notification-des-traitements_20250724.pdf)).

Alimun's database runs in Supabase eu-west-1 (Ireland), which is on the list. Its video, email, hosting and payment processors (Daily.co, Resend, Vercel, Stripe) are US companies, which may store or route data in the US (inference). Whether Alimun "uses means located in Morocco" is arguable. Cookies on Moroccan devices and Moroccan teachers handling learner data could both count, and no CNDP decision on a purely foreign online service was found. The cheapest defensible position (inference) has three parts. Add a Law 09-08 section to the privacy notice (rights of access, rectification and objection, and the transfer destinations). Collect express consent to transfers outside Morocco and the listed countries at Moroccan sign-up. Ask the lawyer whether to appoint a Moroccan representative and file a CNDP declaration now, or once Moroccan teachers handle learner data.

Two further legal points shape the copy. Private education law 06-00 covers language teaching, tutoring and distance teaching ([Law 06-00](https://www.axl.cefan.ulaval.ca/afrique/maroc-loi-06-2000.htm)). Applying it to a foreign online provider is untested, so do not present Alimun as a Moroccan school or as awarding recognised diplomas (inference). If an authorisation were ever required, its number would have to appear in the offer ([Law 31-08, art. 29](https://www.onssa.gov.ma/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Reglementation/C.Reglementation-Connexe/4.%20Divers/LOI.31-08.FR.pdf)). Name exams only as preparation "in the format of" Goethe, telc or IELTS, which matches the site's copy rules.

## Moroccan teachers hit a 30% tax wall at about two German rooms

A Moroccan teacher contracts as an auto-entrepreneur. In 2026 that means 1% tax on services turnover, a **MAD 200,000** yearly cap, and a **30% withholding on turnover above MAD 80,000 a year from a single client**, which the client deducts ([Le Matin](https://lematin.ma/economie/auto-entrepreneur-ce-que-prevoit-le-regime-fiscal-en-2026/361537); [Captain Legal](https://www.captain.legal/ma/blog/auto-entrepreneur-sarl-seuil-80000-dh/)). No source says how this works when the client is a foreign company with no Moroccan presence. A Canadian company has no way to withhold Moroccan tax, so the likely result (inference) is that the teacher declares the excess themselves. Service-export income must come home: the exchange office expects repatriation within **90 days**, and it has treated foreign platform balances as infractions ([Office des Changes](https://www.oc.gov.ma/fr/personnes-morales/rapatriement-du-produit-des-exportations-de-services); [FNH](https://fnh.ma/article/actualite-economique/office-des-changes-le-rapatriement-des-remunerations-doit-etre-realise-dans-un-delai-de-90-jours)). Pay Moroccan teachers in MAD into a Moroccan bank account in their name.

Here is the threshold at the current rate card ([docs/LAUNCH_PRICING_SPEC.md](../../LAUNCH_PRICING_SPEC.md), section 7), with 10 learners present. MAD 80,000 ÷ 11 = €7,273 a year. A German class pays €12 + 10 × €1 = €22 an hour, so the threshold arrives after 7,273 ÷ 22 = 331 hours, or **6.4 hours a week** over 52 weeks: two German level-pack rooms at 3 hours a week each. An English class pays €8 + 10 × €0.75 = €15.50, so the threshold comes after 469 hours, or **9.0 hours a week**, three rooms. The MAD 200,000 cap (€18,182) arrives at 826 German hours, about **15.9 hours a week**, exactly the staffing spec's full load. So in practice, Moroccan German teachers on the spec's 15 hours a week would pay 30% on most of their Alimun income or need another tax regime. The fix lies in staffing and advice, not in withholding. Track each Moroccan teacher's year-to-date billing and send each a yearly statement. Tell teachers about the MAD 80,000 line when they join and point them to an accountant. Spread German rooms across more Moroccan, Tunisian and other teachers, rather than capping hours as a device. Capping hours to dodge the rule would look like the very arrangement the rule targets (inference, consistent with the earlier contracting research).

## German for Ausbildung leads, sold against Goethe's exam calendar

### Segments, ranked by willingness to pay

German for migration is the clearest paid segment. About **2,500 recognition applications** for Moroccan qualifications were filed in Germany in 2025, up 45% from about 1,700 ([Bladi.net citing BIBB](https://www.bladi.net/allemagne-tourne-travailleurs-qualifies-formes-maroc,123995.html)). A Moroccan nurse earns about **500-600 EUR a month**, while Moroccan care trainees in Germany earn 1,524-1,687 EUR in training and about 3,550 EUR once qualified ([Hespress](https://fr.hespress.com/436975-sante-et-soins-les-marocains-en-renfort-dans-les-maisons-de-retraite-allemandes.html)). Ausbildung needs at least **B1**, and university needs B2 or C1 ([Diakonie brochure](https://www.diakonie.de/diakonie_de/user_upload/diakonie.de/PDFs/Publikationen/250916_diakonie_info_MAR_GER_digital_RZ.pdf)). Nurse recruiters ask for B2 ([bghit-nekhdem.com](https://bghit-nekhdem.com/opportunite-de-carriere-devenez-infirmier-en-allemagne-grace-a-nocare/)). Goethe advertises B1 in 9 months and B2 in 13 months in one intensive format ([Goethe flyer](https://goethe.de/resources/files/pdf329/kursflyer__studium_final.pdf)). A 12-week pack is therefore about one CEFR level, and the path from A1 to B1 is three or four packs (inference). For a nurse on 5,500-6,600 MAD a month, the proposed German pack at 889 MAD is 13-16% of income, high but plausible against a three-to-six-fold pay rise (inference).

English demand is broader but poorer. The offshoring sector employed about **148,500** people at the end of 2024, with a target of 270,000 by 2030 ([FNH](https://fnh.ma/article/actualite-economique/services-externalises-maroc)). Entry-level advisers earn **4,000-6,500 MAD a month** ([Jobsquare](https://www.jobsquare.ma/blog/emploi-centre-appel-maroc-2026/)), so the app and open class suit them better than packs, and their employers are the better buyers (inference). An IELTS test costs **2,850 MAD** at British Council centres ([IELTS.org](https://ielts.org/test-centres/british-council-agharass-campus-tanger)), which marks out a smaller exam-pack segment of people who have already committed real money.

### Competitors and the gap they leave

Goethe-Institut teaches only in **Rabat and Casablanca**, in groups capped at 18 that may be cancelled below 12 ([Goethe Marokko](https://www.goethe.de/ins/ma/de/spr.html); [Goethe AGB](https://goethe.de/resources/files/pdf88/AGBDeutsch.pdf)). Its 2026-27 exams cost **2,000-2,500 MAD for B1** and **2,200-2,800 MAD for B2**, with sessions almost monthly from September to July and registration one to two months ahead ([Goethe exam calendar 2026-27](https://www.goethe.de/resources/files/pdf359/pruefungskalender-2026-2727.pdf)). Goethe also runs a fully funded A2 programme for people who cannot pay course fees ([Goethe tender](https://www.goethe.de/resources/files/pdf354/ausschreibung-sprachkurse-jan-2026.pdf)), so part of the market is not a paying market at all. Private German schools charge about **2,800-3,500 MAD per level** of 8-10 weeks by one third-party estimate ([studienkolleg.org](https://www.studienkolleg.org/fr/blog/studienkolleg-guide-etudiants-maroc/)), and they sell through Facebook and WhatsApp rather than published price lists. The gap for Alimun (inference) has four parts: learners in Tangier, Fès, Marrakech, Agadir and smaller cities; rooms of 8-12 rather than 12-18; an app included between classes; and a room schedule that names the Goethe session it prepares for.

### Channels, language and messages

Facebook reaches **22.8M** Moroccans, YouTube **21.6M**, TikTok **16.7M** adults (up 19% in a year) and Instagram **15.1M** (up 21%) ([DataReportal Digital 2026 Morocco](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-morocco)). Brands spent over 800M MAD on influencers in 2025, and TikTok is now the leading platform for it ([Jobsquare](https://www.jobsquare.ma/blog/metiers-salaires-marketing-influence-maroc/)). Influencer marketing is now regulated ([FNH](https://www.fnh.ma/article/actualite-economique/influenceurs-maroc-fin-far-west-numerique)), so paid posts need disclosure. No Morocco-specific CPM was found. Egypt's 2-4 USD is the nearest proxy, and Ramadan raises CPMs by 30-50% ([adigitalboom](https://adigitalboom.com/glossary/cpm)). The earlier pricing research set a ceiling of roughly **€15-30 to win a paying Moroccan learner**, from an estimated lifetime value at three times acquisition cost ([Alimun market and pricing research](Alimun%20market%20and%20pricing%20research.md)). A small test campaign should establish the real cost before any budget is committed.

On language, Moroccan advertising has long used French and Modern Standard Arabic as status markers, with Darija rising in digital and influencer work ([AMU Poznań study](https://pressto.amu.edu.pl/index.php/linpo/article/view/52263); [artboulevard](https://artboulevard.org/items/1420925/)). No study compared their conversion rates. The working split (inference, to test) is French for German, Ausbildung and IELTS landing pages and search, since the visa vocabulary is French and German; Darija for TikTok and Instagram video hooks and WhatsApp replies; and Arabic for formal terms and trust content. The site already has `fr`, `ar` and `ma` folders.

Messages should be outcomes and facts, in sentences, with no AI framing and no invented numbers, per the site's rules. Examples: "Ready for the Goethe B1 session in June, in a room of at most 12, from any city in Morocco." "Three live classes a week with a qualified teacher, and your homework corrected between classes." "The exam fee is paid to Goethe or telc; we prepare you for it." "Pay once for 12 weeks with your bank's virtual card." Trust rests on teacher qualifications and legitimacy, which a Diakonie brochure tells Moroccan candidates to check ([Diakonie brochure](https://www.diakonie.de/diakonie_de/user_upload/diakonie.de/PDFs/Publikationen/250916_diakonie_info_MAR_FRA_digital_RZ.pdf)). Teacher profiles with real credentials therefore do more than any claim.

## Open questions for the Moroccan accountant

Are live, teacher-led online classes "services rendus à distance de manière dématérialisée" under articles 88 and 115 bis, like the app, or can they be treated differently? Must Alimun register before its first Moroccan sale, and once registered, must it file nil returns for quarters without sales? How is the VAT paid from Canada (currency, method, bank details), and what are the penalties for late registration or filing? Is the four-indicator list for Moroccan clients (billing address, Moroccan card, IP, +212) in the tax code itself, and which evidence will the DGI accept when the indicators conflict, for example a Moroccan learner using a foreign card or a Moroccan living abroad? Are Moroccans living abroad who log in from Morocco excluded as "occasionally resident"? Can Stripe Tax calculate and report Moroccan VAT, or should a fixed rate be used? Does Google Play, like Apple, collect Moroccan VAT on in-app subscriptions? For company rooms, does the Canada–Morocco treaty reduce the 10% withholding on service payments, how should invoices word reverse-charge VAT, and should the price be grossed up? Does the 30% single-client withholding on auto-entrepreneur turnover above MAD 80,000 apply when the client is a foreign company, and if so, how does the teacher declare it? Which regime should a Moroccan German teacher working about 15 hours a week use (CPU or a company), and what does it cost them? Does Moroccan VAT paid create any Canadian deduction or credit for Alimun?

## Conclusion

The Moroccan launch is less a payments problem than a pricing one. The card routes exist and cost nothing to switch on. The legal steps are mostly checkout text, emails and one registration. The pricing model, though, quietly assumed Morocco would cost 5% in tax when it costs 16.7% of every gross dirham. Fixed before the first sale, that is a price-book edit. Found after a few hundred founding learners have locked in for up to 18 months, it would mean selling the most important early market at near-zero margin. The asymmetry runs the right way: English has headroom against the British Council, while German is priced against Goethe and must earn its margin through fuller rooms.

The second lesson is that Morocco's binding constraints sit on the supply side and with the learner's bank, not with Alimun. The 20,000 MAD allowance, short-lived virtual cards and the teacher's MAD 80,000 single-client line each push the business toward upfront packs, more teachers on fewer hours, and B2B rooms paid by transfer. Designing for those three from day one costs little. Discovering them through failed renewals and unhappy teachers would cost the first cohort's trust.
