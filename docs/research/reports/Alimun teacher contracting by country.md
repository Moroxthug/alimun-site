# Keep Alimun Canadian, redesign teacher contracts

Alimun should stay a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC), incorporated in the province where the owner lives, and engage teachers as genuine local independent contractors. In each country the teacher holds the local self-employed status: auto-entrepreneur, autónomo, partita IVA, F-skatt and so on. Alimun pays them directly, with no employer-of-record (EOR) except for rare near-full-time staff. The legal risk does not sit in the contract template. It sits in the matching engine. A reliability ranking with loss of priority, a platform-set rate card, standby pay, a cover-pool duty and teachers locked into 8-10 week groups with a mandatory curriculum together look like "direction and control". From 2 December 2026 that pattern triggers the EU Platform Work Directive's presumption of employment. It is also what courts have used to reclassify riders, drivers and teachers in Italy, Spain, Germany, the UK, Ireland, the Netherlands, Australia and New Zealand. A Canadian company with no establishment abroad has almost no withholding, registration or reporting duties for genuine contractors anywhere in the twenty-two countries studied. Contracting can therefore stay close to the 3% payout allowance already in the pricing model, provided the platform is redesigned before the first EU teacher is onboarded. Four zones are ready now: Mexico, Chile, Ecuador and New Zealand. Thirteen can go ahead under stated conditions. Five should wait: the Netherlands (its new presumption of employment below about €38/h makes Alimun's €13-20 rates unworkable), Germany-resident teachers, Qatar, expatriates in Saudi Arabia, and Australia. Two findings change earlier plans. First, Morocco is planned as the main base for German teachers, but its 30% tax on single-client income above MAD 80,000 bites at about 7 hours a week at €20/h, and a 20-hour German teacher passes the auto-entrepreneur ceiling altogether. Second, a Canadian company does not get the Stripe Ireland card fees that the pricing model assumed.

*This is a research synthesis, not legal or tax advice. It is built from secondary sources (law firms, tax guides, vendors), with primary texts where they could be read. Sentences with a linked source are sourced facts. Sentences labelled "inference" are reasoning from those facts. Every route below needs a local lawyer or tax adviser to sign off on the final contract template before Alimun relies on it (see the ranked list of open questions).*

## A Canadian CCPC beats every offshore company while the owner lives in Canada

### The decision: one Canadian corporation, in the owner's home province

A corporation incorporated in Canada is deemed resident there under ITA s.250(4). A foreign corporation is also Canadian-resident when its "central management and control" is exercised in Canada, whatever its articles say, and board meetings held abroad do not change that on their own ([CRA, Residency of a corporation](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/businesses-international-non-resident-taxes/residency-a-corporation.html)). The owner runs Alimun alone from Canada. So an Irish Ltd, an Estonian OÜ or a UAE free-zone company would very likely be Canadian-resident as well. Alimun would then face Canadian tax plus foreign filings and a treaty tie-breaker dispute (inference). A foreign-incorporated company is also not a CCPC. It would lose the small-business rate and pay the general rate of about 26.5% instead of about 11-12% (inference from the CCPC definition; verify with a Canadian accountant). Each offshore option also has its own cost. Ireland requires an EEA-resident director or a €25,000 bond, because residence counts, not citizenship, so the owner's Italian passport does not help. The bond costs about €1,500-2,000 a year ([RBK](https://www.rbk.ie/services/audit-assurance/corporate-compliance-governance/eea-resident-directors); [Open Forest](https://www.openforest.co/ie/ireland-company-incorporation/section-137-bond)). A UAE free-zone company loses the 0% "qualifying income" rate on sales to individuals, because Ministerial Decision 265 of 2023 excludes "transactions with natural persons" ([UAE MoF](https://mof.gov.ae/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/27.10.23-EN-Ministerial-Decision-No-265-of-2023-Regarding-Qualifying-Activities-and-Excluded-Activities.pdf)). Estonia's 0% only lasts while profit stays in the company; distributions are taxed at 22% ([EMTA](https://emta.ee/en/business-client/taxes-and-payment/income-and-social-taxes)). If a foreign company were in fact non-resident, the owner would have to file Form T1134 every year, and penalties apply even when no tax is owed ([CRA T1134 Q&A](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/information-been-moved/foreign-reporting/questions-answers-about-form-t1134.html)).

The province matters less than it seems. The combined small-business rate on the first CAD 500,000 of active business income is 11% in British Columbia ([Manning Elliott](https://manningelliott.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/2025-Corporate-Tax.pdf)). It was 12.2% in Ontario in 2025, with a cut to 11.2% proposed from 1 July 2026 ([PwC](https://www.pwc.com/ca/en/services/tax/budgets/2026/ontario.html)). Quebec was at 12.2%, with a cut to 11.2% announced and extra eligibility conditions ([Raymond James](https://www.raymondjames.ca/victoria/insights/2025/03/27/2025-quebec-budget-highlights)). The spread is about one point. Incorporate where the owner lives, because a company is in practice carried on from there anyway (inference). One provincial detail does matter. If the owner lives in Quebec, the extra 9% Quebec withholding under Regulation 105 and Quebec's QST rules come into play. Regulation 105 itself only catches fees for "services rendered in Canada" ([MLT Aikins](https://www.mltaikins.com/insights/withholding-tax-on-services-provided-in-canada-by-a-non-resident-understanding-regulation-105-of-the-income-tax-act/)). Teachers abroad are therefore paid gross, with no Canadian withholding.

Staying Canadian has a second advantage the original question did not anticipate: Alimun stays a foreign client in every teacher's country. An Italian, Spanish or Swedish payer must withhold tax and handle social contributions. A foreign client with no establishment there generally does not. In Italy, a foreign client is not a *sostituto d'imposta*, so no 20% ritenuta applies ([Fiscozen](https://www.fiscozen.it/guide/ricevuta-senza-partita-iva-2/)). In Spain, there is no 15%/7% IRPF retention and services to a non-EU business fall outside Spanish VAT ([Billeo](https://www.billeo.es/blog/factura-con-retencion-de-irpf-cuando-y-cuanto-7-o-15); [Infoautónomos](https://www.infoautonomos.com/facturas/como-facturar-clientes-extranjeros/)). In Chile, the SII says a boleta issued to a non-domiciled client "no estará afecta a retención por parte del receptor" ([SII](https://www.sii.cl/preguntas_frecuentes/boleta_honorario_electr/001_120_3748.htm)). In Finland, a foreign payer without a permanent establishment is as a rule not obliged to withhold ([Vero](https://vero.fi/syventavat-vero-ohjeet/ohje-hakusivu/48735/ennakonpidatyksen-toimittaminen9/)). An EU company would lose that simplicity in its own country. The EU directive still applies to a Canadian company, because it covers platforms organising work "performed in the Union, irrespective of their place of establishment" ([Directive 2024/2831 art. 1(3)](https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32024L2831/section/chapter-1)).

Selling to consumers from Canada means three things. EU learners pay VAT at their own country's rate, because live online education to consumers has been taxed where the consumer lives since 1 January 2025 ([BDO UK](https://bdo.co.uk/en-gb/insights/tax/vat-and-indirect-taxes/new-vat-rules-for-online-entertainment-culture-and-education-in-2025)). Alimun accounts for it through the non-Union One-Stop Shop, registering in a single EU state ([Your Europe](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/taxation/vat/one-stop-shop/indexamp_hr.htm)). Services to non-residents of Canada are generally zero-rated for GST/HST ([CRA GST/HST Memorandum 4.5.3](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/4-5-3/exports-services-intangible-personal-property.html)). Canadian learners are charged GST/HST, and QST in Quebec, as normal registrant supplies. Stripe fully supports Canada, whereas Morocco is not supported for merchant accounts ([Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/stripe-supported-countries-alternatives)).

### What changes if the owner moves to Italy

Since 2024, Italy treats an individual as resident after more than half the year present, counting partial days. A company is resident if its registered office, place of effective management or principal place of ordinary management is in Italy for most of the tax period ([Agenzia delle Entrate Circular 20/2024](https://agenziaentrate.gov.it/portale/documents/20143/6519685/Circolare_20_del_4_11_2024_Eng.pdf/bdacb2e0-a699-5180-719e-665210459d75)). Once the owner lives in Italy and runs Alimun from there, the Canadian corporation becomes resident in both countries: in Canada by incorporation and in Italy by management. The Canada-Italy treaty tie-breaker, which was not reviewed, then decides (inference). The clean path is to set up an Italian company (srl) before the move, migrate the business into it, and take treaty advice on what happens to the Canadian one.

The cost is real. IRES is 24%, with no reduced rate even for an innovative start-up ([Stripe Italy guide](https://stripe.com/au/resources/more/how-to-set-up-an-innovative-startup-italy)). IRAP comes on top (about 3.9%, unverified). Alimun would also become a *sostituto d'imposta* for Italian teachers. It would withhold 20% on occasional workers and ordinary-regime professionals, but not on forfettario invoices (confirm). It would owe the client's two-thirds share of Gestione Separata contributions for any occasional worker above €5,000 ([Fiscozen](https://www.fiscozen.it/guide/superamento-5000-euro/)). Italy's art. 2 d.lgs. 81/2015, which extends employee protections to collaborations "organised by the client, including through digital platforms" ([Ministero del Lavoro](https://lavoro.gov.it/en/node/13710)), would then bind a domestic principal directly. Teachers in other EU states would invoice under the reverse charge, and Alimun would self-assess Italian VAT unless an education exemption applies (not researched).

The personal upside is the *impatriati* regime. It exempts 50% of qualifying employment or self-employment income, or 60% with a minor child, up to €600,000. It requires three prior years of non-residence and a multi-year stay, which sources give as four or five years ([Assolombarda](https://www.assolombarda.it/servizi/fisco/riforma-fiscale/i-redditi-delle-persone-fisiche/regime-impatriati)). It covers work income, not dividends, so the owner would pay themselves a salary or fee (inference). Verdict: moving to Italy is workable, but it roughly doubles the corporate rate and adds withholding duties. Decide the move first and restructure second, never the other way round.

### What changes if the owner moves to Morocco

Morocco's corporate rate converged to 20% on profit below MAD 100 million from 2026 ([EY](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/morocco-enacts-finance-law-2023-a-review-of-key-tax-measures)). Morocco keeps exchange controls ([Office des Changes](https://www.oc.gov.ma/en/regulation)), and Stripe does not serve Moroccan merchants. A Moroccan company therefore cannot be the selling entity. It would also become the paying client of Moroccan teachers, and clearly liable to collect the 30% withholding on any teacher's billing above MAD 80,000 ([Captain Legal](https://www.captain.legal/ma/blog/auto-entrepreneur-sarl-seuil-80000-dh/)). The trap runs the other way too. A Canadian corporation that the owner manages from Casablanca risks Moroccan residence claims. Morocco's corporate-residence rules and the Canada-Morocco treaty were not researched (inference; counsel needed). Verdict: keep the selling company outside Morocco. If the owner relocates, get Canadian and Moroccan advice before the move on where management will legally sit. Do not assume that a Canadian incorporation certificate is enough. The owner's Canadian immigration status (citizen, permanent resident or neither) is unknown. It decides whether departure tax and re-entry rules apply, so it should be checked first.

Alimun withholds nothing, files nothing locally and pays gross. The exceptions are countries where that route is unlawful or presumed to be employment at Alimun's rates. ### Latin America: invoice-and-go, with Colombia the one to watch

The platform-work laws in the region target physical work. Mexico's 2024 reform, in force since 22 June 2025, covers paid work "que requiere su presencia física" ([KPMG Mexico](https://kpmg.com/mx/es/tendencias/2025/07/flash-reforma-de-trabajo-en-plataformas-digitales.html)). Chile's Ley 21.431 covers services performed "en un territorio geográfico específico" ([ISL](https://www.isl.gob.cl/beneficios/ley-n21-431-para-empresas-y-trabajadores-as-de-plataformas-digitales-de-servicios/)). Colombia's Ley 2466 platform chapter covers delivery workers ([Actualícese](https://actualicese.com/formalizacion-de-trabajadores-en-plataformas-de-reparto-con-la-reforma-laboral-ley-2466-de-2025/)). Remote teaching therefore falls back to each country's general subordination test (inference). Mexico's reform is still a useful map of what its regulators see as platform control: algorithmic assignment, per-task pay set by the platform, recorded hours and mandatory training ([HLC](https://hlc.com/es/publications/labor-reform-for-digital-platforms)).

Paperwork is light everywhere. A Mexican teacher issues a CFDI to the generic foreign RFC, and the foreign client withholds nothing ([SiempreAlDía](https://siemprealdia.co/mexico/fiscal/rfc-generico-para-extranjeros-en-el-cfdi/); [Praxium](https://praxiumconsultores.com/blog/recibo-de-honorarios-a-cliente-extranjero)). Chile is the simplest country in the study, as the SII confirms. An Argentine teacher must issue a factura E. Since January 2025 they no longer have to convert the receipts to pesos, but the funds must reach a foreign-currency account in their own name at a local bank ([BCRA A8417](https://www.bcra.gob.ar/archivos/Pdfs/comytexord/A8417.pdf)). Ley Bases also amended LCT art. 23, so the presumption of employment no longer applies where services are contracted, invoiced and paid under their own rules ([CAMARCO](https://www.camarco.org.ar/2024/10/24/la-reforma-laboral-de-la-ley-de-bases-y-algunos-aspectos-a-considerar-por-las-pymes-de-la-construccion/)). That strengthens proper invoicing.

Colombia is different in tone. Ley 2466 says hiring should be done "prioritariamente" through open-ended employment contracts ([Cronista](https://www.cronista.com/colombia/finanzas-y-economia/ya-rige-la-reforma-laboral-que-ocurrira-con-los-empleados-contratados-a-termino-fijo-en-el-pais)). Independent contractors pay health at 12.5% and pension at 16% on a base of 40% of income through PILA ([UGPP](https://www.ugpp.gov.co/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/ABC-Trabajador-Independiente-Contrato-Prestacion-Servicios.pdf)). That is 0.4 × 28.5% = **11.4% of gross**. Asking for monthly PILA proof costs Alimun nothing and documents independence (inference). Employing anyone in the region is expensive by comparison: Mexico runs about 30-45% on top of salary ([Start-Ops](https://start-ops.com.mx/research/2026-mexico-labor-cost-index/)), before any EOR fee.

### Europe: Italy and Spain are the hard core, the Netherlands is closed, Germany is a trap

**Italy.** Recurring weekly classes are habitual activity, so the teacher needs a partita IVA, normally under the forfettario regime ([Fiscozen](https://www.fiscozen.it/guide/vantaggi-limiti-prestazione-occasionale/)). The prestazione occasionale route is unlawful for this pattern (inference). The forfettario arithmetic shows what the teacher bears. Take an Italian teacher at €16/h for 15 hours a week, which is €12,480 a year. Taxable income is 78% of fees, €9,734. Gestione Separata at 26.07% takes €2,538 ([INPS circ. 27/2025](https://informazionefiscale.it/IMG/pdf/15871_circolare-numero-27-del-30-01-2025.pdf)). Substitute tax is 5% on the €7,196 left, so €360, or 15% (€1,079) after the start-up years. The teacher keeps **76.8% in the first five years and 71.0% afterwards**.

The bigger issue is status. Cassazione 1663/2020 applied art. 2 to Foodinho riders ([Studio Cataldi](https://www.studiocataldi.it/articoli/37188-rider-la-sentenza-della-cassazione.asp)). Italy's full decree transposing the directive received final approval on 28 October 2026 and covers all platform work, though it was not yet in the Gazzetta Ufficiale ([Teamed Italy](https://www.teamed.global/eu-platform-work/italy)). If a teacher were reclassified, the employer would pay about 29-32% contributions plus TFR at 6.91% and a 13th month ([Deel Italy](https://www.deel.com/blog/employer-costs-for-an-employee-in-italy/)).

**Spain.** An autónomo invoices with no Spanish VAT and no IRPF retention. The cuota is €80 a month in the first year ([INEAF](https://www.ineaf.es/tribuna/nuevo-sistema-de-cotizacion-para-autonomos-o-trabajadores-por-cuenta-propia/)) and about €200 at the lowest bracket afterwards ([Alicante Plaza](https://alicanteplaza.es/alicanteplaza/losautonomospagaranen2025cuotasmensualesde200a590eurosdependiendodesusingresos)). For an English teacher earning €845 a month, that is **9.5% in year one and about 24% afterwards**. If Alimun supplies at least 75% of a teacher's income, that teacher is a TRADE. The contract must be written and registered with SEPE, and it carries 18 working days of rest and compensation for unjustified termination ([Taxfix](https://taxfix.com/es-es/autonomos/autonomos-trade/)). The Supreme Court's Glovo judgment (STS 805/2020) reclassified riders who did not negotiate prices and relied on the platform's app as the essential asset ([Conesa Legal](https://www.conesalegal.com/hubfs/S%20TS%2025_9_2020....pdf)). An employee would cost about 30.65% plus accident cover ([Cuatrecasas](https://www.cuatrecasas.com/es/spain/laboral/art/seguridad-social-nuevos-topes-bases-cotizacion-2026)).

**Germany.** Since the Herrenberg judgment (BSG, B 12 R 3/20 R), teachers no longer get lenient treatment. Integration into the school's organisation decides status ([DRV](https://www.deutsche-rentenversicherung.de/DRV/DE/Home/Herrenberg-Urteil/herrenberg-urteil-was-ist-das)). §127 SGB IV protects teaching contracts until 31 December 2027, but only where both parties assumed self-employment and the teacher consents in writing ([DRV transitional rule](https://www.deutsche-rentenversicherung.de/DRV/DE/Home/Herrenberg-Urteil/herrenberg-uebergangsregelung.html)). Even genuine freelance teachers must insure themselves for pension at 18.6%, which is €242 a month on €1,300 of German teaching ([DRV values](https://www.deutsche-rentenversicherung.de/DRV/DE/Experten/Zahlen-und-Fakten/Werte-der-Rentenversicherung/werte-der-rentenversicherung.html?https=1)). Germany-resident German teachers also expect about €56/h under the BAMF floor, almost three times Alimun's €20, as the earlier pricing research showed. Few of them are worth the status risk. The decision is to recruit German teachers who live outside Germany, as the operations plan already says. Take a Germany resident only with a §127 consent and a §7a status procedure on the final contract template. A group assessment is available until 30 June 2027 ([DRV Expertenlexikon](https://www.deutsche-rentenversicherung.de/DRV/DE/Experten/Arbeitgeber-und-Steuerberater/summa-summarum/Lexikon/S/statusfeststellungsverfahren_im_expertenlexikon.html)).

**Netherlands.** The VBAR act was adopted by the Senate on 16 June 2026 and is expected in force by 31 December 2026. It presumes an employment contract below an hourly rate of about €38 ([SRA](https://www.sra.nl/nieuws/259001/2026/06/rechtsvermoeden-arbeidsovereenkomst-bij-uurtarief-tot-38)). The Supreme Court's Deliveroo ruling held that substitution and freedom to refuse do not rule out employment ([NJB](https://www.njb.nl/nieuws/hoge-raad-bezorgers-deliveroo-zijn-geen-zelfstandigen/)). Reaching €38 costs €38 ÷ €13 = 2.92 times the English rate (+192%) and €38 ÷ €20 = 1.9 times the German rate (+90%). No platform redesign fixes a rate-based presumption, so Dutch residents should not be recruited for now (inference).

**UK and Ireland.** The UK's off-payroll rules exclude "wholly overseas organisations with no UK presence" ([Qdos](https://qdoscontractor.com/ir35/off-payroll-working/clarification-regarding-overseas-clients)), so the live risk is "worker" status, with minimum wage and holiday pay. MyTutor shows the conservative fallback: it treats its schools tutors as workers and builds holiday pay into the hourly rate ([MyTutor](https://help.mytutor.co.uk/en/articles/6359349-how-do-i-get-paid)). The UK is outside the EU directive.

Ireland applies the five-question Karshan test, with control at its centre ([Revenue Code of Practice](https://www.revenue.ie/en/self-assessment-and-self-employment/documents/code-of-practice-on-employment-status.pdf)). The Irish minimum wage of €14.15 from January 2026 ([Sage IE](https://www.sage.com/en-ie/blog/minimum-wage-ireland/)) is above the €13 English rate. If an Irish teacher were found to be an employee, every hour would be an underpayment. Flooring Irish residents at €14.15 costs (14.15 − 13) ÷ 13 = **+8.8%** on English and nothing on the other languages.

**Nordics.** Collect the local business registration and the payer duties largely fall away. A Swedish payer does not withhold if the recipient holds F-skatt, and withholds 30% otherwise ([Skatteverket](https://skatteverket.se/foretag/etjansterochblanketter/svarpavanligafragor/ekonomisktarbetsgivarbegrepp.4.18e1b10334ebe8bc80002640.html)). Finland works the same way through its prepayment register ([Vero](https://www.vero.fi/syventavat-vero-ohjeet/ohje-hakusivu/47881/ennakkoperintarekisteroinnin-vaikutukset-yritystoimintaan5/)). Norway makes people paid without being self-employed reportable "fee recipients" ([Skatteetaten](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/business-and-organisation/employer/the-a-melding/the-a-melding-guide/employment/type-of-employment/freelancers-contractors-and-fee-recipients/)), so an ENK is a precondition. Denmark leaves two questions open: whether a foreign client must report B-income, and whether a foreign employer owes ATP and injury insurance of about DKK 15,000 a year per reclassified employee ([BDO Denmark](https://www.bdo.dk/da-dk/faglig-info/depechen/depechen-artikler-2023/fjernarbejde-over-landegraenser-hvad-med-skatten)).

### Morocco and Algeria: lawful and cheap, but Morocco's tax rule reshapes the German plan

A Moroccan teacher registers as an auto-entrepreneur under Law 114-13. Tax is 1% of turnover, services turnover is capped at MAD 200,000 a year, and "formation" is an eligible service ([Beancount](https://beancount.io/fr/blog/2026/09/15/morocco-auto-entrepreneur-revenue-caps-2026-cpu-cnss-crossing-guide)). Since 2023, turnover above MAD 80,000 a year from a single client is taxed at 30% on the excess. The government's stated aim is disguised employment ([Captain Legal](https://www.captain.legal/ma/blog/auto-entrepreneur-sarl-seuil-80000-dh/); [Africa-laws](https://www.africa-laws.org/Morocco's%20New%20Tax%20Regime%20for%20Auto%20Entrepreneurs%202023.php)). No source says how the rule works when the client is a foreign company.

The arithmetic uses the operations plan's rate of MAD 11.0 per euro. The threshold is 80,000 ÷ 11 = €7,273 a year. That is reached after €7,273 ÷ €20 = 364 hours, **about 7 hours a week, for a German teacher**, and after 559 hours, about 10.8 hours a week, for an English teacher. Take a German teacher at 15 hours a week, earning €1,300 a month or €15,600 a year (MAD 171,600). The 30% on MAD 91,600 of excess comes to MAD 27,480, about €2,498 a year, or 16% of income instead of about 1%. At 20 hours a week the teacher bills €1,730 × 12 = €20,760, or MAD 228,360. That breaks the MAD 200,000 auto-entrepreneur cap, and the teacher has to leave the regime. The Moroccan Cour de cassation held in October 2024 that a contract with an individual entrepreneur is not employment absent subordination ([Hespress](https://fr.hespress.com/406876-406876.html)). The status risk is moderate. The tax rule is the real constraint, and capping hours to dodge it would be exactly the device it targets (inference).

Pay into a Moroccan bank account in the teacher's name. Service-export receipts must be repatriated within 90 days, and the Office des Changes has treated foreign platform balances such as PayPal and Payoneer as infractions ([Office des Changes](https://www.oc.gov.ma/fr/personnes-morales/exportations-de-services); [Le360](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/activites-en-ligne-generatrices-de-devises-le-rappel-a-lordre-de-loffice-des-changes-253246/)). Wise supports MAD payouts ([Wise](https://wise.com/help/articles/2571942/what-countries-and-regions-can-i-send-to)).

Algeria's ANAE auto-entrepreneur status charges a 0.5% IFU, with a DZD 10,000 minimum, up to DZD 5 million of turnover ([AlgeriaTech](https://algeriatech.news/algeria-ifu-tax-regime-digital-services-freelance-gig-economy-2026/)). Auto-entrepreneurs may keep 100% of their foreign-currency export receipts ([TSA Algérie](https://www.tsa-algerie.com/du-nouveau-pour-les-autoentrepreneurs-en-algerie/)). Receipts must now be repatriated within 120 days ([Observalgérie](https://observalgerie.com/2026/08/14/economie/algerie-ce-qui-change-pour-les-exportateurs-dans-le-rapatriement-des-devises/)). Wise does not pay out in DZD and PayPal does not receive in Algeria, so SWIFT in euros is the lawful rail, at USD 25-50 per wire ([AlgeriaTech](https://algeriatech.news/freelancing-algeria-remote-work-payment-barriers-legal-2026/)). Crypto ownership became a crime in 2025 under Law 25-10 (same source), so never pay that way.

### The Gulf: permits decide everything

In the UAE, the free MoHRE private-tutor permit covers coaching "either online or in person, to individuals or groups". It is valid for two years and needs an employer no-objection letter ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/ask-us/uae-private-tutor-permit-who-can-apply-and-what-are-the-rules-1.500274031)). A freelance permit is the paid alternative. No teacher at these rates approaches the AED 375,000 VAT threshold or the AED 1 million corporate-tax threshold ([Middle East Briefing](https://www.middleeastbriefing.com/news/uae-criteria-to-determine-natural-persons-subject-to-corporate-tax/)). Whether the tutor permit covers adult learners abroad for a foreign school is unconfirmed, so make the teacher warrant and upload a valid permit.

Saudi Arabia's Freelance Document is reported to be for Saudi nationals only ([Wafeq](https://www.wafeq.com/en/business-hub/for-business/freelance-accounting-in-ksa)). Fines of up to SR 50,000 and jail have been reported for engaging self-employed expatriates ([GPA](https://gpa.net/blogs/middle-east/saudi-arabia-sr50-000-fine-and-jail-time-for-employers-engaging-self-employed-expats)). Qatar has "no universal personal freelance permit", and its Law 9/2026 freelance framework awaits implementing decisions ([Qatar Living](https://www.qatarliving.com/en/article/side-hustles-freelancing-qatar-young-people); [Envoy Global](https://www.envoyglobal.com/news-alert/qatar-establishes-framework-for-freelance-and-part-time-work/)). An EOR does not rescue part-time Gulf teaching economically: Deel lists Saudi EOR from USD 999 a month ([Deel KSA](https://www.deel.com/fr/hiring/employees/saudi-arabia/)).

### Australia and New Zealand: opposite answers

Australia's s15AA, in force since 26 August 2024, looks at the "real substance, practical reality and true nature" of the relationship ([AustLII](https://www5.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/fwa2009114/s15aa.html)). The opt-out only applies above about A$183,100 ([FWO](https://fairwork.gov.au/find-help-for/independent-contractors/whole-of-relationship-test/opting-out-of-the-whole-of-relationship-test)). The sham-contracting defence now requires a *reasonable* belief, and penalties reach the greater of 1,500 penalty units or three times the underpayment ([DEWR](https://dewr.gov.au/download/15948/compliance-and-enforcement-civil-penalties-and-sham-contracting/36514/compliance-and-enforcement-civil-penalties-and-sham-contracting-factsheet/pdf)).

Superannuation is due on contracts paid mainly for labour, even for genuine contractors, and foreign employers owe it for work done in Australia ([BDO Australia](https://www.bdo.com.au/en-au/insights/superannuation/articles/should-you-be-paying-your-contractors-superannuation); [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/international-tax-for-business/in-detail/income/employees-who-work-in-a-foreign-country)). The 12% rate is unverified. The minimum wage is A$26.44/h from July 2026 ([FWO](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/about-us/workplace-laws/annual-wage-review/annual-wage-review-2026)), above the roughly A$22 that €13 buys. That also feeds the "pays less than an employee" limb of the employee-like platform-worker regime. Australia combines a high penalty tail with a time zone that serves none of Alimun's three regions well, so it waits (inference).

New Zealand is the reverse. The Supreme Court found Uber drivers to be employees in November 2025 ([Courts of NZ](https://www.courtsofnz.govt.nz/assets/cases/2025/MR-2025-NZSC-162.pdf)). But the Employment Relations Amendment Act 2026, in force since 21 February 2026, lets an arrangement meeting all of its gateway criteria put the worker beyond challenge as a "specified contractor". The criteria are: a written contractor agreement, freedom to work for others, no required availability or a right to subcontract, no termination for declining work, and a chance to take advice ([McVeagh Fleming](https://www.mcveaghfleming.co.nz/articles/employment-relations-amendment-bill-passes-2026-reforms-explained)). Some sources count four criteria rather than five, so the enacted text should be checked. The redesigned platform below meets these by construction.

## The matching engine, not the contract, decides employment status

Alimun meets all four limbs of the directive's "digital labour platform" definition. It is provided online, at a learner's request, it organises paid work by individuals as an essential component, and it uses automated decision-making ([Directive art. 2(1)(a)](https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32024L2831/section/chapter-1)). Online tutoring is not excluded. Status is "guided primarily by the facts … including the use of automated monitoring systems or automated decision-making systems … irrespective of how the relationship is designated" (art. 4(2)). Where "facts indicating direction and control" are found, employment is presumed and the platform must prove otherwise. For contracts running on 2 December 2026, the presumption covers only the period from that date ([Directive art. 5](https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32024L2831/section/chapter-2)). It does not apply in tax or social-security proceedings unless a member state extends it (art. 5(3)). A governing-law clause naming a Canadian province does not help either. Rome I art. 8 keeps the mandatory protections of the country where the teacher habitually works ([Rome I art. 8](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2008/593/article/8/data.htm)).

Every test in the study comes back to the same features: the directive, Herrenberg, art. 2 d.lgs. 81/2015, Glovo, Karshan, Deliveroo, Uber in the UK and NZ, and s15AA. The table maps each planned feature to its lawful replacement. The replacements are inferences from those tests, not court-tested designs.

| Planned feature | Why it reads as direction and control | Lawful alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Reliability ranking, with loss of priority after late cancellations | A disciplinary sanction applied by an algorithm; an automated decision of "equivalent detriment" under art. 10(5); Uber-style penalty | Offer classes on objective eligibility only (approved language and levels, teacher's own published availability, time zone), then first to accept or rotation. Handle late cancellation of *accepted* work only by contract: that session is unpaid and a pre-agreed liquidated sum (the same for any supplier) covers cover costs. Never penalise declining an offer. Any restriction is decided by a named person, with written reasons. |
| Platform-set rate card (€13-20 plus premiums) | Client-set pay is a core employment indicator (Glovo, Uber NZ) | Publish the rate card as Alimun's *standing offer*. Let each teacher set an asking rate at or above it, and let the existing "money check" decide whether a class can carry that rate. Premiums for hard slots become part of the offer the teacher accepts or declines. Change terms only on notice, for future offers. |
| Pay for learner no-shows | Mild: removes commercial risk | Keep, reframed as a *booked-session fee*: Alimun buys a scheduled session, and its own customers' attendance is Alimun's risk. This is ordinary B2B practice, and the Australia/NZ analysis rates it a minor factor. |
| Standby fee for instant classes | Paying for availability regardless of output looks like wages for working time | Drop the per-hour standby fee. Instant requests go out as open offers. If guaranteed cover is needed, sell it as an optional, separately priced "reserved block" the teacher quotes for and may decline, with no ranking effect. |
| Priority for teachers below their desired hours | Workforce planning toward target hours, as for staff | Drop it, or publish it as a disclosed fairness rotation with no promise of hours. |
| 15% cover pool | An on-call duty is an employment indicator | No duty. Cover offers go out like any other offer. The teacher has a contractual right to send a qualified substitute from the approved pool, whom they arrange. |
| 8-10 week fixed groups | Long integration into Alimun's timetable (Herrenberg pattern) | Each cohort is a separate *course order*: N sessions, dates and a fee per session. The teacher accepts it, may substitute for individual sessions, and may decline renewal. Tell learners they have "the teacher who took this course", not a guaranteed employee. |
| Mandatory curriculum and materials | Instructions on how to work; client owns the essential means (Glovo) | Specify outcomes: CEFR targets, the unit to cover and the assessment. Methods are the teacher's choice and materials are optional resources. Learners still get a consistent course. |
| Lesson monitoring and ratings used for discipline | Performance supervision (Uber) | Learner feedback goes to the teacher and helps learners choose. It is not used to sanction. No recording for evaluation without consent and a lawful basis. |
| Exclusivity, non-competes, Alimun-only branding | Dependence | Express non-exclusivity. Teachers may name themselves to learners as independent teachers and teach elsewhere, except that they may not solicit Alimun's learners during a course they are teaching. |

Some duties apply even when every teacher is genuinely self-employed. Under art. 1(2), the algorithmic-management chapter protects "persons performing platform work in the Union, including those who do not have an employment contract". Five builds follow:

- **Art. 9, written explanation.** A plain-language notice of the decision types, "the main parameters … and the relative importance of those main parameters", and the grounds for restricting an account or refusing payment. It goes to applicants before selection, on day one, before any change, and on request ([Directive ch. III](https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32024L2831/section/chapter-3)). Teachers can also export their ratings and reviews.
- **Art. 7, data limits.** No emotion or sentiment analysis of teachers on video, no reading of private chats, nothing collected while the teacher is offline, and no biometric matching against databases.
- **Art. 10, human oversight.** Trained human overseers who can override the engine, and an impact evaluation at least every two years. Under art. 10(5), every decision to restrict, suspend or terminate an account, "or any other decision of equivalent detriment", is taken by a human being.
- **Art. 11, explanation and review.** A named contact person, written reasons for any suspension or refused payment, and a reply to a review request within two weeks.
- **Art. 8, data protection impact assessment.** A GDPR DPIA of the matching engine, prepared after seeking teachers' views.

Australia's unfair-deactivation regime for employee-like workers points the same way: written criteria, warnings and a right of reply before access is cut ([Marsdens](https://www.marsdens.net.au/about-us/latest-news/gig-economy-workers-protection/)). Build it once, globally.

A contractor platform is worth paying for only in zones where Alimun cannot verify local paperwork itself. ## The pricing model needs five corrections

**Card fees.** The earlier model used Stripe Ireland fees: 1.5% + €0.25 on EEA cards, plus 0.7% Billing and 0.5% Tax. On a Canadian Stripe account, EU cards are foreign cards and euro charges may carry a conversion fee. Stripe Canada's actual rates were not in the notes, so the following is a sensitivity, not a quote. Treat EU cards at the Irish international rate as a proxy: 3.15% + 2% conversion + 0.7% + 0.5% = 6.35% + €0.25.

The EU English Regular plan at €55 then pays €3.74 in fees instead of €1.73. Net revenue falls from €44.10 to €42.09, and the class of four nets €21.05 instead of €22.05. After the €14.46 class cost, the contribution margin falls **from 34% to about 31%**. Every EU margin in the price list drops by roughly three points under this proxy. Holding EUR balances or settling in EUR could avoid part of the conversion fee (unverified). Get Stripe Canada's quote before launch. Do not move the company to Ireland to save it: the residence analysis above shows that would cost more than three points.

**VAT registration.** The euro price still includes VAT at the learner's rate. Only the registration changes, from the Union OSS assumed in the pricing work to the non-Union OSS a Canadian seller uses. Canadian learners pay GST/HST and QST on top as before. The Canadian company now recovers input tax credits as a normal registrant, which the simplified non-resident regime would not allow ([Forvis Mazars](https://www.forvismazars.com/ca/en/insights/latest-news/new-gst-hst-rules-for-digital-economy-businesses)).

**Country floors above the rate card.** Ireland's €14.15 minimum wage adds 8.8% for Irish English teachers. Australian super adds 12% × €13-20 = €1.56-2.40 per hour, about €101 a month for a 15-hour English teacher, which is a reason Australia waits. The Netherlands needs €38/h, which no format in the price list can carry: a German group of 5 at €79 for 8 classes already plans a 45% margin around a €20 teacher. The money check should therefore use each teacher's actual cost per hour (asking rate, floor and super) rather than the language rate. That also makes teacher-set rates workable.

**Teacher-side costs that decide supply.** At the rate card, a Spain-based English teacher loses about 24% of €845 to the cuota after year one, a Germany-based teacher 18.6% to pension, and an Italian forfettario teacher 23-29% to INPS and tax. A Moroccan or Algerian teacher loses about 0.5-1%, until the Moroccan single-client rule bites. This confirms the earlier advice from a legal angle: recruit outside the high-contribution EU states. It also shows a new limit. Morocco's best German teachers cannot work 20 hours a week for Alimun as auto-entrepreneurs, and above about 7 hours a week they pay 30% on the excess. Either Moroccan German teachers move to a company or CPU regime with adviser help (the CPU's services cap is MAD 500,000 according to [Beancount](https://beancount.io/fr/blog/2026/09/15/morocco-auto-entrepreneur-revenue-caps-2026-cpu-cnss-crossing-guide)), or the German timetable spreads across more Moroccan, Tunisian, Turkish and Balkan teachers at fewer hours each. That would raise the "teachers per 1,000 learners" figure from about 25 toward 33.

**The academic coordinator.** The operations plan adds one coordinator per language at about ten teachers. If that person is employed in an EU country or concludes contracts for Alimun, it raises permanent-establishment and employer-registration questions that home-based contractors do not. The OECD's 2025 commentary treats a home office as generally not a permanent establishment below 50% of working time, but it can still be one where a key decision-maker works from it ([PwC Belgium](https://www.pwc.be/en/news-publications/news/2025/will-employees-working-from-home-trigger-a-pe-new-commentaries-from-the-oecd.html)). Keep coordinators as contractors without signing authority, or employ them in Canada (inference).

## Conclusion

The question looked like twenty-two separate legal puzzles. It is really one design decision made once in software. A Canadian company with no foreign establishment already sits in the cheapest legal position available: no withholding, no registrations, no payroll anywhere a teacher holds local self-employed status. Choosing where to incorporate is not what reduces liability. What reduces it is giving teachers real control over which work they take, at what price, with whom as a substitute, and how they teach, while keeping the learner-facing promise of fixed groups through course orders rather than through obligations. The same redesign meets the directive's transparency and human-review duties, New Zealand's gateway test and Australia's deactivation rules. It also turns teacher-set rates into an input the existing money check can already price.

The surprise is that the binding constraints are teacher-side tax rules rather than employment law. They sit in exactly the countries the plan relies on for cheap supply. Morocco's single-client rule and its auto-entrepreneur ceiling cap how many hours one Moroccan German teacher can give Alimun efficiently. EU self-employed contributions take a quarter of a Spanish or Italian teacher's pay. The next decision is therefore not where to incorporate. It is how many teachers, in which countries, each working how many hours. The answer favours a wider pool of part-time teachers across Morocco, Algeria, Latin America and the UK. It also needs one Moroccan tax opinion and one Italian-Spanish employment opinion before the first cohort opens.
