# Engaging remote teachers in Mexico, Colombia, Chile, Argentina and Ecuador (2025-2026)

Researcher notes, compiled 4 Nov 2026. This is not legal advice. Many sources are secondary (law-firm alerts, accounting blogs, news), and the notes say so wherever that matters. I made about 24 tool calls. Some questions could not be closed, and those are listed under Gaps.

## Mexico: platform-work reform, contractor test, freelancer invoicing, employment cost

### Takeaway
Mexico's December 2024 platform-work reform to the Ley Federal del Trabajo (LFT), in force 22 June 2025, applies to platform work that requires the worker's physical presence, such as drivers and couriers. On the sources found, remote online teaching appears to fall outside it, but no source says so expressly. A Mexican teacher can invoice a foreign client as a self-employed person, either under honorarios (actividad profesional) or under RESICO. The invoice is a CFDI addressed to the generic foreign tax ID. A formal employee costs roughly 30-45% on top of salary, according to vendor estimates.

### Cited Findings
- The platform reform was published in the DOF in December 2024 and came into force on 22 June 2025. IMSS published the pilot rules on 24 June 2025, and STPS published its rules on 27 June 2025, effective 1 July 2025. — [KPMG Mexico flash, Jul 2025](https://kpmg.com/mx/es/tendencias/2025/07/flash-reforma-de-trabajo-en-plataformas-digitales.html)
- **Scope:** the reform covers people who work for platforms "bajo una relación laboral remunerada que requiere su presencia física para prestar el servicio", i.e. paid work that requires physical presence. — [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/mx/es/tendencias/2025/07/flash-reforma-de-trabajo-en-plataformas-digitales.html). HLC also says the reform covers work that requires the person's physical presence and is managed through a platform. It amends LFT arts. 49, 50, 127 and 997-B and adds Chapter IX Bis. — [HLC](https://hlc.com/es/publications/labor-reform-for-digital-platforms)
- **Threshold:** workers who earn at least one monthly minimum wage (Mexico City) in net income from platform activity are treated as subordinated platform workers. Workers below the threshold "serán clasificadas como trabajadores independientes". Net income is computed after exclusion factors of 36% for cars, 30% for motorbikes and 12% for non-motorised work. Transitional factors of 60%, 50% and 15% applied in the first months. — [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/mx/es/tendencias/2025/07/flash-reforma-de-trabajo-en-plataformas-digitales.html). HLC adds that the threshold applies "independientemente del tiempo efectivamente trabajado", regardless of time actually worked. — [HLC](https://hlc.com/es/publications/labor-reform-for-digital-platforms)
- **Platform obligations:** a contract registered with the Centro Federal de Conciliación y Registro Laboral, an explanation of the assignment algorithm in an algorithmic-management policy, payment within one week, recording of hours, enrolment in IMSS, training, transparent per-task pay, no registration fees, and a ban on subcontracting or intermediation schemes that hide a direct employment relationship. Users and customers are not employers. — [HLC](https://hlc.com/es/publications/labor-reform-for-digital-platforms); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/mx/es/tendencias/2025/07/flash-reforma-de-trabajo-en-plataformas-digitales.html)
- **IMSS and Infonavit:** platforms must enrol workers above the threshold with IMSS and pay both employer and employee contributions, including occupational risk. They must also pay 5% to Infonavit, bimonthly. Notices are due within 5 days of the following month. — [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/mx/es/tendencias/2025/07/flash-reforma-de-trabajo-en-plataformas-digitales.html)
- The IMSS pilot began on 1 July 2025 and ran for 180 days. In its first semester, 1,052,653 platform workers reached the minimum income needed for coverage. A reform of the Ley del Seguro Social was to follow. — [IMSS bulletin 002/2026](https://www.imss.gob.mx/node/111772); [Holland & Knight, Jul 2025](https://www.hklaw.com/es/insights/publications/2025/07/seguro-social-obligatorio-para-trabajadoras-de-plataformas-digitales)
- **Freelancer invoicing:** an export of services can be invoiced at 0% VAT only if the service appears in the LIVA art. 29 fr. IV list and is used abroad. Invoicing a foreign client does not make the rate 0% automatically. — [Praxium (accounting blog)](https://praxiumconsultores.com/blog/art-29-liva-exportacion-de-servicios-al-0); [SoyConta](https://www.soyconta.com/exportacion-casos-en-los-que-aplica-la-tasa-del-0-para-fines-de-iva/)
- A RESICO taxpayer is not exempt from VAT. They apply art. 29 at 0% when it qualifies and file monthly. — [Praxium](https://praxiumconsultores.com/blog/exportacion-de-servicios-digitales-iva-0-desarrolladores)
- The CFDI for a foreign client uses the generic RFC XEXX010101000 as receiver, with regime 616, use S01 and the client's country of residence. — [SiempreAlDía](https://siemprealdia.co/mexico/fiscal/rfc-generico-para-extranjeros-en-el-cfdi/)
- A foreign client makes no withholdings. The 1.25% RESICO ISR withholding applies only when the payer is a Mexican legal entity. — [Praxium](https://praxiumconsultores.com/blog/recibo-de-honorarios-a-cliente-extranjero); [SiempreAlDía](https://siemprealdia.co/mexico/fiscal/guia-practica-para-la-emision-de-cfdi-en-el-resico/)
- **Employment cost:** vendor guides put the total employer cost at roughly 30-45% above base salary. The main components:
  - IMSS (rate estimates range from about 21% to 31.5% of a capped base);
  - INFONAVIT 5% and SAR 2%;
  - state payroll tax of about 1-4%;
  - aguinaldo of at least 15 days' pay and a 25% vacation premium;
  - PTU of 10% of taxable profit, not of salary.
  
  One worked example at MXN 30,000 a month comes to about 32% on top of salary. — [Start-Ops 2026 calculator](https://start-ops.com.mx/research/2026-mexico-labor-cost-index/); [Howdy 2026 guide](https://howdy.com/blog/mexico-payroll-benefits-compliance-guide-us-startups); [Rivermate](https://www.rivermate.com/guides/mexico). These are marketing pages, so treat the figures as estimates.

### Inferences
- Because the reform is limited to work requiring physical presence, Chapter IX Bis most likely does not apply to remote teachers. The general LFT subordination test (art. 20, "subordinación") still applies, and Alimun's features would be judged against it.
- The reform's list of obligations is a good map of what Mexican authorities treat as platform control: algorithmic assignment, pay set per task, recorded hours, training. A matching engine that ranks teachers and removes priority for cancellations looks much like those features.
- REPSE (the 2021 outsourcing reform) concerns the supply of personnel. It is probably irrelevant when Alimun buys services directly from an individual, but this was not verified.

### Gaps
- No source confirms in so many words whether a foreign platform with no Mexican entity falls under Chapter IX Bis, or whether online-only services are excluded.
- I did not find whether the Ley del Seguro Social follow-up reform had been enacted by Nov 2026.
- I did not verify whether "servicios de enseñanza" qualify for 0% VAT under LIVA art. 29 fr. IV. Education services also have their own VAT exemptions, so a Mexican accountant should check this.
- No official SAT source was found. The RESICO income cap (MXN 3.5M) comes from memory and is unverified.

## Colombia: prestación de servicios, contrato realidad, Ley 2466/2025, social security, withholding

### Takeaway
Ley 2466 of 2025 was signed on 25 June 2025 and is mostly in force from 26 June 2025. Its platform chapter covers digital **delivery** (reparto) workers. It allows either a dependent or an autonomous relationship, but makes the platform pay social security even for independents. The independent contractor pays health and pension on 40% of income through PILA. The usual practice is for the client to ask for proof of payment. No source was found on a foreign client's duties.

### Cited Findings
- Law 2466 was enacted on 25 June 2025. Most provisions took effect on 26 June 2025, and some come in later. — [MSI Global](https://www.msiglobal.org/resource/colombia-enacts-landmark-labor-reform-under-law-2466-of-2025.html)
- The law establishes that hiring "se realizará prioritariamente mediante contrato de trabajo a término indefinido", i.e. preferably on open-ended employment contracts. — [Cronista (CO)](https://www.cronista.com/colombia/finanzas-y-economia/ya-rige-la-reforma-laboral-que-ocurrira-con-los-empleados-contratados-a-termino-fijo-en-el-pais)
- **Platform chapter:** art. 24 defines the "trabajador digital en servicios de reparto". The relationship may be dependent and subordinated, or independent and autonomous. — [Actualícese](https://actualicese.com/formalizacion-de-trabajadores-en-plataformas-de-reparto-con-la-reforma-laboral-ley-2466-de-2025/); [Alpa Auditores](https://alpa-auditores.pages.dev/docs/alpa-auditores-reforma-laboral.pdf)
- Art. 27 requires platforms to pay health, pension and occupational-risk contributions for each courier, even without an employment contract. It also creates a mandatory register of delivery platforms, with quarterly reporting of how many workers they have and in which modality. — [Cronista (CO)](https://www.cronista.com/colombia/finanzas-y-economia/es-oficial-se-aprobo-la-reforma-laboral-y-las-plataformas-digitales-deberan-pagar-seguridad-social-a-sus-domiciliarios)
- As of May 2026 the platform provisions were still not regulated. — [WageIndicator, May 2026](https://wageindicator.org/es-co/ai/que-hacemos/noticias-e-historias/trabajadores-de-aplicacion-colombia-reforma-laboral/)
- **Independent workers' social security:** contributions are calculated on 40% of income. Health is 12.5% and pension 16% of that base. The contractor pays through PILA, a month in arrears. — [UGPP ABC, Jan 2026 (official)](https://www.ugpp.gov.co/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/ABC-Trabajador-Independiente-Contrato-Prestacion-Servicios.pdf); [ColombiaMove 2026](https://colombiamove.com/blog/como-pagar-seguridad-social-independiente-colombia-2026/)
- The independent worker is solely responsible for contributions. There is no general legal duty for a private contracting company to withhold them. Decree 1273/2018 withholding by the contracting party applies to particular cases, such as public entities. Compliance guides still tell clients to ask for proof of payment before paying. — [Buk](https://www.buk.co/blog/como-hacer-el-pago-de-la-seguridad-social); [Decree 1273/2018 text](https://cancilleria.gov.co/normograma/compilacion/docs/decreto_1273_2018.htm)
- **Withholding (retención en la fuente):** legal persons are withholding agents. Natural persons withhold only on payments abroad (ET art. 368-2). For residents, typical rates are 10% or 11% on honorarios and 6% on services. — [Gerencie](https://www.gerencie.com/retencion-fuente-pagos-exterior.html); [ColombiaMove](https://colombiamove.com/blog/es/freelancer-invoicing-colombian-clients-cuenta-de-cobro/)
- **Employment cost:** the employer pays pension of 12% and health of 8.5%, plus occupational risk of 0.348-8.7%. The source is from 2018. — [Ius Laboris (2017/2018, flagged old)](https://iuslaboris.com/insights/comparing-social-security-contributions-rates-across-latin-america/)

### Inferences
- The platform chapter targets delivery, so it most likely does not cover online teaching. Even so, the general contrato realidad doctrine (primacy of reality where personal service, pay and subordination coexist) still applies. I found no source on it in this pass, so verify it. It is the main risk.
- A foreign company with no Colombian presence is probably not a Colombian withholding agent, so the teacher would self-declare the income. No DIAN concept confirming this was found.
- Asking teachers to show monthly proof of PILA payment costs little and is good practice, even if not legally required of a foreign client.

### Gaps
- The exact Ley 2466 text on prestación de servicios and contrato realidad was not retrieved.
- No DIAN source confirmed that foreign payers have no withholding duty.
- The current-year figures for the total employer burden were not found. Missing items include parafiscales (SENA, ICBF, Caja ~9%, with exemptions), prima, cesantías and intereses. They are known from training data only and unverified.

## Chile: Ley 21.431, boleta de honorarios, retention, employment cost

### Takeaway
Ley 21.431 has been in force since 1 Sep 2022. It covers platforms whose services are performed "en un territorio geográfico específico", such as delivery and transport, so online teaching appears to fall outside it. A Chilean teacher can issue an electronic boleta de honorarios to a foreign client using the generic RUT. The foreign client does **not** withhold; the teacher pays the retention (15.25% in 2026) through the monthly PPM.

### Cited Findings
- Ley 21.431 regulates relationships between dependent and independent platform workers and digital-service platform companies. It entered into force on 1 Sep 2022. — [Carey](https://www.carey.cl/api/archivo/entra-en-vigencia-ley-que-regula-el-contrato-de-trabajadores-plataformas-digitales-de-servicios?lang=es)
- A platform company is one that runs a tech system connecting workers with users for services provided "en un territorio geográfico específico", e.g. goods delivery and minor passenger transport. A dependent worker provides personal services "por cuenta ajena". An independent worker "no depende de una entidad empleadora" and has no staff of their own. — [ISL (government)](https://www.isl.gob.cl/beneficios/ley-n21-431-para-empresas-y-trabajadores-as-de-plataformas-digitales-de-servicios/)
- Platform obligations include damage insurance for the worker's tools (minimum 50 UF a year) and safety training for both dependent and independent workers. Meeting these obligations is not an indication of subordination. Independent classification can be challenged in court. — [LexLatin](https://lexlatin.com/opinion/plataformas-digitales-tendran-que-adecuarse-nuevas-disposiciones-laborales-chile)
- **Boleta to a foreign client:** SII allows an electronic boleta de honorarios to a client with no domicile in Chile, using RUT 44.444.446-0. It "no estará afecta a retención por parte del receptor": the issuer pays the tax through its Pago Provisional Mensual. — [SII FAQ (official)](https://www.sii.cl/preguntas_frecuentes/boleta_honorario_electr/001_120_3748.htm)
- **Retention rate:** 15.25% in 2026 under the Ley 21.133 schedule. It rises to 16% in 2027 and 17% in 2028. — [Buk](https://www.buk.cl/novedades/finanzas/retencion-boleta-honorarios-2026); [El Dínamo, Dec 2025](https://www.eldinamo.cl/buen-dato/2025/12/23/trabajas-con-boletas-de-honorarios-sii-aumentara-el-monto-de-retencion-en-2026/). The brief gave 15.25% for 2025. The sources indicate 15.25% for **2026**, and I believe 2025 was 14.5%, but this is unverified.
- Honorarios workers who file the annual Operación Renta are covered by work-accident insurance. The SII collects their mandatory contributions through the annual return (Ley 21.133). — [ISL](https://www.isl.gob.cl/beneficios/ley-n21-431-para-empresas-y-trabajadores-as-de-plataformas-digitales-de-servicios/)
- **Employment cost:** employer contributions were historically low: work-accident insurance of 0.9% plus up to 2.4% extra, and unemployment insurance of 2.4%. — [Ius Laboris (2018, flagged old)](https://iuslaboris.com/insights/comparing-social-security-contributions-rates-across-latin-america/). The 2025 pension reform adds a new employer contribution that started at 1% in Aug 2025 and rises to 8.5% by 2033. — [WTW, Mar 2025](https://www.wtwco.com/en-sa/insights/2025/03/chile-strengthening-social-security-and-increasing-labor-costs)

### Inferences
- The SII procedure makes Chile one of the simplest countries for a foreign client. There is nothing to register and nothing to withhold. The teacher issues a boleta to RUT 44.444.446-0 and pays the PPM.
- Ley 21.431 is tied to a "territorio geográfico específico", so remote teaching is very likely outside it. General Labour Code subordination indicators still apply.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the full text of Ley 21.431, including its foreign-platform applicability and its minimum-pay and disconnection rules for independents.
- No 2025-2026 figure was found for the total employer burden, which would include mandatory gratificación and severance.

## Argentina: monotributo, factura E, Ley Bases, FX rules, employment cost

### Takeaway
An Argentine teacher can invoice Alimun as a monotributista and must issue a factura E for exports of services. Since Jan 2025, individuals no longer have to convert service-export receipts into pesos. They must still bring the funds into a local foreign-currency account, and the rule was broadened in 2026. Ley Bases (27.742, 2024) created the independent worker with up to three independent collaborators, and changed how the presumption of employment in LCT art. 23 works.

### Cited Findings
- Monotributo caps are indexed to CPI and updated every six months. For early 2026, Category A was about ARS 8.99M and Category E about ARS 26.98M of annual turnover. Exceeding the Category K cap means exclusion from the regime. — [Cronista](https://www.cronista.com/economia-politica/monotributo-2026-arca-fijo-hasta-cuanto-se-puede-facturar-en-cada-categoria); [Ámbito](https://www.ambito.com/informacion-general/arca-confirmo-quienes-mantienen-el-monotributo-2026-y-cuales-seran-los-montos-n6227317/amp). A 14.3% update from June 2026 is reported. — [iProfesional](https://www.iprofesional.com/impuestos/456551-arca-confirmo-los-valores-del-monotributo-que-rigen-desde-junio-2026-cuales-son). The figures in different news sources conflict, so check ARCA's tables.
- People working for clients abroad must issue a factura tipo E (export of services), even if the client does not ask for it. — [Cronista](https://www.cronista.com/finanzas-mercados/la-guia-definitiva-para-inscribirte-al-monotributo-sin-pagarle-al-contador-como-anotarse-y-cuanto-hay-que-pagar/)
- **Ley Bases art. 97:** an independent worker may have up to three independent collaborators. It was regulated by Decree 847/24 and ARCA RG 5599/2024. Collaborators may work for other clients, and the art. 23 LCT presumption does not apply to them. — [Ámbito](https://www.ambito.com/novedades-fiscales/trabajador-independiente-colaboradores-lo-que-hay-que-saber-n6206795); [Tributum](https://tributum.news/res-gral-5599-2024-arca-ley-bases-trabajadores-independientes-y-colaboradores-contratantes-simultaneidad-padron-padaic-implementacion/)
- **Ley Bases art. 89** amended LCT art. 23. Under the new wording, the presumption of an employment contract does not apply where services are contracted, invoiced and paid under the rules governing them. — [CAMARCO](https://www.camarco.org.ar/2024/10/24/la-reforma-laboral-de-la-ley-de-bases-y-algunos-aspectos-a-considerar-por-las-pymes-de-la-construccion/); [Cronista column](https://www.cronista.com/columnistas/nueva-reforma-laboral-los-tres-limites-que-enfrenta-y-un-dato-que-el-gobierno-no-contempla). The sources differ on the exact wording, so verify it in the Boletín Oficial.
- **FX:** since Jan 2025, individuals do not have to convert service-export receipts at the official market, but must bring the funds into the country. BCRA Com. "A" 8417 (Apr 2026) extended this to all service concepts. Com. "A" 8481 (Sep 2026) reportedly removed the closed list of concept codes, for individuals only. Funds must reach a foreign-currency account in the person's own name at a local bank within the set deadline. — [BCRA A8417 (official)](https://www.bcra.gob.ar/archivos/Pdfs/comytexord/A8417.pdf); [Abogados.com.ar](https://abogados.com.ar/bcra-flexibiliza-controles-de-cambio/38887); [Contadores en Red](https://contadoresenred.com/exportadores-de-servicios-sin-limite-el-bcra-libera-montos-y-elimina-comisiones-para-personas-humanas/). The sources conflict on whether an annual cap (USD 12k or 36k) still applies.
- **Employment cost:** employer contributions were 21% for services firms (17% for others) plus 6% health and 1.8-8.2% work-injury insurance, uncapped. The source is from 2018. — [Ius Laboris (flagged old)](https://iuslaboris.com/insights/comparing-social-security-contributions-rates-across-latin-america/)

### Inferences
- The cheapest compliant route is a monotributista teacher who issues a factura E and receives USD into a local USD account. Wise or Payoneer then a local USD account is the usual path, but this is unverified. Alimun has no Argentine registration or withholding to handle as a foreign client.
- Art. 23 as amended by Ley Bases makes properly invoiced service contracts more defensible. Courts can still find subordination on the facts.

### Gaps
- I did not find the monthly cuotas per category for services, or how ARCA treats exports within monotributo caps.
- No 2025-2026 figure was found for the employer burden, which would include the aguinaldo (SAC) and severance regime.

## Ecuador: RUC and professional services, RIMPE, IESS voluntary affiliation, invoicing abroad

### Takeaway
An Ecuadorian teacher registers a RUC and invoices the foreign client. Exports of services are taxed at 0% VAT. RIMPE Emprendedor excludes professionals with a third-level degree, which many teachers have. Voluntary IESS affiliation costs 17.60% of declared income, on a minimum base of one SBU (USD 482 in 2026, about USD 84.83 a month).

### Cited Findings
- RIMPE Emprendedor is for annual sales above USD 20,000 and up to USD 300,000. It excludes people providing professional services with a third-level degree, and people in dependent employment. — [Siigo EC](https://www.siigo.com/ec/blog/obligaciones-fiscales/rimpe-emprendedores/). VAT under RIMPE Emprendedor is filed every six months. — [Siigo EC](https://www.siigo.com/ec/rimpe-emprendedores/)
- Services to a client abroad count as exports at 0% VAT. The invoice should say "exportación de servicios" and include the foreign client's ID. — [LookupTax (secondary)](https://lookuptax.com/docs/country/ecuador-vat-guidelines-indirect-tax)
- **IESS voluntary affiliation:** 17.60% of declared income, never below one SBU. The SBU was USD 470 in 2025 (about USD 82.72) and USD 482 in 2026 (about USD 84.83). An active RUC is required. — [El Universo](https://www.eluniverso.com/noticias/economia/salario-basico-unificado-482-aportes-iess-ecuador-2025-nota/); [El Comercio](https://www.elcomercio.com/actualidad/negocios/iess-ofrece-seguro-independientes-afiliacion-aportes/); [Expreso](https://www.expreso.ec/actualidad/economia/afiliacion-voluntaria-al-iess-requisitos-aporte-y-como-hacer-el-tramite-261287.html)
- The Ley Orgánica de Eficiencia Económica y Generación de Empleo (RO 20 Dec 2023) amended RIMPE. A Constitutional Court ruling reportedly found RIMPE unconstitutional for certain taxpayers, but its scope is unclear. — [NMS Law](https://nmslaw.com.ec/blog/2023/12/26/ley-organica-eficiencia-economica-en-ecuador/); [Orbitax](https://orbitax.com/news/country/article/Ecuador-Constitutional-Court-F-51353)
- The employee IESS rate is 9.45%. — [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/country-list/social-security-rate-for-employees)

### Inferences
- Teachers with degrees would be taxed under the general regime as professionals, with a RUC and annual income tax. Voluntary IESS affiliation is optional for them.
- Ecuador uses the USD, so FX risk and conversion friction are minimal.

### Gaps
- I found no source on the Labour Code subordination test or on Ecuadorian platform-work rules.
- The employer IESS rate (commonly 11.15%, plus décimo tercero, décimo cuarto, fondos de reserva and 15% profit sharing) comes from training data only and was not verified.

## Cross-cutting: registration and withholding for a foreign company, EOR pricing, payout rails, platform-design risk

### Takeaway
On the sources found, a foreign company with no local entity has no withholding duty in Chile (the SII says so explicitly) and none in Mexico, where no withholding applies to foreign clients. Colombia's rules are framed around local withholding agents. Social-security duties fall on the self-employed teacher everywhere, except under the delivery and transport platform regimes, which appear not to apply to online teaching. EOR list prices run about USD 499-699 per employee per month, plus local employer costs, against USD 29-99 a month per contractor for contractor-management tools.

### Cited Findings
- **EOR pricing:** Deel and Remote EOR list prices start around USD 599 per employee per month. Oyster's list price is USD 699, or about USD 499 with annual billing. Contractor management runs about USD 29-49 per contractor per month for Deel and Oyster, while Remote is listed at either USD 29 or USD 99 depending on the source. Employer taxes are extra. — [CostBench](https://costbench.com/compare/deel-vs-remote); [CostBench Oyster](https://www.costbench.com/software/hr/oyster/); [Pin.com](https://www.pin.com/blog/deel-pricing/). These are third-party comparison sites and their figures conflict.
- Mexico vendor estimates put direct hiring at about 42-45% above base salary, with an EOR fee on top. — [Gegidze 2026](https://www.gegidze.com/post/how-much-does-an-eor-cost-in-mexico-the-complete-2026-breakdown)
- Chile's SII confirms that a foreign recipient of a boleta does not withhold. — [SII](https://www.sii.cl/preguntas_frecuentes/boleta_honorario_electr/001_120_3748.htm)
- Mexico treats as platform control the following: algorithmic task assignment, per-task pay, recorded hours, mandatory training. Its law requires these to be disclosed and treated as an employment relationship once income reaches one monthly minimum wage, for physical-presence work. — [HLC](https://hlc.com/es/publications/labor-reform-for-digital-platforms); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/mx/es/tendencias/2025/07/flash-reforma-de-trabajo-en-plataformas-digitales.html)
- Chile's law states that providing safety training and insurance is not an indication of subordination. — [LexLatin](https://lexlatin.com/opinion/plataformas-digitales-tendran-que-adecuarse-nuevas-disposiciones-laborales-chile)

### Inferences
These are reasoned from the cited regimes. No single source covers them, and none is legal advice.
- **Features that raise reclassification risk:**
  - penalties for declining or cancelling (losing priority), which reads as disciplinary power;
  - ranking by "reliability", which reads as performance supervision;
  - unilateral rate setting;
  - fixed 8-10 week groups with set times;
  - mandatory curriculum and scripted delivery;
  - pay for no-shows and a standby fee, which look like wages for availability;
  - exclusivity;
  - monitoring of lessons.
- **Lower-risk design:**
  - teachers set or accept availability freely, and no penalty for declining offers;
  - a cancellation consequence written into the contract as an agreed fee or term rather than a "loss of priority" sanction;
  - matching on objective eligibility (level, language, slot) rather than discretionary ranking;
  - no exclusivity, and teachers may work for other schools;
  - curriculum offered as a deliverable standard rather than step-by-step instructions;
  - per-class pricing agreed in a service contract;
  - teachers invoice using their own local tax documents (CFDI, cuenta de cobro or factura, boleta, factura E, RUC invoice);
  - no standby fee, or a standby fee priced as a booked service.
- **Cheapest compliant route per country:**
  - Mexico: a direct service contract, with a CFDI to XEXX010101000.
  - Colombia: a cuenta de cobro or factura, plus a request for PILA proof.
  - Chile: a boleta to RUT 44.444.446-0, with the teacher paying the PPM.
  - Argentina: a monotributo factura E, paid into a USD account.
  - Ecuador: a RUC invoice at 0% VAT.
  
  EOR (about USD 600 a month) is uneconomic at a pay rate of €13-20 an hour unless a teacher works close to full time.
