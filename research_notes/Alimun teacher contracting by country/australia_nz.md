# Australia and New Zealand: engaging remote language teachers as contractors (2025-2026)

Not legal advice. Researched 4 Nov 2026. Some official pages (ATO, Fair Work Ombudsman) returned 403/503 to the fetch tool, so several points rely on search-result summaries of those pages or on law-firm commentary. Those points are flagged. The NZ Supreme Court judgment PDF could not be parsed, so the NZ case points come from law-firm summaries.

## Australia: contractor test, Closing Loopholes and the high-income opt-out

### Takeaway
Since 26 Aug 2024, the Fair Work Act decides employee status on the "real substance, practical reality and true nature" of the whole relationship (s15AA). That reverses the contract-first approach of *Personnel Contracting* and *Jamsek* (2022). Alimun's teachers cannot use the high-income opt-out because they would earn far less than the threshold (A$183,100 in 2025-26, A$190,100 from 1 July 2026).

### Cited Findings
- In 2022 the High Court in *Personnel Contracting* and *Jamsek* held that a comprehensive written contract largely decides the employee/contractor question ("contract is king") — [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2022/03/contract-is-king-high-court-of-australia-provides-clarity-on-employee-vs-contractor-test); [Piper Alderman](https://piperalderman.com.au/insight/contract-rules-in-two-landmark-decisions-the-high-court-has-restored-some-confidence-in-contracting-with-individual-workers/)
- Section 15AA (from 26 Aug 2024) was enacted in response to those cases. It requires the relationship to be assessed on its "real substance, practical reality and true nature", taking the totality of the relationship into account, including how the contract is actually performed — [AustLII s15AA](https://www5.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/fwa2009114/s15aa.html); [No Borders Law Group](https://noborderslawgroup.com.au/articles/evolution-of-contractor-agreements-a-return-to-a-multifactorial-approach/); [FWC decision [2025] FWC 304](https://fwc.gov.au/documents/decisionssigned/pdf/2025fwc304.pdf)
- The commencement date of 26 Aug 2024 is confirmed by [Legal500/Mondaq briefing](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/australia/employment-and-hr/new-minimum-standards-and-protections-for-regulated-workers) and [Jones Day 2024](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2024/12/recent-amendments-to-australia-fwa-impact-whether-independent-contractors-are-employees)
- **Opt-out (s15AB):** an individual contractor earning above the contractor high income threshold can give a written opt-out notice. The "start of relationship" (contract-based) test then applies instead of s15AA. The opt-out is limited to individuals, not workers engaged through a company — [Fair Work Ombudsman: opting out](https://fairwork.gov.au/find-help-for/independent-contractors/whole-of-relationship-test/opting-out-of-the-whole-of-relationship-test); [Russell Kennedy](https://www.russellkennedy.com.au/insights-events/insights/opting-out-of-the-new-definition-of-employee)
- **Threshold amounts:** A$183,100 for 2025-26 and A$190,100 from 1 July 2026, according to search summaries of [Employment Hero](https://employmenthero.com/blog/high-income-threshold/) and [Russell Kennedy](https://www.russellkennedy.com.au/insights-events/insights/increase-to-the-contractor-high-income-threshold-and-superannuation-considerations). I did not verify these against the FWO page itself.
- **Sham contracting (s357):** since 27 Feb 2024 the defence has been narrowed. The business must prove it *reasonably believed* the worker could validly be engaged as a contractor (an objective test). Under the old test the business only had to show it did not know and was not reckless — [DEWR factsheet](https://dewr.gov.au/download/15948/compliance-and-enforcement-civil-penalties-and-sham-contracting/36514/compliance-and-enforcement-civil-penalties-and-sham-contracting-factsheet/pdf)
- **Sham contracting penalties:** for bodies corporate that are not small businesses, the maximum rose to the greater of 1,500 penalty units (A$469,500 at the time of the factsheet) or 3x the underpayment. Serious contraventions carry up to 15,000 penalty units — [DEWR factsheet](https://dewr.gov.au/download/15948/compliance-and-enforcement-civil-penalties-and-sham-contracting/36514/compliance-and-enforcement-civil-penalties-and-sham-contracting-factsheet/pdf). Secondary sites quote indexed figures of A$495,000 per contravention for companies and A$99,000 for individuals — [ausbusinessregister](https://ausbusinessregister.com.au/sham-contracting-penalties-australia/) (unverified; small businesses face lower maxima).

### Inferences
- At €13-20/h, no Alimun teacher comes close to A$183k-190k, so **the opt-out is not available**. Teacher status will be decided on how the relationship works in practice, not on the contract wording.
- Under s15AA the following features point towards employment: Alimun sets pay through a fixed rate card, pays for no-shows (no commercial risk to the teacher), provides the curriculum, materials and platform, assigns a teacher to a group for 8-10 weeks, and ranks or penalises teachers by reliability. A contractor agreement alone will not protect Alimun. The reasonable-belief defence needs documented, objective reasons why contractor status is valid (for example a legal review recorded at the time).

### Gaps
- I could not open the full text of the Fair Work Ombudsman's whole-of-relationship guidance or the 2025 FWC/FCA cases applying s15AA to education or online workers.

## Australia: employee-like workers on digital labour platforms and FWC minimum standards orders

### Takeaway
"Employee-like" workers performing digital labour platform work can receive FWC minimum standards orders and are protected against unfair deactivation. The only order found so far covers on-demand delivery, with nothing for tutoring or education. However, Alimun's matching engine could plausibly fall within the platform definition, so the deactivation rules are the live risk.

### Cited Findings
- **Who is "employee-like":** a worker who performs a significant majority of digital platform work as an individual party to a services contract and has two or more of these characteristics: low bargaining power, pay lower than an employee doing similar work, a low degree of authority over how the work is performed, or another prescribed characteristic — [Legal500/Mondaq](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/australia/employment-and-hr/new-minimum-standards-and-protections-for-regulated-workers); [business.gov.au summary via search](https://business.gov.au/People/Contractors/Sharing-economy)
- **Where the definitions sit:** digital labour platform is s15L, platform operator s15M, employee-like worker s15P and services contract s15H — [Explanatory Statement, Digital Labour Platform Deactivation Code](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2024L01573/asmade/2024-12-05/es/original/pdf)
- **What orders can and cannot do:** minimum standards orders can cover payment terms, working time, insurance, consultation and representation. They cannot change the form of a worker's engagement — [Legal500/Mondaq](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/australia/employment-and-hr/new-minimum-standards-and-protections-for-regulated-workers)
- **Unfair deactivation:** employee-like workers with six months of regular work can challenge an unfair deactivation within 21 days. Deactivation includes changing, suspending or terminating access — [Legal500/Mondaq](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/australia/employment-and-hr/new-minimum-standards-and-protections-for-regulated-workers); [Marsdens](https://www.marsdens.net.au/about-us/latest-news/gig-economy-workers-protection/)
- **First order:** the first minimum standards order is the *Interim On-Demand Delivery Employee-Like Worker Minimum Standards Order*. A search summary says it applies from 17 Aug 2026 — [FWO page (search summary only; the page returned 503 when fetched)](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/find-help-for/regulated-workers/minimum-standards-for-on-demand-delivery-workers); [HCA Mag](https://www.hcamag.com/au/specialisation/employment-law/gig-delivery-riders-get-australias-first-minimum-pay-order/586292). I found no order or application covering tutoring or education platforms.
- The government has consulted on extending FWC minimum-standards powers to further "employee-like forms of work" — [DEWR consultation paper](https://dewr.gov.au/download/15647/extend-powers-fair-work-commission-set-minimum-standards-employee-workers/36520/extend-powers-fwc-include-employee-forms-work/pdf) (I did not read the contents).

### Inferences
- The matching engine offers classes to eligible teachers, gives the job to the first to accept, and ranks teachers by reliability. It resembles a digital labour platform as the term is commonly understood. If it is one, teachers paid below employee rates with low bargaining power would be "employee-like". No order currently sets their pay, but cutting a teacher's access or priority after six months could become an unfair-deactivation claim. Practical design: publish written, objective criteria for losing priority or access, give warnings and a right of reply, and keep records.
- For costs, being "employee-like" is not the same as being an employee. It does not trigger super, leave or award obligations by itself.

### Gaps
- I could not verify the exact wording of s15L/s15M, in particular whether a business that supplies its own classes (rather than connecting third-party clients and workers) is a "digital labour platform operator". The answer depends on the statute text and should be checked.

## Australia: superannuation, ABN/GST, PAYG and payroll tax for a foreign client

### Takeaway
Super is the main cost risk. The ATO's position is that employers outside Australia must still pay super guarantee for work performed in Australia. Under s12(3) of the Superannuation Guarantee (Administration) Act, an individual contractor paid mainly for their labour counts as an "employee" for super purposes, even if they are a genuine contractor under the Fair Work Act. GST and no-ABN withholding are mostly the teacher's matter or do not apply.

### Cited Findings
- **Foreign employers and super:** the ATO says a non-resident employer need not pay super for work done outside Australia, but should pay super guarantee for work done within Australia. Without a certificate of coverage, non-Australian employers must pay super in Australia unless an exemption applies — [ATO: super from your employer (via search summary; the page returned 403 when fetched)](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/super/growing-and-keeping-track-of-your-super/super-from-your-employer); [ATO: employees who work in a foreign country](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/international-tax-for-business/in-detail/income/employees-who-work-in-a-foreign-country)
- What matters is whether the work is physically performed in Australia. There is no day-count threshold — [Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.com.au/insights/client-alerts/ato-passenger-movement-data-matching-tax-and-super-implications-for-employers/); [ATO Community answer (not official)](https://community.ato.gov.au/s/question/a0JRF000002AO4j/p00328403)
- **Contractors paid mainly for labour:** such a contract (more than half its value for the person's own labour, paid for labour rather than for a result) attracts super. Contracting with the worker's company, trust or partnership generally does not — [BDO](https://www.bdo.com.au/en-au/insights/superannuation/articles/should-you-be-paying-your-contractors-superannuation); [LegalVision](https://legalvision.com.au/does-the-superannuation-guarantee-apply-to-contractors-and-subcontractors); [ATO "Am I entitled to super" tool](https://www.ato.gov.au/calculators-and-tools/super-am-i-entitled-to-super)
- **ABN and no-ABN withholding:** a supplier who does not quote an ABN to an Australian business may have 47% withheld. No-ABN withholding takes precedence over foreign-resident withholding — [ATO: withholding if ABN not provided](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/hiring-and-paying-your-workers/payg-withholding/payments-you-need-to-withhold-from/withholding-from-suppliers/withholding-if-abn-not-provided); [ATO: ABN for non-residents](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/international-tax-for-business/foreign-residents-doing-business-in-australia/australian-business-number-abn-for-non-residents)
- **GST:** under s38-190, certain supplies to non-residents who are not in Australia are GST-free. The ATO's draft ruling GSTR 2005/6DC dates from 2005 — [ATO GSTR 2005/6DC](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/pdf/pbr/gstr2005-006dc1.pdf); [Tax Institute submission](https://www.taxinstitute.com.au/content/dam/thetaxinstitute/resources/submissions/2018/Tax%20Institute%20submission%20-%20Supplies%20no%20longer%20GST%20free%20due%20to%201%20October%202016%20amendments.pdf) (older sources, flagged)

### Inferences
- The SG rate of **12% from 1 July 2025** comes from prior knowledge of the legislated schedule and was **not verified in this session**. Payday Super (super paid with each pay rather than quarterly, from 1 July 2026) is also unverified here. Both should be checked on ato.gov.au.
- A teacher living in Australia who is paid per class hour as an individual is very likely "paid mainly for labour", so 12% SG is likely owed even if the teacher is a genuine Fair Work contractor. Lawful ways to reduce this exposure:
  - Simply budget for and pay the 12% to the teacher's chosen fund. On €13-20/h this is roughly €1.56-2.40/h, which is cheap compared with an EOR.
  - Contract only with teachers who operate through their own Pty Ltd or trust (super then generally falls away, but it narrows the pool).
  - Pay a fixed price per course or outcome rather than per hour. This is weaker for Alimun because classes are billed and paid per hour.
- Not paying when SG applies leads to the SG charge (shortfall plus interest plus admin fee, and the amount is not tax-deductible). Enforcement against a foreign company with no Australian assets is practically harder, but the liability exists. This should be treated as compliance, not optional.
- **No-ABN withholding** applies to payments "in the course of an enterprise carried on in Australia". A foreign company with no Australian enterprise is probably outside it (inference, not verified). Asking each teacher for an ABN is still good practice and helps evidence contractor status.
- **GST:** a teacher supplying teaching services to a non-resident company that is not in Australia is likely making GST-free supplies. The teacher would register for GST only above the A$75k turnover threshold (threshold from prior knowledge, not verified this session). Alimun has no GST cost.
- **Payroll tax:** this is state-based and only arises above state annual wage thresholds (around A$1m+). It is not relevant at Alimun's scale (inference, not verified).

### Gaps
- I could not fetch the ATO's own page for foreign employers paying super to contractors, or a ruling confirming that SGAA s12(3) applies to a foreign company's offshore-directed contractor. This should be confirmed with the ATO or an adviser.
- State payroll tax thresholds and the contractor deeming provisions for foreign employers were not researched.
- Permanent establishment risk: no source found. Inference: one teacher working from home for a foreign company, without authority to conclude contracts, is unlikely to create a PE, but this is unverified.

## Australia: the employment alternative's cost (minimum wage, award, EOR)

### Takeaway
If a teacher is employed, the floor from 1 July 2026 is A$26.44/h, or about A$33.05/h for an award-free casual, plus 12% super. On top of that, an EOR charges roughly US$599-699 per employee per month. That fee alone can exceed a part-time teacher's monthly pay.

### Cited Findings
- National Minimum Wage: A$24.95/h from 1 July 2025, raised to **A$26.44/h (A$1,004.90/week)** from the first full pay period on or after 1 July 2026. Modern award rates rose 4.75% — [FWO Annual Wage Review 2026](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/about-us/workplace-laws/annual-wage-review/annual-wage-review-2026); [Hall & Wilcox](https://hallandwilcox.com.au/news/minimum-wage-increase-2026-national-minimum-wage-exceeds-1000-per-week/); [Squire Patton Boggs](https://www.squirepattonboggs.com/insights/publications/2026-annual-wage-review-decision-fwc-increases-the-national-minimum-wage-and-award-rates/)
- An award-free casual gets a 25% loading on top of the minimum wage, about A$33.05/h — [FWO 2026](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/about-us/workplace-laws/annual-wage-review/annual-wage-review-2026) (as summarised in search)
- EOR list prices are about US$599-699 per employee per month (Deel US$599-699, Remote US$599-699, Oyster US$699). Third-party comparison sites conflict with each other — [Costbench Deel vs Remote](https://costbench.com/compare/deel-vs-remote); [Costbench Deel vs Oyster](https://costbench.com/compare/deel-vs-oyster-hr/); [Oyster](https://www.oysterhr.com/library/deel-competitors). Remote has its own Australian entity — [inkl roundup](https://www.inkl.com/news/the-10-best-employer-of-record-eor-services-in-australia)

### Inferences
- €13-20/h is about A$22-34/h, depending on the exchange rate (no FX source cited). The **lower end of the rate card falls below the Australian minimum wage**. That matters if teachers are reclassified, and it also feeds the "pays less than an employee" limb of the employee-like test.
- **Example:** a teacher working 40 h/month at €17 earns €680/month. A US$599-699 EOR fee is roughly 80-95% on top of that pay, before super and leave. EOR only makes sense for teachers working many hours.

### Gaps
- I did not confirm which award would cover an online language teacher employed by a private provider: the Educational Services (Post-Secondary Education) Award, the Teachers award, or award-free. Its rates are therefore unknown.
- No AU-specific quotes from EOR vendors were found.

## New Zealand: employment status, Uber v E Tū (2025) and the 2026 gateway test

### Takeaway
The Supreme Court held on 17 Nov 2025 that Uber drivers are employees, looking past the contracts to the real nature of the relationship. However, the Employment Relations Amendment Act 2026 (in force 21 Feb 2026) adds a statutory "gateway test". A worker in an arrangement that meets all five criteria is a "specified contractor" and cannot challenge their status. This is the cheapest lawful route in NZ, but Alimun's platform design has to meet every criterion.

### Cited Findings
- *Rasier Operations BV v E Tū Inc* [2025] NZSC 162 (17 Nov 2025) unanimously found Uber drivers to be employees under s6 of the Employment Relations Act whenever they were logged on — [Courts of NZ media release](https://www.courtsofnz.govt.nz/assets/cases/2025/MR-2025-NZSC-162.pdf); [Buddle Findlay](https://www.buddlefindlay.com/insights/supreme-court-upholds-the-court-of-appeals-decision-that-uber-drivers-are-employees/)
- The Court found the contracts "merely disguise the true nature" of the relationship. The factors pointing to employment were:
  - Uber controls the terms of service and changes them at will.
  - Uber holds the fare and sets the drivers' share.
  - Drivers have no control over pay rates or customer relationships, because the customers are Uber's.
  - Uber monitors performance.
  - Drivers are integrated into Uber's business and dependent on it.

  Owning the car and choosing hours pointed the other way — [Simpson Grierson](https://www.simpsongrierson.com/insights-news/legal-updates/from-rideshare-to-rights-the-supreme-courts-uber-decision)
- **Gateway test:** the Employment Relations Amendment Bill passed its third reading on 17 Feb 2026 and commenced on 21 Feb 2026. The gateway test is not retrospective — [McVeagh Fleming](https://www.mcveaghfleming.co.nz/articles/employment-relations-amendment-bill-passes-2026-reforms-explained); [Lane Neave](https://www.laneneave.co.nz/news-events/major-reform-to-employment-law-the-employment-relations-amendment-act-2026/); [1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/02/17/govts-controversial-employment-relations-bill-passes-third-reading/)
- **Gateway criteria** (all must be met):
  1. A written agreement records that the worker is an independent contractor.
  2. The worker is free to work for others, except for a brief restriction during specific work.
  3. The worker is not required to be available at particular times, or can subcontract.
  4. The worker cannot be terminated for declining additional work.
  5. The worker had a reasonable opportunity to seek independent advice before signing.

  If any criterion fails, the "real nature" test applies — [McVeagh Fleming](https://www.mcveaghfleming.co.nz/articles/employment-relations-amendment-bill-passes-2026-reforms-explained); [Simpson Grierson](https://www.simpsongrierson.com/insights-news/legal-updates/from-rideshare-to-rights-the-supreme-courts-uber-decision). Some sources describe four criteria rather than five, so the enacted text should be checked — [MinterEllison](https://minterellison.co.nz/insights/employment-relations-amendment-act-is-a-new-era-of)

### Inferences
- Alimun can design for the gateway test:
  1. Use a written contractor agreement.
  2. Include no exclusivity or non-compete clause.
  3. Let teachers set their own availability and refuse any offer. Allow a vetted substitute or subcontractor (another approved Alimun teacher) to cover sessions in a committed 8-10 week group, because the commitment to fixed class times otherwise strains criterion 3.
  4. Never remove or deactivate a teacher for declining offers. "Losing priority" for *late cancellation of accepted work* is arguably different from termination for declining additional work, but any rule that pushes non-accepters off the platform risks failing criterion 4.
  5. Send the contract in advance with a written recommendation to take independent advice and a reasonable period to do so, and keep a record.
- If the gateway is not met, the Uber factors map onto Alimun: it sets pay, owns the learners, provides the curriculum and platform, and monitors reliability. Reclassification risk would then be material.

### Gaps
- I could not read the Supreme Court judgment text (the PDF was not parseable), so the detailed reasoning on ratings and acceptance rates is unverified.
- I did not confirm whether the gateway test applies when the hiring entity is a foreign company. It presumably does, if NZ employment law governs a worker based in NZ.

## New Zealand: tax, ACC, minimum wage, KiwiSaver and EOR

### Takeaway
A self-employed NZ teacher handles their own income tax, ACC CoverPlus levies and GST (their services to an offshore non-resident company are likely zero-rated). Whether a foreign payer has schedular withholding duties is unclear: teaching does not obviously appear on the schedular list, and no source addressed foreign payers. Employment would cost at least NZ$23.95/h plus 3.5% KiwiSaver from 1 April 2026, plus an EOR fee.

### Cited Findings
- Schedular payments are payments for the activities listed on the IR330C form, for example labour-hire contractors, journalists, directors' fees, commission agents and non-resident contractors. The IRD page does not mention teaching — [IRD: about schedular payments](https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/withholding-taxes/schedular-payments/about-schedular-payments-for-contractors)
- The non-resident contractor rules (15% deduction, 20% for a non-resident company without an IR330C) concern **non-resident contractors working in NZ**, not NZ residents paid from abroad — [IRD: paying non-resident contractors](https://www.ird.govt.nz/roles/non-residents/paying-non-resident-contractors); [IRD IS 10/04](https://taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz/interpretation-statements/is1004-non-resident-contractor-schedular-payments)
- **GST:** under s11A(1)(k), services supplied to a non-resident who is outside NZ when the services are performed are zero-rated — [IRD tax policy paper](https://taxpolicy.ird.govt.nz/publications/2013/2013-ip-gst-treatment-immigration/chapter-2-zero-rating-rule). The NZ$60,000 registration threshold is cited in [Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/us/en/vatlive/country-guides/oceania/new-zealand/new-zealand-gst-rates-and-compliance.html) (secondary).
- **ACC (2026/27):**
  - Earners' levy 1.75%, up to NZ$156,641 of earnings — [iPayroll](https://support.ipayroll.co.nz/portal/en/kb/articles/changes-for-the-01-april-2026-31-march-2027-tax-year); [Markhams](https://www.markhams.co.nz/news/1-april-2026-payroll-changes/)
  - Self-employed people also pay a work levy that depends on their Classification Unit (average about NZ$0.69 per NZ$100 in 2026/27), plus a Working Safer levy of NZ$0.08 per NZ$100. The minimum liable income is NZ$50,501 — [calculate.co.nz](https://www.calculate.co.nz/reference/nz-acc-levy-rates.php); [Xero NZ](https://www.xero.com/nz/guides/acc-levies-nz-business/) (secondary)
- **Minimum wage:** NZ$23.50/h in 2025, NZ$23.95/h from 1 April 2026 — [iPayroll](https://support.ipayroll.co.nz/portal/en/kb/articles/changes-for-the-01-april-2026-31-march-2027-tax-year); [PaySauce](https://www.paysauce.com/nz/blog-post/whats-changing-on-1-april-and-what-you-need-to-do)
- **KiwiSaver:** the default employer contribution rose from 3% to 3.5% on 1 April 2026, and rises to 4% from 1 April 2028 — [Markhams](https://www.markhams.co.nz/news/1-april-2026-payroll-changes/); [iPayroll](https://support.ipayroll.co.nz/portal/en/kb/articles/01-april-2026-changes-for-you-and-your-employees)
- EOR pricing is the same global list price of about US$599-699 per employee per month as above — [Costbench](https://costbench.com/compare/deel-vs-remote)

### Inferences
- **Withholding:** a foreign company with no NZ presence paying a NZ-resident teacher for a non-listed activity probably has no schedular withholding obligation. The teacher pays provisional tax themselves. This is an inference that needs IRD or adviser confirmation.
- €13-20/h is roughly NZ$25-39/h, depending on the exchange rate (no FX source cited), so it is at or above the NZ minimum wage. An employed NZ teacher would also be owed holiday pay (8% if casual), sick leave, ACC through the employer and 3.5% KiwiSaver (holiday and sick leave figures not researched this session).

### Gaps
- I did not check the full IR330C activity list, so I cannot confirm teaching is absent.
- No IRD source on foreign payers' PAYE or schedular obligations when they have no NZ presence.
- No source on NZ permanent establishment risk from a single home-based contractor.

## Both countries: platform features that most increase reclassification risk, and lawful design alternatives

### Takeaway
The riskiest features are the ones courts cited against Uber and that s15AA looks at in practice:
- unilateral control of pay and terms
- performance monitoring with sanctions
- the business owning the customers
- integration into the business
- penalties for declining work

In NZ, meeting all five gateway criteria largely neutralises the status risk. In Australia nothing does so for low earners, so the platform should be built to look genuinely like contracting, and Alimun should budget for super.

### Cited Findings
- Factors pointing to employment in *Uber* (NZ): control of terms and pay, performance monitoring, customers belonging to the platform, integration and dependency — [Simpson Grierson](https://www.simpsongrierson.com/insights-news/legal-updates/from-rideshare-to-rights-the-supreme-courts-uber-decision)
- NZ gateway: freedom to work for others, flexible time or a right to subcontract, and no termination for declining work — [McVeagh Fleming](https://www.mcveaghfleming.co.nz/articles/employment-relations-amendment-bill-passes-2026-reforms-explained)
- AU: under s15AA, how the contract is performed in practice decides status. A reasonable-belief defence must be objectively supported — [No Borders Law Group](https://noborderslawgroup.com.au/articles/evolution-of-contractor-agreements-a-return-to-a-multifactorial-approach/); [DEWR](https://dewr.gov.au/download/15948/compliance-and-enforcement-civil-penalties-and-sham-contracting/36514/compliance-and-enforcement-civil-penalties-and-sham-contracting-factsheet/pdf)
- AU employee-like or deactivation exposure attaches to restricting or suspending a worker's platform access — [Marsdens](https://www.marsdens.net.au/about-us/latest-news/gig-economy-workers-protection/)

### Inferences (design alternatives; not legal advice)
Each item names the risky feature and a lawful alternative.
- **Priority ranked by "reliability" and loss of priority for late cancellation:** use transparent, published rules that apply only to cancelling *accepted* work. Never penalise declining offers. Use warnings and an appeal step before restricting access (relevant to AU deactivation and NZ gateway criterion 4).
- **Fixed rate card set unilaterally:** let teachers set or choose a rate within a band, or quote per course. At minimum, change terms only on notice and with consent.
- **"First to accept" and standby fees:** a standby fee paid for being available reads like paying for availability, which is employment-like. Consider dropping it, or paying it per accepted on-call engagement.
- **Teacher locked to a group for 8-10 weeks:** allow approved substitutes or subcontracting for individual sessions.
- **Mandatory curriculum and materials:** frame them as a quality specification of the deliverable rather than a direction on how to teach, and leave teaching methods to the teacher.
- **Exclusivity:** include no exclusivity or non-compete clause, and state expressly that teachers may teach for other schools.
- **Teacher pool:** prefer teachers with an ABN, or NZ self-employed status, who already teach elsewhere.
- **Paying for learner no-shows:** this protects teachers but removes commercial risk from them. It is a minor factor and can stay, framed as a cancellation fee owed by Alimun as the client.
