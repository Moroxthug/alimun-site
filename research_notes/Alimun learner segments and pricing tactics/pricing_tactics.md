# Pricing and packaging tactics for subscription services (edtech and live classes), 2024-2026

Scope note: about 25 tool calls were used. Evidence quality varies a lot. Peer-reviewed or primary sources are marked [primary]. Vendor blogs and secondary write-ups are marked [secondary/vendor]. Older classics are marked [classic, pre-2022]. Anything marked "Inference" is my reasoning, not a sourced fact. Alimun context throughout: live small-group classes (max 6) plus private lessons, monthly subscription at 4/8/12 classes, exam cohorts, private packs, three country price bands, a EUR 3 / 14-day trial. Owner rules: no invented stats, fake counters, fake testimonials, badges/pills; urgency must be true.

## Price anchoring, decoy effect, good-better-best, charm pricing, per-class framing

### Takeaway
Three-tier menus reliably pull choices toward the middle option. The decoy effect is real but rests mostly on lab and classroom evidence, not recent field tests. Per-day or per-unit framing makes recurring prices feel smaller at low amounts but can backfire at larger amounts and can feel manipulative. Charm pricing has a long evidence base, but the newest meta-analysis could only be confirmed second-hand. Showing a per-class price is the honest, comparable unit for Alimun, and the 12-class tier naturally works as the anchor.

### Cited Findings
- Economist subscription example (Ariely) [classic, classroom study with MIT students, not a field test]: web-only $59, print-only $125, web+print $125. With only web-only and the bundle offered, 68% chose web-only. With the dominated "print-only" decoy added, 84% chose the bundle. — [UBC blog summary](https://blogs.ubc.ca/onurkurtic/2015/07/10/the-decoy-effect/); same split reported (16% / 0% / 84%; the bundle falls to 32% without the decoy) — [flexprice glossary](https://flexprice.io/glossary/good-better-best-pricing)
- The revenue uplift attributed to the Economist decoy differs between secondary sources: "approximately 30%" in one, and $8,012 to $11,444 (+43%) in another. — [Decoy effect summaries via search](https://blogs.ubc.ca/onurkurtic/?p=318); [thinkinsights](https://thinkinsights.net/index.php/strategy/decoy-effect). Sources conflict; treat both as illustrative.
- No peer-reviewed 2023-2026 field experiment on decoys in subscription conversion turned up. The results were mostly blogs repeating the Economist example. — search of the decoy literature (see Gaps)
- Extremeness aversion / compromise effect: in a camera-choice experiment, adding a third, higher-priced option raised the middle option's share to about 57%, with each extreme taking about a fifth. [classic, Simonson & Tversky-type study, reported second-hand] — [cience.com](https://www.cience.com/programmable-revenue/issue-12/buyers-reach-for-the-middle/)
- Charm pricing: the left-digit effect means $2.99 is encoded anchored on "2" and so reads as markedly lower than $3.00 (Thomas & Morwitz 2005) [classic]. — [kolenda.io](https://kolenda.io/tactics/charm-prices); a marketing site cites a meta-analysis of about 40k participants (Troll et al., 2023) supporting the effect, but I did not verify the paper. — [kolenda.io](https://kolenda.io/tactics/charm-prices)
- Anderson & Simester field test: dresses sold 16 units at $34, 21 at $39 and 17 at $44. The 9-ending beat both a lower and a higher round price, which suggests a learned "bargain" signal rather than pure left-digit perception [classic]. — [Wikipedia: Psychological pricing](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Odd_price) and search summary
- Per-day framing, "pennies-a-day" (Gourville 1998, JCR) [classic]: restating an aggregate cost as small daily amounts raised compliance across product categories in lab studies. The mechanism is that daily framing brings small everyday expenses to mind as the comparison. — [IDEAS/RePEc abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/v24y1998i4p395-408.html)
- Commonly cited result: 52% agreed to donate when asked for "85 cents per day" versus 30% for "$300 per year" (second-hand; not checked against the original). — [Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/the-science-behind-behavior/201904/the-powerful-influence-pennies-day-price-offers)
- Gourville (2003) found per-day and per-month forms preferred to per-year in cellular, health-club and charity domains. Bambauer-Sachse & Mangold (2009) found per-day framing raises price attractiveness but also raises perceived complexity and a feeling of being manipulated. — [Frontiers in Psychology 2021 review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.532696/pdf)
- Atlas & Bartels (2018, JCR): periodic (per-day) framing backfires for larger amounts. — [Atlas & Bartels 2018 PDF](https://home.uchicago.edu/~bartels/papers/Atlas-Bartels-2018-JCR.pdf)
- DellaVigna & Malmendier (2006, AER) [classic]: gym members on flat monthly plans over $70 attended about 4.3 times a month, paying more than $17 per visit when a 10-visit pass cost $10 per visit. They forgo about $600 on average. This is evidence that people overestimate future usage when choosing a volume. — [NBER w10819](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w10819/w10819.pdf)

### Inferences
- Alimun's 4/8/12 ladder is a natural good-better-best. Showing the per-class price falling with volume (e.g. "EUR X per class at 12 a month") makes 12 the anchor and 8 the likely compromise choice. Price the 8-class tier as the plan most people should pick, and make sure its per-class saving over 4 is clearly visible.
- An honest decoy is fine when it is a real plan someone might want, such as a private pack priced per hour next to the group per-class price. That anchors the group plan as good value without inventing a fake option. Avoid adding a plan that exists only to be dominated, because it reads as manipulation if noticed.
- Per-class framing is the truthful comparison unit for live classes, and comparable to a single private lesson or a language school's hourly rate. Per-day framing ("less than EUR 1 a day") is weaker and riskier at Alimun's price level (Atlas & Bartels) and conflicts with plain-language copy rules. Prefer per class plus the monthly total, both shown.
- DellaVigna & Malmendier imply that learners will over-buy the 12-class tier and then under-attend. That boosts short-term revenue but raises churn once people notice. Unused-class rollover (a limited one, e.g. one month) or a downgrade nudge after low attendance is the honest answer.
- Charm endings (EUR 39 vs EUR 40) are low-risk and legal. Across three country bands, keep the endings consistent within each currency.

### Gaps
- No 2022-2026 randomized field test of decoys in subscription pricing was found. The original Huber, Payne & Puto (1982) asymmetric-dominance paper was not retrieved.
- No Simon-Kucher primary figure on middle-tier share was found. Their newsroom pages returned no tier data.
- Troll et al. (2023) charm-pricing meta-analysis effect size not verified.

## Upsell and cross-sell: next-tier prompts, mid-cycle upgrades, add-ons, attach rates

### Takeaway
I found no reliable published attach rates for add-ons in comparable live-class or tutoring businesses. The strongest edtech evidence is Duolingo's tier and family mix: higher tiers are a small share early on, and family plans grew to almost a quarter of subscribers, with better retention. Usage-limit prompts and proration are standard billing features, but no uplift figures were found.

### Cited Findings
- Duolingo FY2023: family plan bookings grew more than 100% year over year, yet family plan subscribers were about 18% of paid subscribers. — [Duolingo Q4 FY2023 shareholder letter (SEC 8-K)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000156208824000048/q4fy23shareholderletter.htm)
- Duolingo Q4 2024 call: the family plan was 23% of total subscribers and showed higher retention and LTV than individual plans. Max (the top tier) was about 5% of subscriptions. Management called Max monetisation "very early". — [Motley Fool Q4 2024 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2025/02/27/duolingo-duol-q4-2024-earnings-call-transcript/)
- Duolingo Q1 2025: 10.3M paid subscribers (+40% YoY), paid penetration 8.9% of LTM MAUs. — [Duolingo investor static file](https://duolingo.gcs-web.com/static-files/01420520-3377-4985-887b-55ed3c1e4fc5) (per search summary)
- Lingoda's post-Sprint upsell: after a Sprint, customers roll into a "Keep Running" pack (20 classes a month after Super Sprint, 12 a month after regular Sprint) on a monthly recurring subscription, and can cancel the rollover during month two. — [Lingoda Sprint 72a rules, Oct 2026](https://www.lingoda.com/downloads/sprint/72/2026-Sprint-72a-Rules-October-Start-EN.pdf)
- Preply's subscription is described by a reviewer as tied to the language rather than one tutor, transferable between tutors, pausable, with plan changes and billing every 28 days (first-hand review, not official). One review site reports complaints about unknowingly being signed up to a subscription. — [languageadvisor.net](https://languageadvisor.net/learning-a-language-online-my-experience-with-preply/); [matetranslate blog](https://blog.matetranslate.com/preply-pricing-decoded-navigating-language-learning-costs-and-alternatives/)
- Paddle's Retain "Term Optimization" moves monthly customers to quarterly or annual and computes a discount per customer. Paddle's docs say annual plans "drastically reduce churn" but give no magnitude. — [Paddle Retain term optimization](https://www.paddle.com/help/profitwell-metrics/retain/get-started/retain-term-optimization)

### Inferences
- Honest next-tier prompts for Alimun: when a learner has used all their classes before the cycle ends, show the actual next-tier price and the per-class difference. Offer "upgrade now, prorated" (Stripe supports proration natively) or a one-off extra class at the single-class price. The prompt is truthful because it is triggered by real usage.
- Add-on candidates in rough order of fit, based on Inference and not attach data: extra single group class; 1:1 session at pack price; exam mock test with marked feedback (fits exam cohorts); certificate of attendance (low cost, useful to employer-funded learners); conversation club (cheap to deliver in groups of 6); recordings (check consent and privacy for small groups). Vocabulary packs are the weakest fit for a live-class brand.
- Lingoda's "Keep Running" shows a cohort or intensive that converts into a smaller ongoing plan. Alimun exam cohorts could end with a default offer of the 8-class plan, cancellable, clearly disclosed at sign-up.
- A family or household plan works for Duolingo (23% of subscribers, better retention). For Alimun it is better framed as "bring a friend or family member into the same group", which also fills seats.

### Gaps
- No published add-on attach rates for tutoring or live-class businesses (Lingoda, Preply, italki, Cambly, Berlitz) were found.
- No figures on usage-limit upgrade prompts or proration uplift were found.

## Urgency and scarcity done truthfully: seat counts, cohort dates, founding-member locks, price-increase notices

### Takeaway
Scarcity messages work best for perishable, time-bound inventory, and a seat in a live class starting on a fixed date is exactly that. UK and EU regulators are actively prosecuting false urgency: countdown timers that reset and "running out" claims that are not true. Real seat counts and real enrolment deadlines are both effective and legal. Fabricated ones carry fines of up to 10% of global turnover under the UK DMCC Act.

### Cited Findings
- Park, Rabinovich & Yin (ASU): "only X left" scarcity messages work best for time-sensitive, perishable goods (hotel rooms, plane tickets) and unique items. — [ASU W. P. Carey summary](https://news.wpcarey.asu.edu/20200327-%E2%80%98only-2-left-stock-order-now%E2%80%99-does-it-work); [working paper PDF](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Park-et-al_2019_SSRN-id3400431.pdf) [2019-2020]
- Li, Tsekouras & Cheng (ICIS 2018) ran a randomized field experiment on scarcity and popularity information in a retailer's free-shipping promotion. Effect sizes were not available in the excerpt. — [AIS eLibrary](https://aisel.aisnet.org/icis2018/economics/Presentations/12)
- UK law: false claims of time scarcity are blacklisted in Schedule 1 of the Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008. The test is whether the deadline reflected reality. — [Travers Smith on CMA v Emma](https://www.traverssmith.com/knowledge/knowledge-container/online-sales-do-countdown-timers-break-consumer-law-cma-investigates-emma-sleep/)
- The CMA took legal action against Emma Sleep (Oct 2024) over countdown clocks and incorrect "discount ending" claims. — [Hausfeld](https://www.hausfeld.com/what-we-think/perspectives-blogs/a-wake-up-call-for-online-retailers-as-cma-tackles-misleading-choice-architecture)
- CMA on Wowcher (July 2024): "Running out!" and "In high demand!" labels and daily countdowns may create false urgency. Most products stayed available at a similar price after the timer ended. — [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/en/insights/2024/07/19/cma-issues-update-in-wowcher-dark-patterns-investigation-102jdyy)
- The CMA's open letter covers countdown timers, scarcity and "act fast" messages, and price-reduction claims, with examples unlikely to comply. — [GOV.UK online choice architecture collection](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/online-choice-architecture)
- DMCC Act 2024: the CMA can fine up to 10% of global turnover. — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/cancel-culture-uk-subscription-contracts-regime-takes-shape)
- EU Omnibus (Art. 6a, Price Indication Directive, in force 28 May 2022): any announced price reduction must show the lowest price applied in at least the prior 30 days. Products on the market for under 30 days may use a shorter period where member states allow. — [RPC on Commission guidance](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/consumer/spring-2022/european-commission-publishes-guidance-on-price-promotions-under-the-omnibus-directive/); [Sellforte](https://sellforte.com/blog/omnibus-directive-how-does-it-impact-your-business)
- Lingoda Sprint uses fixed start dates and fixed months (e.g. Month 1: 20 Oct to 18 Nov 2026; Month 2: 19 Nov to 18 Dec 2026). Deadlines are real because the programme is date-bound, and new Sprints run regularly (Sprint numbers 71a, 71b, 72a, 72b in 2026). — [Lingoda Sprint 72a rules](https://www.lingoda.com/downloads/sprint/72/2026-Sprint-72a-Rules-October-Start-EN.pdf); [search listing of 2026 Sprint rule files](https://www.lingoda.com/downloads/sprint/71/2026-Sprint-71a-Rules-July-Start-EN.pdf)
- Deadlines in education: a randomized deadline in Israeli vocational colleges had no effect on project completion or graduation. A SIGCSE 2024 study found well-chosen deadline policies improved timeliness and pass rates in a self-paced online course. Both concern learning behaviour, not purchase conversion. — [IUCC record](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/do-deadlines-affect-project-completion-experimental-evidence-from/); [SIGCSE 2024](https://sigcse2024.sigcse.org/details/sigcse-ts-2024-Papers-1/157/Effect-of-Deadlines-on-Student-Submission-Timelines-and-Success-in-a-Fully-Online-Sel)

### Inferences
- Alimun's seats are genuinely perishable: a group of 6 that starts on a date. Truthful urgency options:
  - Live seat count pulled from the database ("3 of 6 seats taken in the Tuesday 19:00 B2 group"). The owner bans pills/badges, so put it in a sentence, not a chip.
  - Fixed exam-cohort start dates with a real enrolment close (e.g. the cohort is full or enrolment closes when the first class is held).
  - Founding-member price lock: a written commitment that the price stays the same for as long as the subscription is active, offered until a stated date or member number, then honoured. It is truthful only if Alimun will actually raise prices for later joiners.
  - Price-increase announcement: give a real date and a real new price, and grandfather existing subscribers (see below).
- Never use resetting timers, "X people are looking", or "almost full" without the number behind it. These are exactly what the CMA cases target, and they also break the owner's rule against fake counters.
- Under Omnibus, any "was EUR X, now EUR Y" intro offer in the EU must use the lowest price of the prior 30 days. Since Alimun is launching, "introductory price for the first 3 months, then EUR X" is clearer and lower-risk than a strike-through.

### Gaps
- No quantified conversion lift from honest seat counts or cohort deadlines in edtech was found. Taggstar and Omniconvert claims are vendor material and unverified.
- The outcome of the CMA v Emma case was not found.
- EU guidance specifically on introductory and launch offers under Art. 6a was not retrieved.

## Commitment and retention: prepay discounts, pause, downgrade, cancel flows, win-back, deposits and cashback, streaks

### Takeaway
Prepay discounts cluster at about 16.7% ("two months free"). Pause options in cancel flows save roughly 1 in 10 would-be cancellers, per vendor data. Lingoda's Sprint, which refunds 50-100% of fees if every required class is attended, is the clearest edtech commitment device. Behavioural research supports self-funded commitment contracts, but also shows that people systematically overestimate their future attendance.

### Cited Findings
- Recurly subscription data: the most common annual discount is 16.7% (two months free), followed by 20%, 10% and 8.3%. Lower-priced plans (under $10) offer the biggest annual discounts. — [Recurly SaaS benchmarks for subscription plans](https://recurly.com/research/saas-benchmarks-for-subscription-plans)
- ProfitWell analysis (cited second-hand): typical annual-prepay discount range 10-20%, averaging about 16%. — [Monetizely](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/should-you-offer-discounts-for-annual-commitments-a-strategic-guide-for-saas-leaders) [secondary]
- ProfitWell's 2018 discounting study (6,000+ companies, 600k consumers) warns that unnecessary discounts hurt retention. Numbers were not retrieved, and the page is marked possibly out of date. — [Paddle Studios](https://paddle.com/studios/shows/profitwell-report/discounting-benchmarks) [classic]
- DellaVigna & Malmendier: monthly-contract gym members were 17% more likely to stay enrolled beyond one year than annual-contract members. — [NBER w10819](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w10819/w10819.pdf) [classic]. Inference: annual lock-in does not automatically mean longer retention.
- Pause in cancel flows: an average 9.6% save rate across 1,700+ merchants that added a pause option. The vendor is unnamed, so this is vendor-reported. — [eCommerce Fastlane](https://ecommercefastlane.com/reduce-your-cancellations-by-10-with-pause-subscriptions/)
- Recurly docs recommend surfacing pause in the cancel flow. The average paused subscriber takes about 80 days to decide whether to return or cancel. — [Recurly docs](https://docs.recurly.com/recurly-subscriptions/docs/navigate-retain-pause-subscriptions-benefits)
- Claimed: about 3 in 4 paused subscribers return, versus 5-15% of cancelled ones. There was also a 337% YoY rise in pause usage, attributed to Recurly "2026 State of Subscriptions". I could not verify either against the primary report. — [Eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/average-ecommerce-subscription-pause-rate-by-vertical-2026) [unverified]
- Lingoda Sprint (Oct 2026 rules): Super Sprint is 30 classes a month for 2 months and refunds 100% of paid fees. Regular Sprint is 15 classes a month and refunds 50%. Every required class must be attended in full. Regular Sprint allows a maximum of 5 classes a week and only one class per day. Private classes don't count. Cancellations must be made 7 days ahead. Refunds go to the original payment method, or as credits. Classes Lingoda itself cancels still count. — [Lingoda Sprint 72a rules](https://www.lingoda.com/downloads/sprint/72/2026-Sprint-72a-Rules-October-Start-EN.pdf)
- Royer, Stehr & Sydnor (AEJ Applied 2015): a 4-week gym incentive alone had small lasting effects. Adding an optional self-funded commitment contract produced behaviour changes still detectable a year or more after the incentive ended. — [NBER w18580](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w18580/w18580.pdf)
- Spika et al. (2023), Stockholm gym chain, 1,629 participants: over 82% accepted a soft or hard commitment contract, and 41% of the hard-contract group chose to add stakes. Outcome results were not retrieved. — [Lund University (LUSEM)](https://www.lusem.lu.se/article/put-bet-it-self-funded-commitment-contracts-get-people-gym)
- Duolingo streaks: the retention team ran 600+ streak-related experiments. Copy changes ("commit to my goal" rather than "continue") reportedly "significantly" increased retention, but no figures were published in the source. — [Lenny's Newsletter](https://lennysnewsletter.com/p/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks)
- Grandfathering: a video-streaming field experiment found that grandfathering raised activity among long-tenured subscribers, while price increases lowered it. The effect was insignificant for new users (conference abstract). — [NUS seminar abstract](https://mysoc.nus.edu.sg/~cmsem/SemPDF/sem_13264.pdf). Netflix (2017) lost long-tenured members when a two-year grandfathering period ended, partly because some read it as a new price rise. — [MacTrast](https://www.mactrast.com/?p=133827) [classic]
- Regulation of cancel flows: the US FTC "click-to-cancel" rule was vacated by the Eighth Circuit on 8 July 2025, before its 14 July effective date. Section 5 enforcement and state laws (e.g. California's B2C click-to-cancel) remain. — [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/07/ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule-vacated-ahead-of-planned-july-14-effective-date); [Womble Bond Dickinson](https://www.womblebonddickinson.com/us/insights/alerts/ftcs-click-cancel-rule-vacated-remember-it-was-not-alone)
- UK DMCC subscription regime (government response 2 Apr 2026) now expected to start spring 2027. It brings a 14-day cooling-off at sign-up plus a second 14-day cooling-off when a free or discounted trial converts to paid, or when a 12-month+ contract renews, with a notice on day one of that period. Proportionate refunds are allowed in renewal cooling-off. — [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/en/insights/2026/04/02/consumer-law-update-subscriptions-regime-delayed-again-to-spring-2027-102mops); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/cancel-culture-uk-subscription-contracts-regime-takes-shape)

### Inferences
- Prepay: offer a quarterly plan (e.g. about 10% off) before an annual one. Learners' goals are often 3-month horizons (exam dates, a move abroad), and the gym evidence suggests long lock-ins don't by themselves improve retention. If an annual plan is offered, "2 months free" is the market-standard frame.
- Pause instead of cancel: allow a 1-2 month pause with the learner's group seat held where possible. This is especially valuable for summer and exam breaks. Expect saves of about 1 in 10 cancellers (vendor figure).
- Downgrade path in the cancel flow: 12 to 8 to 4 classes, and then a "conversation club only" low tier if one is created.
- Save offers must be one honest screen, with cancel reachable in the same number of clicks as sign-up. California, the UK (from 2027) and FTC Section 5 all reward simplicity.
- Commitment-cashback ("Alimun Sprint"): a cohort where attending every class earns back a share of fees as credit, not cash, keeps the cost contained. It is truthful and FOMO-free urgency because the cohort has a fixed start. Requires clear written rules like Lingoda's (attendance in full, cancellation window, what counts). Risk: refund liability and gaming. Paying out as class credits limits cash cost and feeds retention.
- Attendance rewards should be real and modest (e.g. a free conversation club session after a full month attended). Avoid streak mechanics that punish illness. Duolingo publishes no numbers to justify heavy streak pressure for a live-class product.
- Founding-member lock and later price rises: grandfather existing subscribers for as long as they stay. Evidence (NUS, Netflix) shows ending grandfathering is itself a churn event, so either lock "for as long as you stay subscribed" or announce the end date well ahead.

### Gaps
- No primary Recurly or Paddle save-rate benchmark for cancel flows in education was found.
- No published data on Lingoda Sprint completion rates, payout costs, or retention after Sprint.
- Effect sizes for Royer et al. and Spika et al. were not retrieved.

## Acquisition: trials vs freemium vs guarantees, referrals, friend/family/student discounts, employer invoices

### Takeaway
RevenueCat's 2025-26 data show longer trials convert annual plans much better. For monthly plans, conversion peaks at around 5-16 days and later renewal improves with trial length. Alimun's 14-day paid trial sits in the best band for monthly plans. Referred customers are worth at least 16% more and churn about 18% slower (bank study). Money-back guarantees mostly shift choice between sellers rather than expanding total sales.

### Cited Findings
- RevenueCat (17,000+ apps, Aug 2025 to Jul 2026, via SaaStr), monthly plans: trial-to-paid conversion 39.6% (≤4 days), 45.9% (5-9), 46.6% (10-16), 43.7% (17-32). First renewal 54.2%, 62.8%, 72.0% and 77.5% across the same bands. Without a trial, first renewal was 49.5%. — [SaaStr](https://saastr.com/what-17000-subscription-apps-tell-us-about-free-trial-length-annual-plans-convert-86-better-with-30-day-trials-monthly-tops-out-at-two-weeks-and-ai-apps-hit-a-wall-at-16-days)
- Same source, annual plans: conversion 24% (≤4 days), 33% (5-9), 43% (10-16), 44.6% (17-32). First renewal 18.3% up to 47.5%. RevenueCat notes the results are correlational. — [SaaStr](https://saastr.com/what-17000-subscription-apps-tell-us-about-free-trial-length-annual-plans-convert-86-better-with-30-day-trials-monthly-tops-out-at-two-weeks-and-ai-apps-hit-a-wall-at-16-days)
- Regional: on monthly plans, the Middle East & Africa, India & SE Asia and Latin America convert best at 5-9 days. The Middle East & Africa fell from 38.3% (5-9 days) to 27.4% (17-32). — same SaaStr source. Relevant to Alimun's "ma" (Morocco) and "ar" locales and its lower price bands.
- Earlier RevenueCat data: median conversion of 30.2% for trials under 4 days versus 48.8% for 17-32 days. — [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20240313-apps-subscription-stats) [2024]
- RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2025 covers about 75,000 apps and over $10B revenue. Education-category figures were not accessible in the preview. — [RevenueCat](https://revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)
- Adapty 2026 report: trials boost annual LTV in Education, Utilities and Health & Fitness, and Education's 1-year LTV is about $45.10 (competitor dataset, mobile apps). — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions-report/)
- Referral value: Schmitt, Skiera & Van den Bulte (2011, Journal of Marketing), about 10,000 customers of a German bank. Referred customers were at least 16% more valuable, with a higher (eroding) margin and higher (persistent) retention. — [Wharton PDF](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2012/04/Schmitt-Skiera-vandenBulte-2011-Referral-Programs-Customer-Value.pdf) [classic]. Churn speed was about 18% lower. — [GfK MIR 2014](https://reference-global.com/download/article/10.2478/gfkmir-2014-0020.pdf). Van den Bulte et al. (JMR 2018) attribute this to better matching and social enrichment. — [Wharton PDF](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/VdB-et-al.-JMR-2018.pdf)
- Babbel referral terms (2018 page): two-sided reward once the friend buys a 6+ month subscription above a country minimum. In the US, the advocate received a $10 Amazon gift card. — [Babbel refer-a-friend terms](https://about.babbel.com/en/refer-a-friend-us/). Duolingo reportedly gives both sides a week of premium (secondary, undated). — [GrowSurf](https://growsurf.com/examples/language-learning-referral-programs/)
- Talkable (vendor) claims 3x higher LTV for referred e-learning subscribers. No methodology is given. — [Talkable](https://get.talkable.com/industries/e-learning) [vendor, unverified]
- Money-back guarantees: an NBER field experiment on an online labour market (Barach, Golden & Horton) found guarantees strongly steered buyers to guaranteed sellers but did not raise total sales. They worked as a quality signal. — [NBER w25917](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25917/w25917.pdf). An anecdotal counter-case is a 90-day guarantee reportedly reducing KitchenAid sales (podcast, not controlled). — [conversion.com](https://conversion.com/?p=8751)
- Family plans: 23% of Duolingo subscribers, with higher retention and LTV (Q4 2024). — [Motley Fool transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2025/02/27/duolingo-duol-q4-2024-earnings-call-transcript/)
- UK DMCC: a discounted trial (like Alimun's EUR 3 trial) converting to paid will trigger a second 14-day cooling-off and a mandatory notice, expected spring 2027. — [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/en/insights/2026/04/02/consumer-law-update-subscriptions-regime-delayed-again-to-spring-2027-102mops)

### Inferences
- The EUR 3 / 14-day trial sits in the 10-16 day band where monthly conversion peaked (46.6%) and renewal was strong. Keep it. Make sure the trial includes at least 2 live classes so the "aha" moment (speaking in a real group) happens inside 14 days. The small fee filters out no-shows, which matters because each no-show takes a real seat from a group of 6. Send a clear reminder before conversion; this will be legally required in the UK from 2027 and is good practice in the EU.
- For lower price bands (Morocco and the MENA region), RevenueCat suggests shorter trials (5-9 days) may convert better. This could be A/B tested per band.
- Freemium doesn't fit a live-teacher cost model. A free, recurring open conversation club or level test is a cheaper "free tier" that is honest and builds a pipeline.
- Money-back guarantee: evidence says it mostly signals quality rather than expanding demand. A simple "if your first class isn't right, we refund the trial" adds trust at almost no cost (EUR 3). Avoid broad long guarantees on monthly plans.
- Referral: two-sided credit in classes, not cash (e.g. both get a free class once the friend pays a first full month). "Bring a friend into your group" fills seats in existing groups at near-zero marginal cost. Referred learners are likely to stay longer (16-18% bank evidence; not edtech-specific).
- Student discount: verified (e.g. student email or ID), stated plainly. Employer route: provide VAT invoices and a "certificate of attendance / progress letter" for reimbursement. This is a real add-on with value and supports higher-tier purchases. No attach data was found.

### Gaps
- No published referral share-of-acquisition or conversion figures for Babbel, Duolingo, Preply or Lingoda were found.
- No education-category trial data from RevenueCat 2025 were accessible (the full PDF is gated).
- No evidence was found on student-discount or employer-reimbursement uptake in language learning.

## Market-entry pricing: penetration vs skimming, stepped intro prices, price increases for existing subscribers

### Takeaway
I found no rigorous 2022-2026 study comparing penetration and skimming for edtech. Practical evidence centres on price-increase handling: grandfathering protects long-tenured users, ending it triggers churn, and EU rules constrain how "reduced" prices are advertised. For a new entrant with a real capacity cost per seat, a transparent intro price that steps up on a published date, plus a founding-member lock, is the most defensible way to combine market share with honesty.

### Cited Findings
- Grandfathering raised activity among long-tenured subscribers in a video-streaming field experiment, with no significant effect for new users. — [NUS seminar abstract](https://mysoc.nus.edu.sg/~cmsem/SemPDF/sem_13264.pdf)
- Netflix lost long-tenured members when grandfathering ended after two years. Some members read it as a new price increase. — [MacTrast](https://www.mactrast.com/?p=133827) [classic, 2016-17]
- Piano 2026 benchmark (via blog): publishers that pushed increases to over 25% of subscribers saw +7.3% churn growth versus +10.2% for cautious raisers. This is unverified and counter-intuitive. — [subi.co](https://subi.co/post/how-to-raise-subscription-prices) [unverified]
- EU Omnibus: an announced price reduction must reference the lowest price of the prior 30 or more days. A progressive reduction can keep one reference price only within a single continuous campaign. — [Sellforte](https://sellforte.com/blog/omnibus-directive-how-does-it-impact-your-business); [RPC](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/consumer/spring-2022/european-commission-publishes-guidance-on-price-promotions-under-the-omnibus-directive/)
- Recurly: lower-priced plans give larger annual-prepay discounts. — [Recurly](https://recurly.com/research/saas-benchmarks-for-subscription-plans)

### Inferences
- Penetration with a time limit: launch at the intended long-run price, then offer a clearly stated "founding member" price for the first N members or until a date. Lock it for as long as they stay subscribed. This builds share without later "fake discount" exposure under Omnibus, because the headline price never changes and the founding rate is an offer, not a strike-through.
- Avoid "intro price that steps up after 3 months" for monthly learners unless it's shown up front with the date and amount. It can feel like bait, and under the UK DMCC regime a discounted period converting to full price may trigger the renewal cooling-off.
- Country price bands are a legitimate way to win share in lower-income markets (Morocco, LatAm, Asia) without discounting core markets. Keep bands tied to billing country to limit arbitrage.
- When prices rise, notify existing subscribers at least 30 days ahead, grandfather founding members, and give everyone else a real choice: stay, move to a cheaper tier, or pause.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or company-published 2022-2026 penetration vs skimming results for edtech or live classes were found.
- ProfitWell/Paddle price-increase churn studies were cited only second-hand (e.g. "30% lower churn from segmented increases") and could not be verified.
- Simon-Kucher and OpenView primary benchmarks were not accessible in this session. The OpenView annual-discount and CAC-payback link exists only via secondary blogs. — [Monetizely](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/why-annual-billing-discounts-work-better-than-you-think-a-revenue-game-changer-for-saas)
